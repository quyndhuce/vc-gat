In the heart of Hanoi's Old Quarter, nestled along the picturesque Đinh Tiên Hoàng Street, lies the renowned Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre, a cultural gem that has been captivating audiences since its inception in 1969. This iconic institution, named after the majestic Thăng Long era, when King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital and beheld the rising dragon, has become an integral part of the city's heritage. The theatre's rustic charm is reflected in its wooden architecture, adorned with intricate lacquerwork, which seems to come alive as the rhythmic melodies of the đàn nhị blend with the bustling beats.

As visitors stroll along the tree-lined street, they are drawn to the captivating sight of a human and a frog engaged in a playful chase on the rippling surface of Hoàn Kiếm Lake. The vibrant green of the frog's costume contrasts beautifully with the rustic brown of the farmer's shirt, showcasing the glossiness of the lacquer on the wood core. The signature feature of this performance is the leap, as the clever frog evades the nơm, leaving a trail of white foam in its wake. This bustling scene stands out against the mysterious, drooping bamboo screen, transporting viewers to a rural village festival.

As the performance unfolds, the entire space seems to come alive, carrying the pride of the Thăng Long era. The laughter and applause of the audience, comprising both locals and tourists, are a testament to the enduring appeal of this cultural treasure. This excerpt not only showcases masterly puppetry techniques but also serves as a lens reflecting childhood, offering a unique cultural experience that helps us temporarily escape the hustle and bustle of urban life to return to our rustic roots. As the performance concludes, what remains in everyone's heart is a sense of refreshment and immense affection, a reminder of the timeless appeal of this beloved cultural institution.

Nestled along the picturesque Đinh Tiên Hoàng Street, adjacent to the bustling Hoàn Kiếm Lake in Hanoi, the Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre stands as a testament to Vietnam’s rich cultural heritage. Since its inaugural performance in 1969, the theatre has been a beacon of traditional arts, embodying the legacy of the ancient capital Thăng Long, which was established by King Lý Thái Tổ in the 11th century. The theatre’s performances, particularly the whimsical act known as “Câu ếch” or Frog Fishing, have captivated audiences with their masterful puppetry and charming storytelling.

The act of Frog Fishing is a delightful spectacle that transports viewers back to the rustic charm of Vietnamese villages. Set against the serene backdrop of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, the performance features a dynamic chase between a farmer and a mischievous frog. The puppets, crafted from intricately lacquered wood, are brought to life through the skilled manipulation of puppeteers hidden behind a bamboo screen. The farmer, dressed in a rustic brown shirt, engages in a spirited pursuit of the vibrant green frog, whose agile movements are highlighted by the splash of white foam as it leaps away from the farmer’s net, or nơm.

This performance is not merely an entertaining display; it is a reflection of the enduring cultural traditions of Vietnam. The rhythmic music of the đàn nhị, a traditional Vietnamese two-stringed fiddle, harmonizes with the lively beats of percussion instruments, creating an atmosphere reminiscent of a village festival. The combination of these elements—music, puppetry, and storytelling—immerses visitors in a nostalgic journey that celebrates the resilience and vitality of Vietnamese culture.

The Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre, founded in 1969, has become an integral part of Hanoi’s cultural landscape, attracting both domestic and international tourists. Its performances serve as a window into the country’s past, offering a glimpse into the rural life and folklore that have shaped Vietnam’s identity. The act of Frog Fishing, in particular, captures the essence of this cultural heritage, blending humor, skill, and tradition in a way that resonates deeply with audiences. It is more than just entertainment; it is a cherished cultural experience that connects people to their roots and provides a momentary respite from the fast-paced urban environment.

As visitors watch the playful antics of the farmer and the frog, they are reminded of the simple joys and the enduring spirit of the Vietnamese people. The theatre’s location near Hoàn Kiếm Lake adds to its allure, providing a serene setting that complements the enchanting performances. The Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre continues to thrive, preserving and celebrating the rich tapestry of Vietnamese culture for generations to come.

Hang Duong Street, also known as Pho Hang Duong, is a captivating thoroughfare that stretches from the bustling Dong Xuan Market to the picturesque Hoan Kiem Lake, forming a sweet and nostalgic realm within the thirty-six streets of Hanoi's Old Quarter. This historic street has been a hub of commercial activity for centuries, dating back to the time when merchants would gather to trade honey, rock sugar, and hoa mai sugar from the Red River Delta to the imperial capital. The name Hang Duong, which translates to "sugar street," was born from the sweet aroma that wafted through the yin-yang tile roofs of the era, creating a poetic and enduring legacy.

As one walks along this charming street, the eye is drawn to the rows of tube houses bearing strong imprints of ancient architecture, their glossy brown color blending seamlessly with the transparent glass of the giant glass jars filled with glittering five-colored jam and ô mai. The unique visual feature of the street is the hundreds of these jars, arranged closely together along the facades, creating a vibrant and captivating scene nestled beneath moss-stained balconies.

The street's rich history is palpable, with the sounds of vendors calling out their wares and the aroma of traditional ô mai and candied fruits wafting through the air. As Tết approaches, the street transforms into a festive and bustling hub, teeming with buyers and sellers carrying the warm breath of reunion. The experience of savoring the sour and crispy ô mai or choosing elegant and pure mứt sen trần is like fully tasting the ups and downs and the eternal elegance of this land of a thousand years of civilization.

Hang Duong Street is a testament to the city's cultural heritage, with its unique blend of history, architecture, and tradition. As a popular tourist destination, the street attracts visitors from all over, who come to experience the sights, sounds, and flavors of this enchanting place. Whether one is a local resident or a traveler, Hang Duong Street is a must-visit destination, offering a glimpse into the city's rich past and a taste of its vibrant present.

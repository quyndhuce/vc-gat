Hang Duong Street, also known as Phố Hàng Đường, is a historic street located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi, running from north to south. It connects at its southern end to Hang Ngang Street and at its northern end to Dong Xuan Street. The street intersects with Hang Ca Street and Ngoc Gach Alley. The name "Hang Duong" originates from the traditional goods sold there, which include sweets, candies, and products made from sugar and honey.

The history of Hang Duong Street dates back to before the 15th century when it was part of the dike system in the villages of Dong Hoa Noi, Hau Dong Hoa Mon (later renamed Duc Mon), and Vinh Thai (later changed to Vinh Hanh), all within the Hau Tuc district of Tho Xuan city, then known as Thang Long. Today, the street falls under the jurisdiction of Hang Dao ward, Hoan Kiem district. In the past, the To Lich River flowed through this area, featuring a stone bridge called Dong Bridge. Although the bridge no longer exists, its name lives on in the nearby Chua Cau Dong temple. There was once a market near the bridge called Chua Dong Market, which later moved to Hang Duong Street and became known as Dong Xuan Market.

During the French colonial period, the street was named Rue du Sucre, and a tram line ran along the route between Ho Tay and Dong Xuan streets. Currently, Hang Duong Street operates as a one-way street from Hang Ngang to Dong Xuan. It is part of the pedestrian zone that opens during weekend evenings.

Historically, the main products sold on Hang Duong Street included various types of sweets, candies, and goods made from honey and sugarcane. Sugarcane syrup from Quang Ngai and honey from different regions were brought by traders to sell in small quantities or to produce sweets and candies. The busiest months were those leading up to Tet and the Mid-Autumn Festival.

Today, many shops still sell candied fruits and sweets on Hang Duong Street, particularly famous for its delicious pickled fruits.

Among the notable landmarks on Hang Duong Street are Chua Cau Dong, formerly known as Chua Duc Mon, located at number 38. Inside the temple, besides Buddha statues, there are also statues of Tran Thu Do and Tran Thi Dung. Adjacent to the temple is the Duc Mon communal house, dedicated to Ngo Van Long, a general from the Hung Vuong era. Another significant site is the Vinh Hanh communal house at number 19B, where the ground floor used to serve as a marketplace while the upper level was reserved for worship. However, this communal house no longer functions in its original capacity.

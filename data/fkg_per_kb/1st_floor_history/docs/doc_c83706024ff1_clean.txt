As you step into the first floor of the National Museum of Vietnamese History, you are transported to the dawn of Vietnamese history, where every story begins from prehistoric footprints imprinted on each artifact. Located in the heart of bustling Tràng Tiền Street, this structure, bearing the hallmarks of Indochinese architecture, was built during the French colonial period. The building's wooden brown and antique bronze hues create a warm and profound feeling, as if the very walls are imbued with the whispers of the past.

The spacious area is bathed in a soft yellow light, which illuminates the glass display cases and ancient artifacts, revealing the intricate patterns and designs that adorn the bronze drums. The drums, placed in the central position, are the most prominent feature of the room, their swirling patterns a testament to the Đông Sơn culture's deep connection to the universe and the rhythm of early communal life. It is believed that these patterns were not merely decorative, but also symbols of the cosmos and the harmony of the community.

As you wander through the galleries, you are surrounded by the quiet, solemn atmosphere, which seems to slow your pace, allowing you to absorb the stories and legends that surround each artifact. The bas-reliefs and display cases are filled with relics from the Đông Sơn period, including bronze drums, pottery, and other artifacts that whisper tales of ancient festivals and rituals. The air is thick with the scent of aged wood and the whispers of the past, as if the very walls are sharing secrets with you.

The National Museum of Vietnamese History is a testament to the country's rich cultural heritage, a journey through time that begins with the earliest human settlements and continues through the French colonial era. As you explore the first floor, you are not just "seeing" history, but also "feeling" it, as if the stories and legends are alive and pulsing through the very fabric of the museum. And as you leave, a sense of pride and connection to your heritage lingers, as if you have just touched your own roots.

The first floor of the National Museum of Vietnamese History, known as "Tầng 1: Lịch sử" in Vietnamese, stands as a gateway to the rich tapestry of Vietnam's ancient past, nestled in the heart of bustling Tràng Tiền Street in Hanoi. This architectural marvel, constructed during the French colonial era, exemplifies the distinctive Indochinese style, characterized by its grandiose yet harmonious design. The museum's imposing structure, with its high ceilings and interconnected galleries, invites visitors into a realm where time seems to stand still, offering a serene contrast to the vibrant cityscape outside.

Upon entering, one is immediately enveloped by a warm, inviting atmosphere, thanks to the soft yellow lighting that gently illuminates the glass display cases housing ancient artifacts. The interior decor, featuring a palette of warm wooden browns and antique bronze hues, creates a profound and contemplative ambiance. Central to this space are the large bronze drums, which serve as the focal point of the exhibit. These drums, remnants of the Đông Sơn culture, date back to the Iron Age and are adorned with intricate swirling patterns believed to symbolize the cosmos and the rhythms of communal life. Each drum, a testament to the ingenuity and artistic prowess of ancient Vietnamese civilization, echoes the sounds of long-forgotten festivals and rituals.

Surrounding these monumental relics are numerous display cases and bas-reliefs, each telling a unique story of Vietnam’s storied past. From the earliest prehistoric footprints to the sophisticated craftsmanship of the Đông Sơn culture, the exhibits provide a comprehensive overview of Vietnam’s historical evolution. Visitors often find themselves slowing down, their eyes drawn to the meticulous detail and craftsmanship evident in each artifact. The quiet solemnity of the space allows for a deep connection with the past, enabling visitors to not only see history but also feel its weight and significance.

This floor serves as a cultural cornerstone, preserving and celebrating the heritage of the Vietnamese people. It is here that one can trace the lineage of Vietnamese civilization, from its humble beginnings to the complex societal structures of later periods. The museum’s strategic location in the heart of Hanoi, near significant landmarks such as Ba Dinh Square and the Hoàn Kiếm Lake, enhances its importance as a cultural hub. As visitors leave the museum, they carry with them a profound sense of pride and connection to their ancestral roots, a feeling that lingers long after the physical journey through the exhibits has ended.

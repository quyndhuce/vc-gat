Ngu Xa Island, or Đảo ngọc Ngũ Xã in Vietnamese, is a small, crescent-shaped strip of land nestled peacefully beside Trúc Bạch Lake in Ba Dinh District, Hanoi. This unique island, resembling a floating lotus flower, has been a hub of cultural and culinary significance since the 17th century, when artisans from five bronze casting villages gathered here to establish their livelihoods, giving birth to the name Ngũ Xã, honoring the essence of the ancient Thăng Long bronze casting craft.

As the sun sets, the island transforms into a bustling culinary paradise, with closely packed eateries along the walkway displaying inviting plates of phở cuốn, a specialty of the island. The brilliant yellow lights that illuminate the gray neighborhood tones at night create a warm and inviting atmosphere, drawing visitors from all directions to stroll and savor the flavors of this culinary haven.

The island's architectural style is a blend of traditional and modern elements, with a mix of old and new buildings, including ancient temples and modern eateries. The surrounding waters of Trúc Bạch Lake add to the island's serenity, creating a peaceful and tranquil atmosphere that is perfect for relaxation and contemplation.

As a cultural and historical landmark, Ngu Xa Island is a significant part of Hanoi's urban landscape, offering a unique blend of heritage and modern vibrancy. Visitors can explore the island's rich history and cultural significance, while also enjoying the delicious local cuisine and lively atmosphere. Whether you're a foodie, a history buff, or simply looking for a unique experience, Ngu Xa Island is a must-visit destination in Hanoi.

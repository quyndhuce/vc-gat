The Calligraphy at the Bai Minh Stele before the Temple of Literature in Hanoi is a remarkable piece of art that has captivated visitors for centuries. This stele, located on the Dai Nghien (Inkstone) Pavilion in front of the Temple of Literature, was inscribed during the spring of 2012, marking a significant moment in the history of this revered site. The Dai Nghien itself is a unique structure, designed to resemble an inkstone used by scholars, symbolizing the importance of learning and calligraphy in Vietnamese culture. The stele stands proudly facing the Dai Nghien, its presence a testament to the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam.

The Bai Minh Stele, also known as the "Clear Mind Stele," is a prime example of traditional Vietnamese calligraphy and literature. It is adorned with Han Chinese characters, reflecting the deep-rooted influence of Chinese culture on Vietnamese civilization. The stele's inscription, written by Lam Giang, provides valuable insights into the historical significance of the Dai Nghien pavilion. Visitors can appreciate the intricate details of the stele, which not only showcases the beauty of calligraphy but also offers a glimpse into the cultural and historical context of the Temple of Literature.

The Bai Minh Stele is one of many such steles found throughout Vietnam, each bearing important inscriptions that reflect the values and traditions of the time. Typically, these steles feature inscriptions written in classical Chinese, often in a vertical format, with varying lengths depending on the content. Some steles contain as few as four characters, while others extend to over a hundred characters, like the Thanh Tong Chiểu Long Bình Định stele, penned by Than Nhan Trung for Chiểu Long when Emperor Le Thanh Tong visited the temple.

The Bai Minh Stele at the Dai Nghien pavilion is particularly noteworthy due to its historical significance and the frequent visits it receives from both domestic and international tourists. Over the years, numerous translations and interpretations of the stele's inscription have been produced, each offering a different perspective on its meaning and historical context. One of the earliest translations was done by French scholar G. Doumitier in 1887, thirteen years after the stele was erected. Since then, various Vietnamese scholars have continued to translate and interpret the stele's contents, contributing to a deeper understanding of its cultural and historical value.

The translation process involves careful analysis of the original text, ensuring accuracy in conveying the intended message. Translators must navigate the complexities of classical Chinese, a language rich in nuance and historical context. For instance, the stele's inscription, translated into Vietnamese, reads: "This place is a symbol of harmony and tranquility, where scholars gather to study and reflect. The pavilion is round and symmetrical, embodying balance and unity. It stands tall amidst the serene waters of the Hoan Kiem Lake, surrounded by lush greenery and the gentle breeze of the Thai Nguyen wind. The stele itself is a testament to the dedication and skill of the artisans who crafted it."

The stele's inscription, attributed to Nguyen Van Sieu and Vu Ta Tri, was originally composed in the year Giap Ty (1864). It describes the Dai Nghien pavilion in detail, highlighting its architectural beauty and symbolic significance. The stele was later included in the Phuong Dich Van Loi volume published in the 35th year of the Tong Phuc reign (1882), under the supervision of Quang Luc Khanh Quyen Sung Ha Noi Tuan Phu Vu Nhi Kiem Duyet. In 1937, during the restoration of the temple, the stele was re-engraved onto stone and placed on the Dai Nghien pavilion, where it remains today, a constant reminder of the enduring legacy of Vietnamese calligraphy and scholarship.

Visitors to the Temple of Literature can explore the Dai Nghien pavilion and the Bai Minh Stele, immersing themselves in the rich cultural tapestry of Vietnam. The atmosphere around the stele is serene and contemplative, inviting visitors to reflect on the historical and cultural significance of the site. The surrounding environment, with its tranquil lakes and lush gardens, complements the architectural beauty of the Dai Nghien pavilion, creating a harmonious setting that enhances the visitor experience. The Bai Minh Stele stands as a testament to the enduring importance of calligraphy and literature in Vietnamese culture, a tradition that continues to inspire and educate generations of scholars and visitors alike.

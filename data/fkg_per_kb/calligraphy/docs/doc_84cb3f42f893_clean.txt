Nestled within the ancient precincts of Ngoc Son Temple, a revered site in the heart of Hoan Kiem District, the practice of calligraphy has long been a cherished cultural tradition that captivates visitors and locals alike. This serene activity unfolds amidst the tranquil ambiance of Ho Guom Lake, where the gentle ringing of temple bells harmonizes with the placid waters, creating an atmosphere of profound serenity. During the early spring and Tet holidays, the temple grounds are adorned with the presence of Ông đồ, elderly scribes dressed in traditional ao dai, meticulously crafting calligraphy on red or yellow paper. These artisans, embodying the spirit of studiousness and reverence, bend over their inkstones, each soft brushstroke imbued with the essence of old Hanoi. The custom of xin chu, or asking for calligraphy, stems from the belief that characters like "Phuc" (blessings), "Loc" (prosperity), "Tam" (heart), and "An" (peace) will bestow good fortune and tranquility upon the new year. As a result, this tradition has seamlessly integrated into the cultural fabric of the area around Hoan Kiem Lake, becoming a beloved part of the local heritage.

Visitors to Ngoc Son Temple are drawn not only by its architectural splendor and historical significance but also by the opportunity to witness the artistry of calligraphy. Tourists and residents alike pause to watch the Ông đồ at work, their movements slow and deliberate, each stroke of the brush a testament to patience and reverence. Conversations with these scribes often ensue, allowing for a deeper appreciation of the cultural and spiritual dimensions of the practice. Many leave the temple with a piece of calligraphy as a cherished memento, a tangible symbol of the tranquility and beauty they have experienced. The juxtaposition of the bustling city center with the quiet, contemplative space of the calligraphy session creates a unique contrast that resonates deeply with visitors. Surrounded by the shade of ancient trees, the reflective waters of Hoan Kiem Lake, and the iconic vermilion hue of The Huc Bridge, the calligraphy activity at Ngoc Son Temple offers a glimpse into the spiritual and artistic soul of Hanoi. This tradition, particularly enchanting in the early mornings, on weekends, and during autumn, continues to attract a diverse array of people seeking blessings and a connection to the rich cultural heritage of the city.

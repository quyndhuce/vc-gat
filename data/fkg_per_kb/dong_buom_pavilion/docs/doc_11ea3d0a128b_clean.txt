Nestled within the bustling urban landscape of Hanoi, specifically in the front courtyard of the Hanoi Museum on Pham Hung Street, the Dong Buom Pavilion stands as a unique architectural marvel. Introduced in 2025 as part of the Top 10 Awards, this innovative exhibition space seamlessly blends traditional Vietnamese elements with contemporary design, serving as a testament to the city’s rich cultural heritage and its commitment to preserving indigenous memories.

The pavilion's name, "Dong Buom," translates to "Setting Sail," a metaphorical reference to the ancient maritime journeys undertaken by Vietnamese seafarers. This symbolic title encapsulates themes of movement, exploration, and faith in the future, while the choice of bamboo as the primary construction material underscores a deep connection to the earth and Vietnamese traditions. The golden-brown hue of the bamboo, illuminated by the sun, creates a striking contrast against the greenery of the surrounding trees and the inverted pyramid architecture of the Hanoi Museum itself.

Visually, the Dong Buom Pavilion is dominated by three towering bamboo structures that resemble giant sails, unfurled as if catching the wind. These sails, crafted from woven bamboo slats, not only provide a sense of stability and strength but also evoke a delicate, almost ethereal quality. The inverted pyramid shape of the Hanoi Museum, visible through the gaps between the sails, adds a layer of architectural intrigue, blending seamlessly with the natural environment of the museum’s courtyard.

The pavilion serves as a vibrant cultural hub, attracting both residents and tourists who are drawn to its unique blend of art and public space. Visitors often meander through the courtyard, taking photographs of the towering bamboo structures, observing the architectural projects on display, or simply enjoying the shade beneath the sails. The space encourages contemplation and reflection, offering a serene respite from the hustle and bustle of the city.

Culturally, the Dong Buom Pavilion holds significant importance as it bridges the gap between past and present, reminding visitors of the enduring spirit of Vietnamese voyagers who ventured into the unknown while maintaining a strong connection to their roots. This symbolic representation of movement and exploration resonates with the broader narrative of Hanoi, a city that continues to evolve while honoring its historical legacy. As such, the Dong Buom Pavilion stands not only as an architectural wonder but also as a poignant reminder of the interplay between tradition and modernity in Vietnam’s cultural landscape.

The inauguration of the "Dong buồm" Pavilion at the Hanoi Museum on May 22, 2026 marked a significant milestone in contemporary Vietnamese architecture. This event not only celebrated the opening of the pavilion but also witnessed the awarding of the Top 10 Awards 2025, which has been organized by KIENVIET MEDIA since its inception in 2018. The ceremony was attended by a multitude of architects, designers, and industry experts, reflecting the growing importance of this annual event in the field of architecture and interior design in Vietnam.

The Top 10 Awards, now in its eighth year, has established itself as a reputable platform for recognizing excellence in architectural and interior design projects across the country. This year's awards continued to focus on three main categories: Top 10 Houses, Top 10 Interior Designs, and Top 10 Green Projects, highlighting the prominent trends in contemporary architecture that emphasize creativity, sustainability, and cultural identity. The organizers announced plans to expand the awards from 2026, introducing two new categories: Top 10 Social Housing and Top 10 C-Solutions, aimed at addressing current issues such as community housing development and sustainable material solutions.

Designed by Dplus Vietnam, the "Dong buồm" Pavilion stands out as a remarkable structure that embodies the spirit of Vietnamese architecture. Inspired by the image of a full sail, the pavilion symbolizes the ambition of Vietnamese architecture to venture into international waters while preserving local cultural values. The design is centered around a core concept known as the "Heritage Core," from which radiates the three fundamental values of the Top 10 Awards: locality, innovation, and humanity. The pavilion's construction features a lattice of bamboo, creating a dynamic visual effect that represents the evolving nature of contemporary Vietnamese architecture.

According to architect Vu Dao Hoang, the head of the Top 10 Awards organizing committee, the awards serve as a high-quality filter within the industry, acknowledging exemplary works in residential, interior, and green architecture. This recognition aims to shape a sustainable trajectory for Vietnamese architecture. The pavilion itself is more than just an exhibition space; it is envisioned as a visual symbol of the journey of contemporary Vietnamese architecture. Every element, from the bamboo lattice to the open spatial experience, is designed to bring architecture closer to the public through natural and intimate interactions.

Before the awards ceremony, a panel of judges comprising leading experts conducted a rigorous evaluation process over two rounds, scoring each project on a scale of 100 points. The data was visually represented using radar charts to enhance transparency and help the public better understand the strengths of each project. In the category of Top 10 Houses 2025, several notable projects were recognized, including MÔ He’ritage Villa, Ramp Around House, and Casa Blanca Garden. These projects exemplify the integration of functionality, context, and emotional connection to human life, according to architect Hoang Thuc Hao, the head judge for this category.

In the realm of interior design, the Top 10 Interior Designs 2025 showcased a diverse range of styles and types of design, such as the Co Audio – Gallery, Galaxy CineX Hanoi Centre Cinema, and The 75 Percent Coffee. These designs reflect a clear advancement in the creative capabilities of the Vietnamese interior design industry, as noted by Associate Professor Nguyen Thu Phong. Meanwhile, the Top 10 Green Projects 2025 highlighted numerous constructions that prioritize sustainability and biodiversity, like the Namia River Retreat, The Jungle, and SUN Village in Phu Yen. Architect Douglas Lee Snyder observed that Vietnamese architects are increasingly focusing on the use of native plants, recycled materials, and environmentally friendly design solutions.

Beyond being an annual award, the Top 10 Awards 2025 serves as a professional networking platform where pioneering ideas and local values are disseminated widely to the community. Through the "Dong buoom" Pavilion and its open exhibition space, the program aims to bring architecture closer to daily life and inspire the next generation of Vietnamese architects to integrate and establish their mark on the global creative map.

The "Dong buoom" Pavilion, a striking installation at the Hanoi Museum, has captivated visitors since its unveiling on May 22, 2025, during the opening ceremony of the Top 10 Awards exhibition. This unique structure, comprising three towering sails made of bamboo, stands as a testament to contemporary Vietnamese architecture and design. The pavilion, which is part of a larger exhibition celebrating the finest architectural designs from across the country, was designed to reflect the essence of Vietnamese culture while embracing modern innovation.

The three bamboo sails, each soaring over six meters high, are arranged in a radial formation around a central core, symbolizing the convergence of traditional and modern elements. The choice of bamboo as the primary material underscores the deep connection between the work and Vietnam's natural heritage. Bamboo, a ubiquitous and sustainable resource in Vietnam, is not only a nod to local craftsmanship but also a representation of the country’s commitment to environmental sustainability.

The pavilion's design draws inspiration from the image of full sails, a powerful metaphor for progress and aspiration. Each sail is crafted to point upwards, embodying the spirit of reaching towards new heights while maintaining a strong foundation in cultural roots. Architect Phuong Doan, who led the design team, explains that the "Dong buoom" Pavilion aims to convey a sense of integration and creativity, while simultaneously preserving the intrinsic values of Vietnamese architecture.

The sails themselves are more than just structural elements; they serve as canvases for showcasing winning designs from various categories of the Top 10 Awards. These designs, displayed along the bamboo structures, highlight the innovative solutions and sustainable practices that are increasingly becoming integral to modern architectural projects in Vietnam. The inclusion of these designs transforms the pavilion into a dynamic space where visitors can engage directly with cutting-edge architectural concepts.

Visitors to the Hanoi Museum have expressed great interest in the "Dong buoom" Pavilion, often spending considerable time admiring the intricate construction and symbolic significance of the bamboo sails. The pavilion is situated outdoors, allowing visitors to experience it within the context of the museum's surroundings, creating a harmonious blend of indoor and outdoor spaces. The open-air setting enhances the pavilion's appeal, offering a seamless transition between the built environment and the natural landscape.

The architects and designers behind the project envision the pavilion as a temporary yet impactful installation, intended to remain on display until the end of September. After this period, there are plans to cover the bamboo structures with climbing vines, further integrating the pavilion into its natural setting and extending its life cycle. This approach not only aligns with the principles of sustainable design but also invites visitors to witness the transformation of the pavilion over time, reflecting the dynamic nature of both the built environment and the natural world.

The "Dong buoom" Pavilion serves as a focal point for the Top 10 Awards exhibition, drawing attention to the importance of quality and innovation in architectural design. It represents a significant milestone in the selection process of the awards, which began with over 363 submissions and narrowed down to the top 30 designs. The pavilion's presence at the Hanoi Museum underscores the role of architecture in enhancing urban spaces and promoting sustainable development, while also fostering a deeper appreciation for the cultural and historical context of Vietnam's built environment.

As visitors explore the "Dong buoom" Pavilion, they are invited to reflect on the interplay between tradition and modernity, sustainability and innovation. The pavilion stands as a beacon of hope and progress, embodying the aspirations of Vietnamese architects and designers to create spaces that are both functional and culturally resonant. Through its design and placement, the pavilion offers a unique opportunity for visitors to engage with the latest trends in architectural thinking, while also connecting them to the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam.

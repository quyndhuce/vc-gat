The Water Pavilion at the Vietnam Museum of Ethnic Cultures was officially inaugurated on November 20th, marking a significant milestone in the preservation and promotion of traditional Vietnamese puppetry. The construction of this unique structure began in June 2015 and was completed by skilled artisans from the villages of Chàng Sơn and Sài Sơn in Hanoi. This Water Pavilion is not just a building but a living testament to the intricate art of water puppetry, which has been a cherished part of Vietnamese culture for centuries.

The Water Pavilion, also known as the "buồng trò" in local parlance, is one of the four essential elements that define the art of water puppetry. These elements include the puppets themselves, the puppeteers who control them, the musical ensemble, and the Water Pavilion itself. The pavilion serves as the stage where these elements come together to create a mesmerizing performance. It is designed to reflect the traditional architecture found in the northern plains of Vietnam, drawing inspiration from existing structures and incorporating feedback from various folk puppet troupes.

The design of the Water Pavilion is a meticulous blend of historical and contemporary elements. It is modeled after the ancient Thầy Pagoda’s Water Pavilion, which dates back to the late Lê Dynasty period (1533-1788). This architectural style is considered the oldest surviving example of its kind in Vietnam. The pavilion features two additional side houses called "nanh," where performers can showcase their skills in string puppetry, and a dedicated music room for the orchestra. The entire structure is oriented towards the west, aligning with traditional Feng Shui principles, and its roof is designed with eight curved eaves, symbolizing prosperity and longevity.

Constructed using traditional materials and techniques, the Water Pavilion is built primarily with lim wood, a durable and resilient material that has been used in Vietnamese architecture for centuries. The foundation is made of reinforced concrete, ensuring stability and longevity. The roof is covered with curved ceramic tiles, giving it a graceful appearance reminiscent of flower petals. The decorative elements, such as the dragon heads and finials, are crafted with great care, featuring intricate carvings and vibrant colors that reflect the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam.

The pavilion's layout is carefully planned to accommodate both the performers and the audience. It consists of three main sections: the central hall for the water stage, the side rooms for the puppeteers, and the music room for the orchestra. The central hall measures 20 square meters, providing ample space for the water stage and the puppeteers to maneuver their puppets. The side rooms and music room are smaller, each measuring 9 square meters, allowing for intimate performances and close interaction between the performers and the audience.

The inauguration of the Water Pavilion marks a new chapter in the museum's efforts to preserve and promote traditional arts. Following the completion of the pavilion, the museum plans to revive the performances of 16 folk puppetry troupes, showcasing their talents every Saturday and Sunday. These performances will offer visitors a unique opportunity to witness the art of water puppetry, a tradition that has been passed down through generations. The Water Pavilion not only serves as a venue for performances but also as an educational tool, providing insights into the history, techniques, and cultural significance of water puppetry.

Visitors to the Water Pavilion can expect an immersive experience that combines visual beauty with cultural richness. The pavilion's location within the Vietnam Museum of Ethnic Cultures adds to its appeal, offering a serene setting amidst the natural beauty of the surrounding area. The performances are not just entertainment but also a window into the cultural heritage of Vietnam, showcasing the skill, creativity, and dedication of the puppeteers and musicians. The Water Pavilion stands as a testament to the enduring legacy of Vietnamese traditional arts, inviting visitors to explore and appreciate the beauty and complexity of this unique cultural expression.

One of the most notable creations from this village is the colossal statue of Buddha Amitabha located at Than Quang Pagoda itself, a testament to the artisans' unparalleled expertise.

Than Quang Pagoda, also known as Chùa Ngũ Xá or Phúc Long tự, is a significant religious site located in Hà Nội, renowned for its grandiose statue of Buddha and its rich history deeply intertwined with the local culture and traditions. The pagoda was originally established during the Hậu Lê dynasty in the 18th century, specifically in the middle of the 18th century, and it stands as a testament to the enduring legacy of the influential monk Nguyễn Minh Không, who founded several other temples including the famous Chùa Cổ Lễ in Nam Định and Chùa Keo in Thái Bình. This temple belongs to the Bắc tông sect, reflecting its adherence to Northern Buddhism.

Unfortunately, the original structure suffered from a devastating fire in 1949, necessitating a complete reconstruction under the guidance of the Venerable Thích Mật Đắc. The rebuilding process commenced in 1949 and was completed by 1951, resulting in a modern architectural marvel that seamlessly integrates traditional Buddhist elements with contemporary design. The new temple was designed to withstand heavy loads and accommodate large crowds, thanks to its innovative construction using reinforced concrete.

The centerpiece of Than Quang Pagoda is undoubtedly the colossal statue of Amitabha Buddha, which stands at an impressive height of nearly 4 meters and weighs approximately 12.3 tons. This magnificent statue sits gracefully on a lotus pedestal adorned with 96 petals, symbolizing purity and enlightenment. The statue's serene expression exudes a sense of transcendence, embodying the wisdom and compassion of a fully enlightened being. Interestingly, the materials used to create this awe-inspiring statue were sourced from various bronze statues that once honored French colonial figures, such as the statue of Paul Bert, the former Governor-General of Indochina, and the Statue of Liberty replica, both of which were toppled by the Vietnamese people in 1945. These historical artifacts were later repurposed into the current statue, thus imbuing it with a unique historical significance that reflects the complex interplay between colonialism and indigenous culture.

The architecture of Than Quang Pagoda is a harmonious blend of traditional and modern elements. The main entrance, or tam quan, faces south towards the street, featuring a two-tiered roof with eight ridges, each adorned with intricate carvings and sculptures. The ornate gates are flanked by stone lions and small nine-story pagodas, adding to the temple’s majestic appearance. Beyond the entrance lies a small rock garden that serves as a natural screen for the steps leading up to the main hall, where visitors can admire the exquisite craftsmanship and detailed ornamentation.

Inside, the temple comprises several halls and chambers, each dedicated to different deities and saints. The central hall houses the main altar with the colossal statue of Amitabha Buddha, while side halls contain shrines for other revered figures such as the Holy Monks and the Ancestral Deity. The temple’s layout is designed to facilitate movement and reverence, with wide corridors and spacious rooms that allow for smooth flow during ceremonies and pilgrimages. The upper level of the temple is particularly noteworthy, featuring high ceilings and wooden floors that enhance the sense of grandeur and solemnity.

In addition to the main statue, Than Quang Pagoda houses numerous other valuable relics, including a set of bronze incense burners weighing 300 kilograms each, and pairs of bronze lamps standing tall at 1.2 meters. These artifacts, along with the sixteen stone steles dating back to the period between 1919 and 1947, provide a tangible link to the past and highlight the temple’s historical importance. The preservation of these items underscores the dedication of the Buddhist community and the skillful craftsmanship of the artisans from the Ngũ Xá village, known for their expertise in bronze casting.

Visitors to Than Quang Pagoda are not only drawn by its architectural splendor but also by the profound spiritual atmosphere that permeates every corner of the temple. The serene ambiance, combined with the meticulous attention to detail in every aspect of the temple’s construction and decoration, creates a space conducive to meditation and reflection. The temple’s location within the bustling city of Hà Nội adds another layer of contrast, offering a peaceful retreat amidst the urban landscape.

Overall, Than Quang Pagoda stands as a remarkable example of how traditional Buddhist practices and modern architectural innovation can coexist harmoniously, creating a space that is both historically significant and spiritually uplifting. Its grand statue of Amitabha Buddha, the intricate details of its architecture, and the rich cultural heritage it embodies make it a must-visit destination for anyone interested in the intersection of religion, art, and history in Vietnam.

Thong Nhat park, often referred to as the "green lung" of Hanoi, is a sprawling oasis of tranquility nestled in the heart of the bustling city. Spanning an impressive 50 hectares, this verdant haven is a sanctuary for both locals and tourists seeking respite from the urban hustle and bustle. The park's lush greenery, which covers nearly every inch of its expansive grounds, provides a serene atmosphere that is perfect for leisurely strolls, morning exercises, and family outings. Situated at 354A Le Duan Street, the park is conveniently located between four major thoroughfares—Le Duan, Tran Nhan Tong, Dai Co Viet, and Nguyen Dinh Chieu—making it easily accessible from various parts of the city.

The history of Thong Nhat park dates back to 1954 when the country was divided, and the northern region was in the process of building socialism while the southern region was still embroiled in conflict. In those early years, the park served as a gathering place for southern cadres to hold monthly meetings. It wasn't until 1958 that the park officially opened to the public, becoming a beloved space for community activities and recreation. For many years, the park was known as Lenin Park, but it reverted to its original name, Thong Nhat, in 2003. A significant milestone occurred in late December 2022 when the park removed 400 meters of iron fencing along Tran Thanh Tong and Nguyen Dinh Chieu streets, marking a new era of free access for visitors.

Visitors to Thong Nhat park can enjoy a wide array of recreational activities. The park's vast expanse offers ample space for walking, cycling, and jogging, providing a peaceful setting for physical exercise and relaxation. Families and groups often organize picnics, team-building events, and camping trips within the park's lush surroundings. Additionally, the park features a picturesque lake called Ho Bai Mau, surrounded by a variety of flowers and ornamental plants, creating a vibrant and colorful landscape. The tranquil ambiance of the park is further enhanced by the presence of numerous benches and rock formations where visitors can sit and unwind.

One of the unique attractions within Thong Nhat park is the Mirror House, a distinctive structure that offers visitors a playful experience with optical illusions and interactive exhibits. The park also hosts various events such as book fairs and cultural festivals, enriching the visitor experience with educational and artistic programs. The park's central location makes it a convenient starting point for exploring nearby attractions like the Tran Nhan Tong pedestrian street, which is known for its lively atmosphere and local charm.

Visitors can enjoy a range of Vietnamese cuisine at nearby restaurants and cafes, offering a taste of local flavors and culinary traditions. The park itself is open daily from 6:00 AM to 10:00 PM, allowing visitors ample time to explore its natural beauty and participate in various activities throughout the day.

In summary, Thong Nhat park stands out as a vital green space in Hanoi, combining historical significance with modern amenities. Its rich history, diverse recreational opportunities, and serene natural environment make it a must-visit destination for anyone looking to escape the noise and chaos of the city. Whether you're a resident of Hanoi or a tourist visiting the city, Thong Nhat park offers a refreshing retreat that captures the essence of Vietnamese urban life.

Thong Nhat park, located at 354A Le Duan Street in Phuong Lien Ward, Dong Da District, Hanoi, is a sprawling oasis that has been a beloved green lung for the bustling capital city since its establishment in 1958. Spanning over 50 hectares, this expansive park is nestled between four major streets—Nguyen Dinh Chieu, Le Duan, Tran Nhan Tong, and Dai Co Viet—making it easily accessible to residents and visitors alike. The park's central location and vast expanse make it a popular destination for leisurely strolls, family outings, and recreational activities.

The history of Thong Nhat park is deeply intertwined with the political and social fabric of Vietnam. After the partition of the country in 1954, the park served as a clandestine meeting place for officials from both the North and South, facilitating secret gatherings and maintaining communication channels during a time of division. Over the years, the park evolved into a vibrant public space, hosting various events and becoming a symbol of unity and community spirit. From 1980 to 2003, the park was known as Lenin Park, but it reverted to its original name, Thong Nhat, after the renaming of another local landmark.

The park's layout is designed to offer a serene retreat from the urban hustle and bustle. At its heart lies Ho Ba Mai, a large lake surrounded by lush greenery and meticulously maintained flower beds. The park's extensive grounds feature numerous trees, including several ancient ones planted by Ho Chi Minh himself, which now stand as towering sentinels providing shade and tranquility. These trees, along with the many other species, contribute to the park's nickname as "the lungs of Hanoi," purifying the air and creating a refreshing atmosphere.

Visitors can explore the park's diverse landscape, which includes walking paths, open fields, and scenic spots perfect for relaxation. The park's central lake is a focal point, offering a tranquil setting for boating, fishing, and simply enjoying the water views. A small island in the middle of the lake is connected by a bridge, serving as a secluded spot for picnics and outdoor gatherings. The park also features a variety of recreational facilities, such as playgrounds, sports areas, and a charming area dedicated to children's entertainment, complete with miniature trains, pedal boats, and interactive play zones.

One of the most unique attractions within Thong Nhat park is the House of Mirrors, a fascinating structure that invites visitors to immerse themselves in a surreal world of reflections. This architectural marvel, constructed with ceramic tiles and mirrors imported from Czechoslovakia, spans approximately 812 square meters and recreates an underwater scene, allowing visitors to explore a simulated ocean environment filled with marine life and stunning underwater vistas. The house of mirrors is a testament to the park's commitment to providing educational and engaging experiences for all ages.

In addition to its natural beauty and recreational offerings, Thong Nhat park frequently hosts cultural events and festivals, drawing crowds from across the city. These events range from book fairs and food markets to music concerts and art exhibitions, enriching the park's cultural significance and making it a hub of activity throughout the year. The park's central location and spacious grounds make it an ideal venue for such gatherings, fostering a sense of community and shared enjoyment among visitors.

For those seeking a peaceful escape from the city's fast-paced lifestyle, Thong Nhat park offers a haven of tranquility. Whether it's early morning joggers, families enjoying a weekend outing, or tourists exploring the park's picturesque landscapes, the park remains a cherished space where one can connect with nature and experience the vibrant culture of Hanoi.

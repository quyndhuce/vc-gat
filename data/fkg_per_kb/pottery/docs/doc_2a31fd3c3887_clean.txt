In the heart of the Red River delta region, Bát Tràng pottery has been an integral part of Hanoi's soul for centuries. According to ancient tales, pottery-making families from the Bồ Bát region followed the river's banks to find a place with exceptional fine white clay, establishing the craft village and earning the name Bát Tràng, synonymous with kilns burning bright red throughout the year. Despite the passage of time and historical upheavals, the pottery here has retained its unique charm, characterized by celadon glaze, crackle glaze, and handcrafted patterns imbued with traditional spirit.

As one strolls along the old brick-paved alleys, the air is filled with the scent of damp earth, kiln smoke, and lively chatter, like a market of memories that never sleeps. Artisans meticulously shape clay on potter's wheels, while children laugh and play in pottery experience workshops. Tourists enthusiastically select each bowl, vase, or small statue bearing the characteristic deep brown glaze, their eyes drawn to the intricate hand-painted patterns on the traditional glaze background.

The ceramic shops in Bát Tràng appear densely packed with items of all sizes, from small cups and delicate vases to large jars and vats as tall as a person. Ivory white, blue, earthy brown, and antique crackle glazes cover the ceramic surfaces, creating a feeling that is both rustic and elegant. The most recognizable feature is the intricate hand-painted patterns on the traditional glaze background, a testament to the artisans' skill and dedication to their craft.

Surrounding the shops are typically red brick courtyards, old kilns, small alleys, and bustling crowds of people visiting to look and shop. Every glaze mark or handcrafted line on these ceramics preserves the artisan's handprint and the resilience of a craft village that has lived through time, making each piece a piece of Hanoi's memory.

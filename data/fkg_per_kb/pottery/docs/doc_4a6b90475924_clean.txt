Bát Tràng pottery, a renowned form of ceramic production, has its origins in the village of Bát Tràng, situated on the outskirts of Hanoi, Vietnam. This village is distinguished by its abundant supply of clay, ideal for crafting high-quality ceramics. Bát Tràng's pottery is celebrated alongside other notable Vietnamese ceramic centers such as Chu Đậu, Biên Hòa, Phù Lãng, Hương Canh, Lái Thiêu, and Bầu Trúc. The tradition of pottery-making in Bát Tràng dates back to the 14th century during the Ly-Tran dynasty.

Over time, Bát Tràng has evolved into a significant tourist attraction, drawing visitors from both within and outside Vietnam who come to explore, purchase, and even participate in the pottery-making process. On December 20, 2019, the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism of Vietnam officially recognized Bát Tràng pottery village as a national intangible cultural heritage. Today, the village boasts over 200 enterprises and more than 1,000 households engaged in the production and trade of various types of pottery, encompassing diverse designs and styles.

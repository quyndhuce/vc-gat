The Ly Thai To statue stands as a monumental tribute to King Ly Thai To, who reigned from 974 to 1028 AD, at the heart of Hanoi's historic Hoan Kiem district. Located in the Ly Thai To Garden, formerly known as Chi Linh Park, this iconic statue is situated along the bustling street of Dinh Tien Hoang, offering visitors a glimpse into the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam. The statue was inaugurated on October 7, 2004, marking a significant milestone in the city's cultural landscape, particularly as part of the celebrations for the 50th anniversary of Hanoi's liberation and in anticipation of the millennium anniversary of Thang Long-Hanoi in 2010.

The construction of the statue began on August 17, 2004, under the guidance of sculptor Vi Thi Hoa, a member of the Vietnam Fine Arts Association. The statue is crafted from solid bronze, weighing a total of 34 tons, with the figure itself weighing 14 tons and its base 20 tons. Standing at a height of 10.10 meters, the statue's dimensions symbolize the year 1010, the founding year of Thang Long, now known as Hanoi. This intricate design not only pays homage to the king but also serves as a testament to the enduring legacy he left behind.

The statue's creation process involved a rigorous selection process initiated by the Hanoi Department of Culture and Information in February 2003. From the initial 29 conceptual designs, the final choice was made after two rounds of evaluation and debate. The winning design, created by Vi Thi Hoa, was chosen for its artistic merit and its strategic placement within the garden, overlooking the serene waters of Hoan Kiem Lake. The statue's positioning reflects the historical significance of the site, aligning perfectly with the cultural and geographical importance of the area.

Visually, the statue captures the essence of King Ly Thai To, depicted in regal attire with a celestial crown, symbolizing his divine authority. His right hand holds the "Decree of Moving the Capital," while his left hand points towards the location where the capital was established. This gesture encapsulates the momentous decision that led to the founding of Thang Long, now Hanoi, as the imperial capital. The statue is mounted on a base shaped like an octagon, representing the eight cardinal directions, and is elevated above four gates that mirror those of the ancient Hanoi Citadel. The base is further adorned with three tiers of steps, each layer signifying heaven, earth, and humanity, reflecting the tripartite harmony that guided King Ly Thai To's decision-making process.

The Ly Thai To statue is more than just a monument; it is a living testament to the cultural and historical significance of Hanoi. It stands as a beacon of pride for the Vietnamese people, embodying the spirit of innovation and leadership that characterized King Ly Thai To's reign. Visitors to the statue can immerse themselves in the rich tapestry of Vietnamese history, feeling a connection to the past through the grandeur of the sculpture. The surrounding Ly Thai To Garden provides a tranquil setting, allowing visitors to reflect on the historical importance of the site while enjoying the lush greenery and scenic beauty of the area.

In addition to its historical and cultural value, the Ly Thai To statue offers a unique experience for tourists and locals alike. The atmosphere around the statue is imbued with a sense of reverence and awe, making it a popular destination for both casual strollers and those seeking a deeper understanding of Vietnam's storied past. As visitors approach the statue, they are greeted by the imposing figure of King Ly Thai To, whose presence commands respect and admiration.

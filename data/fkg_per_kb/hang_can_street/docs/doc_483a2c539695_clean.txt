Nestled within the heart of Hanoi's ancient quarter lies Hang Can Street, a place where time seems to stand still amidst the bustling modernity that surrounds it. At number 42 on this historic street, one can find a remarkable testament to the city’s rich heritage – a house that has stood the test of time for over a century. This ancient abode, owned by Mrs. Le Thi Thanh Tam, is a living museum, preserving the essence of old Hanoi through its unique architecture and storied past.

The house at 42 Hang Can Street was constructed towards the end of the 19th century and the beginning of the 20th century, serving as a residence and business hub for generations. It was once known as "Ich-An," a name imbued with positive connotations, symbolizing a life of purpose and tranquility. The original owners, the Tran Huu Lap family, were well-known for their successful trading business, which operated from this very location. The name "Ich-An" reflects the aspirations of the family, embodying a desire for a life filled with meaningful pursuits and serene happiness.

The structure itself is a masterpiece of traditional Vietnamese architecture, characterized by its elongated shape and intricate design. From the outside, the house appears as a slender box, typical of many homes in old Hanoi. Its dimensions are impressive, covering more than 100 square meters and stretching over 40 meters in length, making it a grand yet intimate space. The house's layout is divided into two floors, each serving distinct purposes. The ground floor houses a living room, two skylights that allow natural light to permeate the interior, a kitchen, and a bathroom. The upper level is dedicated to ancestral worship, a practice deeply rooted in Vietnamese culture.

One of the most striking features of the house is its preservation of original design elements. The entire structure, including the ceiling, wooden columns, windows, doors, and staircase, is crafted from lim wood, a material renowned for its durability and aesthetic appeal. These components have endured for over 130 years, standing as silent witnesses to the passage of time. Despite the surrounding neighborhood undergoing renovations and adopting modern aesthetics, this house remains steadfast in its traditional appearance. Hang Can Street, also known as Rue des Balances, is a charming and historic thoroughfare located in the bustling district of Hoan Kiem, Hanoi, Vietnam. The street's name, which translates to "Scale Street," reflects its historical significance as a center for the production and sale of scales, particularly those used for weighing precious metals like gold and silver, as well as dry goods. This unique moniker was adopted towards the end of the 19th century when the area began to specialize in the crafting and selling of these essential tools.

The origins of Hang Can Street trace back to the 19th century when it was initially named Hang Son, or Rue de la Laque, due to its proximity to the To Lich River. During this time, boats laden with lacquer from Phu Tho Province would dock at this stretch of the river, where merchants would sell their wares. However, as the river was eventually filled in, the trade shifted to other locations such as the Lò Sũ wharf, and the lacquer business moved to streets like Cau Go, Hang Be, and Hang Dau.

It was around this period that the craft of making and selling scales emerged in Hang Can Street. The small shops along the street began producing and selling various types of scales, including the traditional Vietnamese scale known as "can ta." These scales were used for weighing precious metals and herbs, as well as dry goods, and became a significant part of the local economy. As more shops opened and the scale-making industry expanded, the street's name was officially changed to Hang Can, reflecting its new identity as a hub for scale production and trade.

Today, Hang Can Street retains much of its old-world charm, with many of the buildings dating back to the colonial era. The narrow lanes and small houses are reminiscent of a bygone era, offering visitors a glimpse into the rich history and cultural heritage of Hanoi. The architecture of the houses is characterized by their modest size and simple design, often featuring wooden doors and windows adorned with intricate carvings. These structures not only serve as a testament to the city’s architectural evolution but also provide a serene backdrop against the vibrant urban landscape.

Visitors to Hang Can Street can explore the quaint shops that line the street, each one a repository of traditional craftsmanship and local culture. The atmosphere is imbued with a sense of nostalgia, as the sounds of hammering and the clinking of metal resonate through the air, echoing the industrious spirit of the past. The street is a living museum, where one can witness the meticulous process of scale-making, from the shaping of metal rods to the precise calibration of the scales. This hands-on approach to craftsmanship is a rare sight in today’s fast-paced world, making Hang Can Street a must-visit destination for those interested in preserving traditional arts and crafts.

In addition to its historical and cultural significance, Hang Can Street is also a place where one can experience the warm hospitality and friendly demeanor of the locals. The narrow streets are lined with small stalls and shops, each offering a unique array of products, from handcrafted scales to traditional Vietnamese snacks. The community here is closely knit, and the street remains a vital part of the neighborhood’s social fabric, hosting various cultural events and festivals throughout the year.

As visitors wander through Hang Can Street, they are transported back in time, surrounded by the sights, sounds, and smells of a bygone era. The street serves as a microcosm of Hanoi’s rich cultural tapestry, blending the old with the new, and offering a window into the city’s enduring traditions and heritage.

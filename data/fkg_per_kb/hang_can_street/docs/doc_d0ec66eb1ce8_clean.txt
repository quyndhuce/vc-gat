Hang Can Street is located within the Old Quarter of Hanoi, specifically in Hang Dao ward, Hoan Kiem district, stretching from Lan Ong Street to Hang Bo Street, with a length of 104 meters. Historically, this area was part of the Huu Dong Mon and Xuan Hoa villages, which belonged to the Thien Tuc prefecture of the former Tho Xuan county. In the mid-19th century, the prefecture was renamed Thuong My, and the village of Xuan Hoa merged with the village of Yen Hoa to form Xuan Yen.

The street is home to two temples: the Xuan Yen Temple of the old Xuan Hoa village at number 44, and the Xuan Yen Temple of the old Yen Hoa village at number 6A on Luong Van Can Street. The old village of Huu Dong Mon now has its communal house, the Dong Mon Communal House, at number 8 on Hang Can Street.

The name "Hang Can" originates from the historical production and sale of various types of traditional scales known as "can ta," which are horizontal scales with weights made of bronze or iron. During the French colonial period, the street was called "rue des Balances," which translates to "Hang Can Street" in Vietnamese.

Bus routes 31 and 146 terminate at the end of Hang Can Street.

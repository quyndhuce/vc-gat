Hang Khoai street is located in the Old Quarter of Hanoi, specifically within Hoan Kiem district, stretching from Tran Nhat Duat street to Hang Loc street, with a length of 360 meters. Historically, this area was part of the Huyen Thien village in the Hau Tuc district (later renamed Dong Xuan district) of the former Tho Xuan county. Today, Hang Khoai street falls under the jurisdiction of Dong Xuan and Hang Ma wards in Hoan Kiem district.

The name "Hang Khoai" originated from the various types of sweet potatoes sold by merchants in the past. Midway along the street, at number 54, stands the ancient Huyen Thien temple, also known as the Huyen Thien shrine, which venerates Huyen Thien Tran Vu, commonly referred to as "the saint," originally worshipped at the Quan Than temple. This temple is dedicated to Daoist worship and still houses a stele erected in the sixth year of the Canh Tri era (1668). Earlier, Trang Nguyen Dan had composed a poem praising this place:

"Ascending to heaven in broad daylight,

Seeking out Yao and Shun is difficult.

In the secular world for sixty years,

How can one face the golden-robed guests with shame?"

During the French colonial period, the street was known as rue des Tubercules, or Street of Tubers.

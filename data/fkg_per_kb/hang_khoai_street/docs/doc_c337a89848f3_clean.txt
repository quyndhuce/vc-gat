Nestled within the bustling heart of Hanoi's historic district, Hang Khoai street stands as a testament to the city’s rich agricultural heritage and vibrant market culture. This narrow thoroughfare, stretching for approximately 350 meters, is located in the Dong Xuan ward of Hoan Kiem district, just a kilometer north of Hoan Kiem Lake. The street runs parallel to Tran Nhat Duat Street, intersecting with Nguyen Thieu and Nguyen Thien Thuat streets, and Ha Giay and Dong Xuan streets, creating a lively network of commerce and community interaction.

The origins of Hang Khoai street date back to a time when it served as a central hub for farmers from nearby rural areas to sell their produce, particularly various types of potatoes and other agricultural products. The name "Hang Khoai" itself is derived from the word "khoai," meaning potato, reflecting the primary goods traded here. During the French colonial period, the street was known as Rue des Tubercules, or "Street of Potatoes," highlighting its continued importance as a marketplace for these root vegetables.

Today, Hang Khoai street remains a vital part of Hanoi’s economic landscape, where vendors gather early in the morning to sell fresh produce sourced from surrounding villages. The street is a bustling scene of activity, with carts laden with vegetables, fruits, and grains arriving from distant regions to supply the city’s markets and households. This daily ritual not only supports local agriculture but also enriches the urban food supply chain, ensuring that fresh produce reaches consumers throughout the city.

One of the notable landmarks along Hang Khoai street is the ancient Huyen Thien Temple, which sits prominently amidst the commercial bustle. Constructed in the 17th century, this temple originally served as a Quan Huyen Thien, a place of worship for Lao Buddhist monks and a significant spiritual center for the Huyen Thien Tran Vuong clan. Over time, the temple underwent several transformations, eventually becoming a Buddhist pagoda with the addition of statues of Buddha and bodhisattvas. Today, visitors can still see the original temple name inscribed on the main gate, a testament to its enduring cultural significance.

The history of Hang Khoai street is deeply intertwined with the broader narrative of Hanoi’s development. In the late 14th century, during the reign of Emperor Tran Nguyen Danh, the area around Hang Khoai was already recognized as a key trading zone. The emperor himself visited the Huyen Thien Quan and left behind a poem praising the site, marking it as a culturally significant location. As the centuries passed, the temple evolved, adapting to changing religious practices and societal needs, yet retaining its core identity as a place of spiritual and communal importance.

The architecture of Hang Khoai street reflects a blend of traditional Vietnamese and colonial influences. The narrow lanes are lined with old houses, many of which have been preserved over the years, offering a glimpse into the city’s past. These structures, often made of brick and wood, feature intricate carvings and ornamental details that speak to the skilled craftsmanship of local artisans. The street’s layout, with its winding paths and small alleyways, creates a sense of intimacy and connection, drawing visitors into the heart of the community.

Visitors to Hang Khoai street are greeted by a vibrant atmosphere that combines the sights, sounds, and smells of a traditional market. The air is filled with the aroma of freshly harvested produce, while the chatter of vendors and customers adds to the lively ambiance. Alongside the bustling market stalls, one can find small shops selling local handicrafts and traditional foods, providing a taste of Hanoi’s culinary heritage. The street’s proximity to Dong Xuan Market further enhances its role as a hub of commerce, making it a must-visit destination for those interested in experiencing the authentic flavors and traditions of northern Vietnam.

In summary, Hang Khoai street is more than just a street; it is a living museum of Hanoi’s agricultural and cultural heritage. From its historical roots as a potato market to its current status as a vibrant commercial and spiritual center, the street continues to play a crucial role in the life of the city. Visitors can immerse themselves in the rich tapestry of local life, exploring the market stalls, visiting the historic temple, and enjoying the warm hospitality of the community. Each visit to Hang Khoai street offers a unique window into the soul of Hanoi, a place where tradition and modernity coexist in perfect harmony.

The street begins at Tran Nhat Duct Street and ends at Hang Loc Street, intersecting with Nguyen Thiep Street and running alongside the left side of Dong Xuan Market, passing through the intersection of Hang Giay and Dong Xuan streets.

The origins of Hang Khoai date back to the time when it was part of the land known as Huyen Thien, which belonged to the Hau Tuc ward (later renamed Dong Xuan ward) in the old Tho Xuong district. The name "Hang Khoai" predates the French colonial era, but during their rule, the French referred to it as "rue des Tubercules," meaning "street of the tubers." After the August Revolution in 1945, the street reclaimed its original Vietnamese name, Hang Khoai, which has remained unchanged since then. Today, Hang Khoai falls within the jurisdiction of Dong Xuan and Hang Ma wards in Hoan Kiem district.

Historically, this street was a hub for vendors selling various types of potatoes and other root vegetables. At number 54 on Hang Khoai street stands the ancient temple known as Chua Huyen Thien, also called Den Huyen Thien, which is dedicated to Huyen Thien Tran, a deity worshipped at Quan Than Temple. This temple is significant as it represents a Daoist shrine, distinct from Buddhist temples. Inside the temple, there is a horizontal board inscribed with the words "Huyen Thien Co Quan," indicating its status as a place of Daoist worship. The term "Quan" here refers to a hermitage or retreat for Daoist practitioners, differentiating it from a Buddhist monastery.

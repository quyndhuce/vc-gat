Hang Gai Street, also known as Phố Hàng Gai, is a captivating thoroughfare nestled beside the picturesque Hoan Kiem Lake, its winding path weaving through the heart of the Old Quarter. This historic street has been a hub of commerce since the ancient imperial capital era, when it was renowned for its skilled artisans who wove ropes and hammocks from the prized gai fibers. The name Hang Gai has been etched in history, as the street gradually transformed, donning a new persona as the capital's most celebrated silk street. Today, it is a haven of elegance and prosperity, where tourists from around the world stroll leisurely, admiring the vibrant silk and brocade displays through glass windows, and trying on traditional áo dài garments.

As one meanders along the narrow, winding street, sandwiched between two rows of closely packed nhà ống, the shimmering colors of the silk and the dazzling yellow light from the glass windows immediately catch the eye. The unique identifying feature of the street is the mannequins wearing traditional áo dài, one after another, lining the entire facade, creating a visually stunning spectacle. This vibrant scene is nestled amidst the bustling traffic, leading directly to a tree-lined fountain square, where the sounds of laughter and chatter fill the air.

Hang Gai Street is a testament to the enduring aristocratic character of the land and people of Tràng An, where the past and present converge. The experience of caressing each cool silk thread brings a sense of tranquility, as one fully understands the elegant beauty and cultural significance of this iconic street. During Tết holidays, the street bursts with vibrant colors, as young women in their festive attire head to festivals, filling the air with joy and celebration. As one gets lost in this space imbued with the soul of the Orient, the heart is suddenly filled with a strange sense of wistfulness, a feeling that lingers long after the visit.

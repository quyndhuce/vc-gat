Hang Gai Street is a historic street located in the Old Quarter of Hanoi, stretching from Dong Kinh Nghia Thuc Square to Hang Bong Street. The street is 252 meters long. Prior to 2025, Hang Gai Street was part of Hang Gai Ward in Hoan Kiem District, but now it belongs to Hoan Kiem Ward, Hanoi City.

Historically, Hang Gai Street was originally part of Dong Ha (the eastern half) and Cau Vu (the western half), both of which were under the former Tien Tuc Commune (later renamed Thuong My Commune) in the old Tho Xuan district. The street houses two ancient communal houses: the Dong Ha communal house at number 46, dedicated to Quy Minh, a son of Mountain God who fought against Water God; and the Cau Vu communal house at number 85, dedicated to Bai Ma and Ling Lang. However, these communal houses have been converted into private residences and kindergartens.

In the past, Hang Gai Street specialized in selling various types of rattan products such as ropes, hammocks, and cords. However, since the 19th century, printing became an important industry in the area. Many shops specializing in engraving blocks, printing books, and selling books opened up, pushing the sellers of rattan products to move to Dong Thanh and Bat Dan streets. During the French colonial period, the street was known as rue de Chanvre and was home to two significant administrative buildings: the Governor-General's Residence at numbers 79-83 and the French Consul's Residence across the street at number 80. According to Nguyen Huu Ngoc, the former chief editor of the World Publishing House, who was born and raised in Hang Gai Street since 1918, number 83 was the residence of the Office of the Governor-General Nguyen Trong Hop. In his memoirs, musician Pham Duy mentions that the cafe Thien Thai, established in 1946 at number 83, belonged to the businessman Dang Tran Van, who moved to France after 1954.

Previously, bus routes 09 and 14 used to run through Hang Gai Street, but since 2011, these routes no longer pass through the area. Currently, only bus route 31 runs through the initial segment from Luong Van Can Street to Hang Trong Street.

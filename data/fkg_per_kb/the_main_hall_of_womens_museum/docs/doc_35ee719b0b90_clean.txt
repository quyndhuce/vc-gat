Amidst the tranquil surroundings of Ly Thuong Kiet Street, where the Vietnamese Women's Museum stands, the main hall emerges as a serene oasis, bridging the urban pace of life with the profound stories of Vietnamese women. Founded in the late 20th century under the care of the Vietnam Women's Union, this esteemed institution was established to preserve the memories of mothers and sisters who endured war, labor, and daily life with silent resilience.

As visitors step into the main hall, they are immediately struck by the expansive space, bathed in soft yellow light that pours through the large glass panels. The polished stone flooring, adorned with cream white tones and wood brown accents, creates a sense of elegance and coziness, inviting visitors to linger and absorb the stories on display. The central display cluster, featuring strips of women's portrait photos stretching along the open space, is a striking focal point, drawing the eye to the rich history and cultural significance of the museum.

The grand staircase, information desk, and visitors themselves become part of the tableau, as natural light pours in from the glass facade onto Ly Thuong Kiet street, lined with green trees. On occasions of thematic exhibitions or cultural events, the space echoes with conversations in many languages, the soft clicks of cameras, and the rustle of áo dài sweeping gently across the polished stone floor. What lingers most profoundly is not the grandeur of the space, but the warm feeling and respect for the very ordinary life stories that unfold within its walls.

As visitors explore the main hall, they are transported to a world of memories, where the struggles and triumphs of Vietnamese women are brought to life through photographs, artifacts, and cultural symbols. The museum's mission to preserve the past and honor the present is palpable, as the stories of mothers and sisters who endured war, labor, and daily life with silent resilience are shared with the world.

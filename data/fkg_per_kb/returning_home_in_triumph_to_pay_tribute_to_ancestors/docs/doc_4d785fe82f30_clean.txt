Let us journey back in time to witness one of the most magnificent and solemn performances on the water stage, the Vinh quy bai to, or "Returning Home in Triumph to Pay Tribute to Ancestors," showcased at the Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre. Situated majestically on the tree-lined Đinh Tiên Hoàng Street, the theatre stretches out over the windswept waters of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, a landmark that has stood since its opening in 1969. The theatre's name, Thăng Long, is imbued with historical significance, commemorating the moment when King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital to Thăng Long, now known as Hanoi, symbolizing the ascension of the golden dragon into the sky. This name serves as a testament to the preservation of the thousand-year-old cultural heritage of Vietnam's wet-rice civilization.

The performance itself is a vibrant celebration of scholarly achievement and traditional values. As the rhythmic beats of the trống phách drums and the enthusiastic sounds of the kèn bầu horns fill the air, the entire auditorium comes alive with the spirit of a village festival honoring academic excellence. Tourists from around the globe and local residents alike are captivated by the grand procession of the quan trạng, or high-ranking officials, who enter the village in a majestic palanquin, accompanied by resounding cheers. The spectacle is a testament to the deep-rooted tradition of respecting teachers and valuing education in Vietnamese culture.

The visual cues of the performance are equally captivating. A solemn procession of small figurines, each meticulously crafted, follows one after another. The vivid red and brilliant yellow colors of their attire accentuate the glossy sơn mài lacquer layers on the wooden cores, creating a striking contrast against the rippling water surface. The centerpiece of the display is the splendid võng lọng, or canopy, carried by the newly-graduated trạng nguyên, the top scholar, adding a regal touch to the scene. This majestic tableau stands out brilliantly against the rustic backdrop of the bamboo screens, enhancing the overall aesthetic appeal of the performance.

The Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre, located in the heart of Hanoi, is not just a venue for entertainment but also a cultural institution that preserves and celebrates the rich heritage of Vietnam. Its architectural style reflects the traditional Vietnamese design, blending seamlessly with the natural beauty of Hoàn Kiếm Lake. The theatre's location in the historic district of Hanoi, near significant landmarks such as Ba Đình Square and the One Pillar Pagoda, makes it a focal point for both tourists and locals seeking to immerse themselves in the country's cultural traditions.

This performance, Vinh quy bai to, is more than just a display of puppetry skills; it is a spiritual honor that touches the very roots of Vietnamese culture. It offers visitors a profound experience that connects them to the ancient traditions of respect for teachers and the value placed on education, even in the fast-paced urban environment of modern-day Hanoi.

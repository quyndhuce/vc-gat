Nestled on the eastern shore of Hoan Kiem Lake in Hoan Kiem District, Hanoi, the "Ready to Die for the Fatherland to Live" Monument stands as a silent sentinel, a testament to the indomitable spirit of Hanoi's defenders during a pivotal moment in Vietnam's history. Erected to honor the brave soldiers of the Capital Regiment and the resilient citizens of Hanoi who fought valiantly for 60 days and nights at the close of 1946, this monument commemorates the onset of the resistance war against French colonial rule. The phrase "To die so the Fatherland may live," which encapsulates the unwavering resolve of these fighters, has become the soul and essence of the monument, each sculpted detail narrating the tale of their heroic sacrifice.

The monument's central feature is a large bronze sculpture group mounted on a high stone pedestal, depicting a Hanoi soldier in a defiant pose, armed and looking resolutely ahead. The dark brown bronze, paired with the gray stone base, evokes a powerful and solemn atmosphere. The most striking element is the soldier's raised weapon, symbolizing the "Determined to Die" spirit that defined the resistance. Surrounded by lush greenery, a walking path along the tranquil waters of Hoan Kiem Lake, and the bustling city life, the monument stands as a poignant reminder of the past, drawing visitors and locals alike to reflect on the sacrifices made for the nation.

Today, the monument serves as a focal point for both tourists and residents, who often pause here after exploring the nearby Hoan Kiem Lake. Many take time to read the historical inscriptions, quietly photographing the site amidst the serene surroundings. During significant holidays and historical anniversaries, the area around the monument witnesses groups of people offering incense, laying flowers, and paying homage to the fallen heroes. The monument's cultural significance extends beyond its physical presence; it embodies the courage and resilience of Hanoi's people, fostering a sense of national pride and remembrance among all who visit.

The ‘Ready to Die for the Fatherland to Live’ Monument, a significant landmark in Hanoi, has been the subject of recent discussions regarding its relocation from its current site at the Đền Bà Kiệu complex in Hoàn Kiếm district. The monument, which was erected in 1984 to commemorate the 30th anniversary of the liberation of Hanoi, holds immense historical and cultural value. Its name is derived from a poignant quote by President Ho Chi Minh, reflecting the unwavering dedication of soldiers and civilians during the early days of the national resistance war.

The decision to move the monument comes after more than four decades since its establishment, during which time the urban landscape around Hoàn Kiếm Lake has undergone substantial changes. The city authorities have taken into account various suggestions and opinions from relevant agencies, experts, and scholars, who collectively agree that the monument's current location no longer aligns well with the evolving urban environment and the need to preserve its historical significance.

On August 22nd, the Party Committee of the Hanoi People's Committee presented a detailed report to the Standing Committee of the Hanoi Municipal Party Committee, outlining the proposed plan to relocate the monument. The committee unanimously agreed to proceed with the relocation but emphasized the importance of selecting a new location that would honor the monument’s historical and cultural importance. The chosen site must provide a solemn atmosphere conducive to public visits and educational tours, thereby enhancing the monument’s role in educating visitors about the spirit of sacrifice and patriotism embodied in the phrase "Ready to Die for the Fatherland to Live."

The Standing Committee has instructed that the final decision on the relocation will only be made after the completion of the Hoàn Kiếm Lake area's planning and development. This ensures that the new location will not only meet the criteria of historical and cultural significance but also integrate seamlessly with the broader urban renewal efforts in the heart of Hanoi. The process of relocating the monument will involve extensive research and consultation to ensure that the new site is appropriate and respectful of the monument’s legacy.

One suggested alternative location is the headquarters of the Hanoi Military Command, which is believed to be a fitting place due to its historical connection to the formation and growth of the city’s armed forces. This proposal reflects the monument’s original purpose and the historical context it represents, as it was designed to honor the bravery and sacrifice of the soldiers and civilians who fought for their country.

The monument itself stands as a testament to the resilience and determination of the people of Hanoi during times of conflict. It serves as a powerful reminder of the sacrifices made by those who fought for the nation's independence and freedom. Visitors to the monument often find themselves immersed in a profound sense of reverence and respect, reflecting on the historical events that shaped modern Vietnam.

In addition to the ‘Ready to Die for the Fatherland to Live’ Monument, Hanoi is home to other significant memorials such as the relief sculpture "Hà Nội Mùa Đông 1946" at Đồng Xuân Market and another monument with the same name located in Vạn Xuân Garden. These monuments collectively highlight the enduring spirit of the people of Hanoi during the initial stages of the national resistance war, reinforcing the city’s identity as a center of historical significance and patriotic fervor.

As the city continues to evolve, the relocation of the monument aims to maintain its integrity and relevance while ensuring that it remains accessible to both locals and tourists. The process underscores the city’s commitment to preserving its rich history and cultural heritage, even as it moves forward with urban development projects. The monument’s eventual new location will be carefully selected to ensure that it continues to serve as a powerful symbol of national pride and a place of reflection for future generations.

The 'Ready to Die for the Fatherland to Live' Monument, a significant landmark in the heart of Hanoi, stands as a testament to the bravery and sacrifice of those who fought to protect their homeland during the early days of the national resistance war. This monument, created by artist Kim Giao in 1984, has been a familiar sight at the Di tích Quốc gia đền Bà Kiệu for over four decades, serving not only as a memorial but also as an integral part of the city's cultural and historical landscape. The decision to relocate this iconic structure is part of a broader urban planning initiative aimed at enhancing the aesthetic and historical value of the eastern shore of Hoàn Kiếm Lake.

The monument's location near the Đinh Tiên Hoàng street, adjacent to the đền Bà Kiệu, opposite the serene waters of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, and close to the đền Ngọc Sơn and the Thê Húc bridge, has made it a focal point for both locals and tourists. Its presence has become synonymous with the spirit of patriotism and resilience that defines the people of Hanoi. The relocation of the monument to the Trung đoàn 692, Sư đoàn Bộ binh 301, Bộ Tư lệnh thủ đô, aims to preserve its historical significance while aligning with the city’s development plans.

The monument itself is a powerful ensemble of three figures, each representing different segments of society that united in defense of the capital city. At the center stands a soldier from the Vệ quốc đoàn, clutching a three-pronged grenade, symbolizing the fierce determination of the combatants who faced off against enemy tanks in the streets of Hanoi. This figure embodies the spirit of self-sacrifice and unwavering commitment to the cause of national liberation. Beside him is a young woman in traditional áo dài, holding a sword, which represents the active participation of women in the resistance movement. Her presence underscores the collective effort of the entire population in defending the city. The third figure is a worker, one hand gripping a rifle and the other holding a grenade, illustrating the solidarity between soldiers and civilians in the face of adversity. Together, these three figures form a cohesive unit, reflecting the unity and strength of the people of Hanoi during times of conflict.

The monument's design and construction reflect the artistic vision of Kim Giao, capturing the essence of the heroic struggle that took place in Hanoi during the winter of 1946-1947. Each element of the sculpture, from the dynamic poses to the choice of materials, conveys a sense of urgency and dedication. The bronze used in the creation of the monument adds a timeless quality to the work, ensuring that the memory of those who fought remains vivid and tangible.

In addition to the original monument at đền Bà Kiệu, there is another similar statue located in the Vạn Xuân garden, near the historic Đồng Xuân market. This second monument, inaugurated on December 22, 2004, is composed of 34 stone blocks, standing at a height of 9.7 meters and weighing over 300 tons. Designed by sculptor Vũ Đại Bình and painter Mai Văn Kế, it mirrors the central theme of the first monument, emphasizing the collective spirit of resistance and sacrifice.

Both monuments serve as poignant reminders of the 60-day battle that took place in Hanoi from December 19, 1946, to February 19, 1947, when the city was under siege. These statues not only honor the fallen heroes but also educate visitors about the pivotal role that Hanoi played in the larger context of the national resistance war. The monuments attract a steady stream of visitors, including school groups, history enthusiasts, and tourists, who come to pay respects and learn about the city's rich heritage.

As the monument prepares for its move to a new location within the military compound, efforts are underway to ensure that its historical and cultural significance is preserved. The relocation process will involve careful planning and coordination among various city departments to maintain the integrity of the monument and its symbolic importance. The move is expected to be completed before September 30, allowing time for the necessary preparations and adjustments to the new site.

The 'Ready to Die for the Fatherland to Live' Monument, with its powerful imagery and profound historical context, continues to resonate with the people of Hanoi and visitors alike. It stands as a living tribute to the courage and sacrifice of those who fought to protect their nation, embodying the enduring spirit of patriotism and resilience that defines the city.

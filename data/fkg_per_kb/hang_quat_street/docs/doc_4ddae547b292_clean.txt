Hang Quat Street is located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi, stretching approximately 200 meters and intersecting only with To Tich Street. It begins at Luan Van Can Street and ends at the junction of Hang Nom and Hang Hom Streets.

Originally, Hang Quat Street was part of the land belonging to the villages of To Tich and Thuong My, in the former district of Tien Tuc, Tho Xuong county. Later, it became part of Hang Gai ward, Hoan Kiem district, which was later merged into the Hoan Kiem ward. The street consists of three small streets combined: the eastern half is the old Hang Quat Street and Hang Dan Street, while the western half is Ma Vi Street. After 1945, the official name of the street was established as Hang Quat Street. Today, it belongs to the Hoan Kiem ward.

The eastern section of the street, known as Hang Quat, was formerly home to shops that sold fans made both by local production and transported from other places. The craft of fan-making was brought by people originally from Dao Xa village, also known as Dong Quat village in Hung Yen province. These settlers lived there, produced fans, and established a communal house to honor their ancestor, Mr. Dao.

The middle part of Hang Quat Street was previously called Hang Dan because many households made and sold various types of musical instruments such as moon lutes, zithers, monochords, and flutes. At that time, there were no large stores, only families engaged in woodworking crafts.

After the French colonizers arrived in Hanoi, the street was named "rue des Eventails." In the early 20th century, most shops specialized in making and selling intricately carved wooden items like dragon pavilions, sedan chairs, and lattice doors. Later, they shifted to producing ordinary furniture like tables, chairs, cabinets, and cupboards.

Today, the western end of Hang Quat Street, from the Dau Temple to the beginning of Hang Nom Street, was formerly known as Ma Vi Street. This area specialized in making and selling traditional costumes, theatrical attire, festival clothing, flags, banners, and stage props made from horsehair, hence the name Ma Vi (horse tail).

Currently, Hang Quat Street no longer sells fans or musical instruments. The shops now deal in various goods such as large and small altars, embroidered paintings, and calligraphy used for mourning, celebrations, longevity wishes, and commendations. Besides the Nguyen Van To Continuous Education School, the street also houses Nguyen Du Middle School and Nguyen Ba Ngoc School.

Perched atop the Gò Đống Đa mound, the majestic Trung Liet Temple stands as a testament to the nation's revered history. Established in the mid-19th century, this sacred sanctuary was built to honor the loyal subjects who sacrificed their lives for the country, their names etched in the annals of time. The temple's name, "Trung Liệt," is a poignant tribute to these heroes, whose bravery and devotion to the nation's cause are forever immortalized.

As one approaches the temple, the mossy gray of the fish-scale tile roof and the dark-colored baked brick walls come into view, exuding an air of serenity and tranquility. The rustic wood-framed hoành phi plaque, adorned with ancient calligraphy, hangs majestically above the main arched doorway, a striking visual feature that sets the tone for the temple's sacred atmosphere. The surrounding old si trees, with their tangled hanging roots and dark green canopies, provide a natural canopy, casting dappled shadows across the temple grounds.

The temple's architecture is a blend of traditional Vietnamese and French colonial influences, reflecting the country's complex cultural heritage. The structure's humble appearance belies its significance as a sacred site, where the spirits of countless righteous soldiers are believed to reside. The temple's tranquil atmosphere is a haven for visitors, who come to light agarwood incense and find solace in the heart of the bustling city.

During the festival on the fifth day of Tết, the temple is transformed into a vibrant hub of activity, with billowing incense smoke and the rhythmic beats of drums echoing from the foot of the mound. As the sun sets, the temple's porch becomes a sacred space, where visitors can breathe in the faint scent of agarwood incense and feel the resilient heartbeat of their ancestors. The experience is a poignant reminder of the nation's rich history and the sacrifices made by its heroes, leaving a profound and lasting impression on all who visit.

In the heart of Hanoi, the Trung Liet Temple stands as a testament to the nation's enduring spirit, a sacred sanctuary where the past and present converge.

In the enchanting world of Vietnamese water puppetry, the musicians and vocal performers are the unsung heroes who bring the ancient art form to life. These skilled artists sit in a row next to the thủy đình stage, their traditional instruments at the ready, including the trống, đàn nhị, sáo trúc, mõ, and thanh la. The vibrant colors of their folk costumes stand out against the rustic red wood backdrop of the stage, creating a visually stunning spectacle.

As the puppeteers manipulate the puppets on the water's surface, the musicians and vocal performers take on the crucial role of narrating the story, voicing dialogues, and creating sound effects. With every movement of the puppets, they harmonize their singing, shouting, laughing, and drumbeats to convey the emotions and atmosphere of the scene. The seamless coordination between the puppeteers behind the bamboo screen and the orchestra in front is nothing short of magical, bringing the entire stage to life.

The art of Vietnamese water puppetry has its roots in the 12th century, when it originated in the Bắc Bộ delta. Over the centuries, folk music has remained an integral part of every performance, with the musicians and vocal performers playing a vital role in transporting the audience to a bygone era. As the puppets dance on the water, the sounds of the instruments and the voices of the performers blend with the gentle lapping of the water, creating an immersive experience that transcends time and space.

The musicians and vocal performers are not just mere accompanists; they are the storytellers, weaving a tapestry of sound and emotion that draws the audience in. When the army goes to battle, when the countryside harvest scene unfolds, or when the mischievous Chú Tễu appears, it is their singing, shouting, and drumbeats that bring the puppets to life, allowing the audience to connect with the characters on a deeper level.

As the performance comes to a close, the audience is left with a lasting impression of the beauty and cultural significance of Vietnamese water puppetry. The musicians and vocal performers have not only entertained but also educated, sharing the rich heritage of their ancestors with the world. As the lights fade, the audience is left to ponder the timeless magic of this ancient art form, which continues to captivate and inspire audiences to this day.

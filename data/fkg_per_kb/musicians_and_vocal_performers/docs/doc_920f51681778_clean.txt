In the heart of Vietnam’s cultural heritage, the musicians and vocal performers play a pivotal role in the enchanting art form of water puppetry, which originated around the 12th century in the fertile plains of the Bắc Bộ delta. This ancient tradition seamlessly blends the grace of puppetry with the soul-stirring melodies of traditional Vietnamese folk music, creating a unique auditory experience that resonates deeply with audiences both local and foreign.

These artists, often seated in a designated area near the thủy đình—a small pavilion built over the water—bring to life the narratives of Vietnamese folklore through their mastery of various traditional instruments. The stage, framed by the curved roof of the thủy đình, serves as a backdrop for the harmonious interplay between the puppeteers hidden behind bamboo screens and the musicians positioned in front. The musicians, dressed in vibrant traditional attire that contrasts beautifully against the red wooden backdrop of the stage, are responsible for more than just playing music; they also narrate the stories, provide voiceovers for the characters, and create sound effects that enhance the overall theatrical experience.

Among the instruments commonly used are the trống (drums), đàn nhị (two-stringed fiddle), sáo trúc (bamboo flute), mõ (gong), and thanh la (clapper). Each instrument contributes uniquely to the soundscape, blending seamlessly with the gentle splashes of water to evoke a sense of nostalgia and connection to the rural landscapes of northern Vietnam. The musicians’ performances are meticulously synchronized with the movements of the puppets, ensuring that the emotional nuances of each scene are conveyed effectively to the audience.

When the character Chú Tễu, a beloved figure in Vietnamese folklore, makes his appearance, or during scenes depicting battles or harvest festivals, the musicians’ voices and instruments become the conduits through which the characters’ emotions are expressed. Their singing, shouting, laughter, and rhythmic beats help the audience understand the complex emotions and actions of the puppets, even though the puppets themselves remain silent. This seamless coordination between the puppeteers and the musicians transforms the stage into a living tableau, where the past and present converge in a celebration of Vietnamese culture.

Tourists visiting Vietnam often leave with vivid memories of the water puppetry performances, not just because of the graceful dances of the puppets on the water, but also due to the emotive folk sounds that echo throughout the venue. These sounds serve as a bridge, connecting the rich cultural heritage of the Bắc Bộ countryside with contemporary audiences, allowing visitors to experience firsthand the enduring traditions of Vietnamese folk music and storytelling. Through their performances, the musicians and vocal performers quietly yet powerfully convey the essence of Vietnamese culture, making each water puppetry show a memorable and culturally enriching experience.

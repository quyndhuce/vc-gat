Nui Sua Temple, nestled within the lush canopy of Hanoi's Botanical Garden, is an ancient and tranquil oasis in the heart of the capital city. Perched atop a high mound, considered a remnant of the ancient Núi Nùng, this sacred space is deeply rooted in the legend of Linh Lang Đại Vương, a deity believed to have aided Emperor Lý Thánh Tông in defeating invaders and protecting the imperial city of Thăng Long. According to folklore, Linh Lang Đại Vương, after becoming a deity, manifested on this mountain, leading the people to build a temple, and thus, the name "Núi Sưa" became inextricably linked with this sacred site.

The temple's small scale belies its profound significance, as its curved tiled roof, covered in moss, exudes an air of antiquity. The reddish-brown hue of the wooden walls and structure blends seamlessly with the deep green foliage of the surrounding ancient trees, creating an atmosphere of serenity. Winding steps lead visitors up to the temple, where they are enveloped by shaded spaces, stone-paved paths, and the fresh green landscape of the botanical garden.

As the sun rises or sets, Hanoians often stroll through the garden, pausing to light incense sticks and find a moment of peace amidst the urban bustle. The air is heavy with the scent of incense and the soft rustling of leaves, as if time has settled, allowing visitors to connect with the past. Leaving this sacred space, people often carry a sense of lightness, as if they have cleansed themselves of the noise and touched a deep corner of Hanoi's consciousness.

Nui Sua Temple is a testament to the city's rich cultural heritage, a symbol of the enduring connection between the past and present. As a sacred site, it has been a place of pilgrimage for generations, and its tranquil atmosphere continues to draw visitors seeking solace, contemplation, and a deeper understanding of Hanoi's storied history.

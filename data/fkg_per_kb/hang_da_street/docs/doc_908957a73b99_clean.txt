Hang Da Street is located in the Old Quarter of Hanoi, specifically within Hoan Kiem District, stretching from Duong Than Street to Hang Bong Street, with a length of 240 meters. Historically, this street was part of the village of Yen Noi, which belonged to the former Tho Xuan district's Tien Tuc commune (later renamed Thuan My). Today, it falls under the Cua Dong ward of Hoan Kiem District.

In the past, Hang Da Street was known for selling various types of cow and buffalo leather. The production of these leathers took place in the area between Tam Thuong alley and Yen Thai Street due to the availability of spacious areas suitable for drying and processing. At the beginning of Hang Da Street, there was a market by the same name, which was a small village-style market primarily selling fresh cow and buffalo hides that had been dried. The market consisted of only a few temporary shelters, making it a convenient venue for performances such as puppet shows and circus acts during the evenings. It wasn't until around 1937-1938 that a bridge was built at the market, giving it a more defined structure. In 1943, when Japanese forces were stationed in Hanoi and American planes began bombing the city, a raid hit the Hang Da Market, resulting in numerous casualties.

Midway along Hang Da Street stands a communal house called the Vu Du communal house, now numbered 42, dedicated to Le Cong Hanh, the founding father of embroidery craftsmanship. During the French colonial period, Hang Da Street was known as Rue des Cuirs.

The only bus route mentioned that passes through Hang Da Street is Route 01, which terminates at the end of the street.

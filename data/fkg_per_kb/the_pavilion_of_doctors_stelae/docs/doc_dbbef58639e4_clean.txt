The Pavilion of Doctors Stelae at the Temple of Literature in Hanoi is a remarkable testament to Vietnam's rich educational and cultural heritage. This site houses 82 stone steles, each meticulously carved to commemorate the names of successful candidates from the imperial examinations held during the Later Le Dynasty and the Mac Dynasty between 1442 and 1779. These steles are placed on the backs of stone turtles, symbolizing the enduring legacy of the nation's scholarly elite and reflecting the cultural and historical significance of Vietnam over a period of nearly three centuries.

The tradition of erecting these steles began in 1484 under King Le Thanh Tong, who initiated the practice to honor the achievements of Confucian scholars who passed the imperial exams. The first seven steles were erected for the examination years of 1442, 1448, 1463, 1466, 1475, 1478, and 1481. Over subsequent years, the Later Le Dynasty added five more steles for the examination years of 1487, 1496, 1502, 1511, and 1514. During the reign of the Mac Dynasty, only two additional steles were erected due to internal conflicts with the Later Le Dynasty, one for the examination year of 1518 and another for 1529.

The restoration of the Later Le Dynasty saw a resurgence in the erection of steles, with significant efforts made by the Later Le Dynasty to honor scholars. From 1653 onwards, a large number of steles were erected, with 25 steles being installed for the examination years from 1554 to 1652. Another major installation occurred in 1717 when 21 more steles were added for the examination years from 1656 to 1715. These additions brought the total number of steles to 68 out of the eventual 82, showcasing the dedication of the Later Le Dynasty to preserving the memory of its scholars.

Each stele is crafted with great care and precision, featuring a flat surface with a curved forehead and a vaulted shape. They are placed on the backs of stone turtles, which are sculpted in a uniform style, characterized by their robust and sturdy appearance. The process of creating these steles involves selecting suitable stones, designing and decorating them with intricate carvings, and inscribing the texts.

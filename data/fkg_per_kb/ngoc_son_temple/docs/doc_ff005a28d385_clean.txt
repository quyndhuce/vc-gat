Ngoc Son Temple, also known as Đền Ngọc Sơn, is situated on an islet in Hoàn Kiếm Lake in the heart of Hanoi, Vietnam. The temple's origins trace back to the early 19th century when it was initially established to honor the "Three Sages": Wenchang Dijun, Guan Yu, and Lü Dongbin. Over time, the revered national hero Trần Hưng Đạo was added to the pantheon of figures worshipped within its walls.

Visitors approaching the temple from the shore cross the iconic Thê Húc Bridge, which connects the mainland to the islet where Ngoc Son Temple stands. The temple complex includes several significant structures, each imbued with symbolic importance. Among these are the Tháp Bút, or "Pen Tower," the Đài Nghiên, or "Ink-slab," the Đắc Nguyệt, or "Moon Contemplation Pavilion," and the Đình Trấn Ba, or "Pavilion Against Waves." These buildings collectively contribute to the rich cultural and historical tapestry of Ngoc Son Temple.

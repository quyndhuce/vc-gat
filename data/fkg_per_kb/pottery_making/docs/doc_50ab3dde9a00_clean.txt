Bát Tràng Pottery, commonly known as Gốm Bát Tràng, refers to various types of Vietnamese pottery produced in the village of Bát Tràng, located in Bát Tràng Township, Gia Lâm District, Hanoi City, and also in Phụng Công Commune, Hưng Yên Province. The name Bát Tràng can be interpreted as "the large communal house specializing in bowls," but according to some historical documents, it may have originated from the term Bạch Thổ Phường, meaning "white clay town."

The village of Bát Tràng was established when King Lý Công Uẩn moved the capital from Hoa Lư to Thăng Long during the Lý Dynasty. Initially, the village was called Bạch Thổ Phường, inhabited by people who specialized in pottery and trade. In 1958, the construction of the Bắc Hưng Hải Canal created a new waterway that facilitated transportation to Bát Tràng, allowing travelers to reach the village via river routes from Hanoi's Chương Dương or Phà Đen docks, or through roadways along the Red River to Giang Cao Slope, approximately 15 kilometers away, or via National Highway 5 to Trâu Quỳ and then to Bát Tràng, around 20 kilometers away. Since 2006, the Hanoi Transport Company has operated bus route 47, connecting directly to the Bát Tràng Ceramic Market, making travel to the village more convenient.

According to the Đại Việt Sử Ký Toàn Thư and the Dư Địa Chí by Nguyễn Trãi, the Bát Tràng Pottery Village was formed during the Lý Dynasty. When King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital from Hoa Lư to Thăng Long, five prominent pottery-making families—Trần, Vương, Nguyễn, Lê, and Phạm—from Bồ Bát, Yên Mô Prefecture, Thanh Hóa Outer Prefecture (now part of Yên Mô District, Ninh Bình Province)—decided to relocate their artisans and families to the vicinity of Thăng Long to establish a new settlement. Upon arriving at Bạch Thổ Phường, which is now part of Bát Tràng Commune, Gia Lâm District, Hanoi, they found suitable clay for pottery production. These families collaborated with the local Nguyễn clan to set up kilns and create the Bát Tràng Pottery Village.

Local folklore and genealogies indicate that one of the clans in Bát Tràng is the Nguyễn Ninh Tràng family. Some suggest that this clan originated from the Ninh Tràng Kiln in Ninh Bình, but there is no confirmed evidence. Genealogical records of other clans like Lê, Vương, Phạm, and Nguyễn trace their ancestors back to Bồ Bát, which was part of the Bạch Bát Administrative Unit in Yên Mô Prefecture, Thanh Hóa Outer Prefecture during the late 14th and early 15th centuries. Today, Bồ Bát consists of two villages, Bồ Xuyên and Bạch Bát, within Yên Thành Commune, Yên Mô District, Ninh Bình Province, where white clay suitable for pottery is abundant. Legends and genealogies of the Vũ clan in Bồ Xuyên suggest that the inhabitants of Bồ Bát were long-time potters. This is corroborated by the discovery of numerous layers of fired earth and fragments of pottery in the area.

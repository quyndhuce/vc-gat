The village of Bat Trang, renowned for its pottery making, is a captivating destination nestled along the banks of the Red River in Hanoi, Vietnam. With a history spanning over five centuries, Bat Trang has been a cornerstone of Vietnamese ceramic artistry since the time of the Ly Dynasty in the 14th and 15th centuries. The village's origins trace back to when the capital was moved from Hoa Lu to Thang Long, now known as Hanoi. Five prominent clans—Tran, Vuong, Le, Nguyen, and Pham—migrated from the pottery village of Bo Bat in Ninh Binh, bringing with them their expertise in crafting ceramics. Over the years, these artisans have honed their skills, contributing to the diverse range of products that Bat Trang is celebrated for today.

Bat Trang is not merely a village but a living museum where visitors can immerse themselves in the rich tapestry of Vietnamese pottery traditions. The village spans across two hamlets, Giang Cao and Bat Trang, within the commune of Bat Trang, Gia Lam District, Hanoi. It is a place where one can witness the seamless blend of ancient techniques and modern innovation, creating a unique atmosphere that resonates with the essence of Vietnamese culture.

Visitors to Bat Trang are greeted by a vibrant landscape of clay workshops, bustling markets, and historic sites. The village's layout is designed to facilitate exploration, allowing tourists to wander through narrow streets lined with traditional houses and encounter the intricate process of pottery making at every turn. The village's architecture reflects its deep-rooted heritage, with structures that range from simple clay huts to more elaborate buildings adorned with intricate carvings and designs that pay homage to the craft.

One of the highlights of visiting Bat Trang is the opportunity to explore the ancient houses and the Wan Van House, which serves as a private museum showcasing the evolution of Bat Trang pottery over the centuries. This house, established as a repository of ceramic artifacts from the 15th to the 19th century, offers a glimpse into the historical significance of the village's pottery industry. Additionally, the Bat Trang Ceramic Museum, inaugurated in 2021, presents a modern interpretation of the traditional pottery-making process, featuring exhibits that highlight the artistic and cultural value of Bat Trang ceramics.

The village also hosts a vibrant market where visitors can peruse a wide array of ceramic goods, from household items to decorative pieces. Here, tourists can engage in the joy of bargaining while selecting souvenirs that reflect the unique craftsmanship of Bat Trang. The market is a testament to the village's commitment to preserving and promoting its traditional crafts, offering a hands-on experience that allows visitors to interact directly with local artisans and learn about the meticulous processes involved in creating each piece.

For those interested in a more immersive experience, Bat Trang offers workshops where visitors can participate in the actual process of making pottery. Under the guidance of skilled craftsmen, tourists can create their own ceramic pieces, gaining insight into the delicate balance between tradition and creativity that defines Bat Trang pottery. These workshops not only provide a practical understanding of the craft but also foster a deeper appreciation for the skill and dedication required to produce each item.

In addition to the pottery workshops, Bat Trang is home to several historical landmarks that enrich the visitor's experience. The ancient kilns, such as the Lo Bau kiln, stand as enduring symbols of the village's long-standing tradition in pottery making. These kilns, some of which date back to the early days of the village, are still operational and continue to produce high-quality ceramics that embody the spirit of Bat Trang.

The village's cultural significance extends beyond its physical boundaries, influencing the broader community and contributing to the preservation of Vietnamese cultural heritage. Bat Trang's pottery is not only a source of pride for the local population but also a symbol of national identity, recognized both domestically and internationally. Visiting Bat Trang is an experience that combines historical discovery, artistic engagement, and cultural immersion.

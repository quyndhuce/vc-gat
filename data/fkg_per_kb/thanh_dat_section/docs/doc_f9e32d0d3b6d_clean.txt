In the heart of Hanoi, nestled within the revered Temple of Literature complex, lies the serene and historic Thanh Dat Section, a tranquil oasis that embodies the profound educational philosophy of Vietnam's ancient scholars. This sacred space, formed during the golden age of the Lý Dynasty, is a testament to the country's rich cultural heritage and its enduring reverence for learning.

As one steps through the moss-covered threshold of Đại Trung Môn, the eye is drawn to the spacious rectangular courtyard, its time-worn reddish-gray Bát Tràng bricks blending seamlessly with the lush green lawns on either side. The unique identifying feature of this tranquil haven is the pair of rectangular ponds, Trường Trì, which lie serenely and symmetrically, like a pair of bright eyes, surrounded by ancient trees that seem to connect seamlessly towards the towering Khuê Văn Các.

The Thanh Dat Section, founded by Emperor Ly Thanh Tong of the Lý dynasty in 1070, is a sacred space that has witnessed generations of students seeking clarity of mind and unwavering determination to overcome the challenging exams ahead. During exam seasons or graduation ceremonies, the courtyard comes alive with the radiant smiles of scholars, their bachelor's gowns a testament to their hard-won achievements. As one strolls through the tranquil shade, the atmosphere is filled with an unusually steadfast belief, as if the very air is infused with the spirit of reverence for learning.

This historic courtyard area, recognized as a national special relic and UNESCO-recognized heritage site, is a must-visit destination for anyone seeking to experience the cultural and educational significance of Vietnam's ancient traditions. As one walks through the gates of the Temple of Literature, the Thanh Dat Section awaits, a serene and sacred space that embodies the eternal aspiration for scholars to achieve success, pass exams, and attain glorious achievements after countless nights of diligent study.

In the heart of Hanoi's historic district, nestled amidst the tranquil surroundings of the Temple of Literature complex, stands the majestic Thai Hoc buildings, a testament to the city's rich educational heritage. This two-story wooden structure, with its towering columns and moss-covered yin-yang roof tiles, is a masterpiece of traditional Vietnamese court architecture, proudly curving upwards towards the sky. The massive dark-brown wooden columns, adorned with intricate carvings, seem to stretch towards the heavens, while the layered curved roof appears to be a work of art in itself.

As one approaches the complex, the scent of incense wafts through the air, carrying the whispers of generations past, who once walked these hallowed halls. The Thai Hoc buildings, a national special relic and UNESCO-recognised heritage site, have witnessed the country's tumultuous history, from the destruction of the ancient campus in 1946 to its grand restoration in 2000. Today, this sacred space is a hub of reverence, where students and visitors from far and wide gather to pay homage to the eternal teacher Chu Văn An and the enlightened monarchs who contributed to the revival of national education.

As the sun casts its golden rays upon the complex, the atmosphere is filled with an air of gratitude, as the rhythmic chanting of incense and the soft rustling of leaves create a sense of serenity. The courtyard, surrounded by ancient tree canopies, is a peaceful oasis, where the sounds of the city fade away, replaced by the whispers of the past. The Thai Hoc buildings, a symbol of Vietnam's long educational tradition, stand as a testament to the country's rich cultural heritage, a reminder of the importance of respecting teachers and valuing morality.

Visitors can explore the complex during its opening hours, from 8:00 to 17:00, and pay an entrance fee of approximately 30,000 VND. As they wander through the halls, they can almost hear the whispers of the past, as the ancient stones seem to hold the secrets of generations gone by. The Thai Hoc buildings, a masterpiece of Vietnamese architecture, are a must-visit destination for anyone seeking to experience the country's rich cultural heritage and educational traditions.

Hang Ma Street, located in Hanoi, is renowned for its diverse array of goods, particularly toys, paper products, and paper votive offerings. With a history spanning over five centuries, this street has been a hub for trading paper goods, contributing significantly to the local economy and cultural heritage. Among the various items sold here, paper votive offerings stand out as a specialty, reflecting both traditional and contemporary Vietnamese beliefs and practices.

These votive offerings include an extensive range of joss paper goods designed to be burned as gifts for the deceased. Modern versions feature replicas of everyday items such as cars, villas, mopeds, and even electronic gadgets like iPads, alongside more traditional offerings like paper servants and horses. These items symbolize the desire to provide comfort and necessities to loved ones who have passed away.

Additionally, the street sells "hell banknotes," which are imitation currencies and credit cards, often referred to as ghost money. These items serve as symbolic representations of wealth and financial resources intended for the afterlife. Historically, during periods of suppression prior to Vietnam's economic reforms, the production and sale of these offerings were restricted. However, Hang Ma Street continues to thrive, embodying the resilience and adaptability of its merchants and the enduring traditions it represents. The term "đồ hàng mã," or "Hàng Mã things," has become synonymous with paper votive offerings, further cementing the street's significance in Vietnamese culture.

Hang Dau Street, a historic artery in Hanoi, carries within its cobblestones the echoes of centuries past, where the traditions of commerce and culture intertwine seamlessly. The name "Hang Dau" itself is a testament to the street's historical significance, derived from the tradition of naming streets after the goods sold there. In days gone by, during market days, villagers from the outskirts would carry various types of beans—such as green beans, black beans, and soybeans—to sell at this bustling thoroughfare. This practice gave rise to the street's original name, reflecting the agricultural heritage of the region.

During the French colonial era, Hang Dau Street was known as Rue des Graines, meaning "Street of Seeds," and served as a boundary marker between the Cua Bac and Cua Dong districts. After the August Revolution in 1945, the street reclaimed its traditional name, Hang Dau, symbolizing continuity and resilience in the face of change.

Today, Hang Dau Street is not merely a relic of the past but a vibrant hub that has evolved over time. Following World War I, the development of transportation led to an increase in automobiles, transforming the street into a center for automobile repair shops and parts suppliers. The strategic location of Hang Dau Street made it a gateway to Hanoi, attracting numerous businesses that catered to the growing number of vehicles. Many families prospered through these ventures, alongside others who thrived in woodworking and pharmaceuticals.

The street still retains vestiges of its ancient past, with notable landmarks such as the Nghia Lap communal house at number 32, which stands as a testament to the community's spiritual life. Additionally, two private elementary schools, one named Cuc Hien, founded by the patriotic scholar Le Dinh Duyen in 1878, continue to educate generations of students. These institutions, along with other structures like the Thang Long Water Tower, contribute to the rich tapestry of Hang Dau Street's history.

The Thang Long Water Tower, one of Hanoi's iconic architectural marvels, once supplied water to the inner city. Constructed in the early 20th century, it stood as a symbol of modernity and progress. However, after 1954, the tower fell into disuse and was repurposed for various functions until 2003 when it underwent restoration to restore its original grandeur. Today, it remains a significant landmark, drawing visitors interested in the city's architectural heritage.

Beyond the immediate vicinity of Hang Dau Street, there are several sites that bear its name, such as the Hoa Dau Garden (formerly known as Van Xuan Garden) and the Hoa Dau Guard Post. These locations, while not directly on the street, are integral to the area's identity and cultural landscape.

The cultural significance of Hang Dau Street extends beyond its commercial activities. It is a place where history and modernity converge, offering visitors a glimpse into the layered narrative of Hanoi's evolution. The atmosphere of the street is imbued with a sense of continuity, where old traditions blend with contemporary life. Visitors can explore the quaint shops, admire the architecture, and experience the vibrant energy of the local community.

In addition to its commercial and historical importance, Hang Dau Street also hosts cultural events and festivals that celebrate the city's heritage. These events provide a platform for artists and performers to showcase their talents, enriching the cultural scene and fostering a deeper appreciation for Hanoi's diverse traditions. The street's role as a living museum allows visitors to immerse themselves in the rich tapestry of Vietnamese culture, from its culinary delights to its artistic expressions.

Overall, Hang Dau Street is more than just a street—it is a microcosm of Hanoi's complex history, a place where the past meets the present, and where the spirit of the city lives on in every corner.

The historic Bốt Hàng Đậu, located at the bustling intersection of several streets including Hàng Than, Hàng Lược, Hàng Giấy, Hàng Đậu, Quán Thánh, and Phan Đình Phùng in the Ba Đình district of Hanoi, has officially opened its doors to visitors from November 17th to December 31st, 2023. This iconic structure, also known as the Tháp Nước Hàng Đậu, was constructed in 1894 during the colonial era by the French, making it one of the oldest water towers in Hanoi. The tower stands as a testament to the city's rich architectural heritage, blending elements of French colonial design with local traditions.

The Bốt Hàng Đậu is a cylindrical structure with a diameter of 19 meters and rises three stories high, topped by a conical roof that resembles a pointed hat. At the center of this distinctive roof is a lightning rod, ensuring the safety of the building and its visitors. The tower houses a large steel reservoir with a capacity of 1,250 cubic meters, which sits atop eight stone walls, providing a significant water storage capacity for the surrounding area. Despite the passage of time, the Bốt Hàng Đậu retains much of its original appearance, showcasing the enduring quality of its construction.

Inside the tower, visitors can explore a unique artistic space that combines modern installations with the historical significance of the site. The interior features a series of sound and light installations designed to highlight the importance of water in daily life and the impact of urbanization on natural environments. These installations include a sound arrangement that recreates the sounds of water in nature, and a lighting system that emphasizes the effects of urban development on the natural world. The aim is to educate visitors about the role of water in sustaining life and to foster a deeper connection between people and their natural surroundings.

The artistic displays within the Bốt Hàng Đậu are not only visually striking but also thought-provoking. Visitors can walk through a wooden path lined with sound-emitting jars, each producing different tones when touched, creating an immersive auditory experience. Additionally, there are exhibits made from recycled materials, demonstrating the theme of sustainability and environmental conservation. These interactive elements provide a unique and engaging experience for tourists visiting Hanoi, offering them a glimpse into the city’s past while reflecting on contemporary issues.

The opening of the Bốt Hàng Đậu to the public marks a significant milestone in the preservation and promotion of Hanoi’s cultural heritage. It serves as a bridge between the old and new, inviting visitors to appreciate the historical value of the structure while experiencing innovative artistic expressions. For those interested in exploring the rich tapestry of Hanoi’s history and culture, the Bốt Hàng Đậu offers a fascinating destination that seamlessly blends architectural grandeur with modern artistic interpretation.

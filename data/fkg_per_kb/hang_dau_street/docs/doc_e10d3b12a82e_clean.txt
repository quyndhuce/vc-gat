Hang Dau Street, also known as Phố Hàng Đậu, is a captivating thoroughfare that stretches across the northern edge of Hanoi's Old Quarter, weaving together the past and present of the capital. This picturesque street, once a bustling dock for merchants, has evolved over the centuries, its name etched by the cries of sellers who traded seeds, mung beans, and soybeans from upstream boats on the Red River. Today, the street's vibrant colors and lively atmosphere remain, as tourists and locals alike stroll along the bustling sidewalks, weaving through the crowds to hunt for a favorite item or linger at a street corner to sip iced tea and engage in lively conversation.

The street's unique charm is evident in its row of narrow tube houses, packed closely together along the curving asphalt road. The vibrant colors of modern signs and moss-covered yellow walls create a striking visual contrast, while the long rows of shoe racks and shimmering glass aquariums displayed along the sidewalk add to the street's eclectic charm. The bàng trees' wide-spreading canopies provide a natural canopy, sheltering the street from the hustle and bustle of the surrounding traffic.

As you walk along Hang Dau Street, you are transported back in time to the 19th century, when merchants would dock their boats and sell their wares to eager customers. The street's name, Hang Dau, is a testament to its rich history, which dates back to the time when the Red River was the lifeblood of Hanoi's economy. Today, the street remains a hub of activity, with vendors selling everything from souvenirs to street food, and tourists and locals alike drawn to its unique blend of old and new.

Despite the changes that have taken place over the centuries, Hang Dau Street remains a beloved landmark in Hanoi's Old Quarter, a testament to the city's enduring cultural heritage. As you explore this charming street, you are reminded of the city's rich history, its cultural significance, and its enduring appeal as a destination for tourists and locals alike.

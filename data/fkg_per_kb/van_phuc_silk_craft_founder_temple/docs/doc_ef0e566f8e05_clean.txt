Nestled quietly within the bustling life of the thousand-year-old weaving village in Ha Dong District, the Van Phuc Ancestral Temple stands as a serene sanctuary, preserving the soul and essence of the imperial land. According to legend, from ancient times, Thành hoàng A Lã Thị Nương, a virtuous and talented woman, stopped here to teach the villagers the intricate art of mulberry cultivation, silkworm rearing, and silk reeling and weaving. The temple was established to honor her significant contributions and to perpetuate the name "Van Phuc," symbolizing aspirations for abundant blessings and perpetual prosperity.

Stepping through its threshold, the vibrant energy of daily life outside seems to fade, making way for a tranquil spiritual space. The temple, a revered site for both local villagers and distant visitors, serves as a place where people light incense and offer the finest silk fabrics to show their gratitude to the craft ancestors. During the village festival, the atmosphere becomes particularly sacred and lively, marked by solemn palanquin processions and sacrificial rituals.

The temple’s architecture is a testament to its historical and cultural importance. Its imposing ancient structure rises gracefully, adorned with intricate carvings and painted in deep red, contrasting beautifully with the dark brown lim wood columns. The most striking visual feature is the grand hoành phi, richly lacquered and gilded in gold, prominently displayed in the main hall. This sacred edifice is nestled under the shade of a lush ancient banyan tree, adjacent to a calm, clear blue lake, creating a harmonious blend of nature and human craftsmanship.

The Van Phuc Ancestral Temple not only serves as a place of worship but also as a cultural landmark, embodying the enduring vitality of the proud craft village in the heart of Hanoi. It stands as a living testament to the principles of “drinking water, remembering the source,” reflecting the deep-rooted respect for tradition and the continuous legacy of the silk craft.

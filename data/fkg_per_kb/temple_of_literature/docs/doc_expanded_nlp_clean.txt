Temple of Literature (Văn Miếu - Quốc Tử Giám) is located in Dong Da district, Hanoi. It is a confucian temple and historic educational complex.
The Temple of Literature was founded in 1070.
The Imperial Academy was established in 1076.
Surviving architecture and scholarly monuments reflect later restorations and additions.
The complex is arranged in successive courtyards along a principal axis.
Van Mieu Gate leads toward Dai Trung Gate.
Khue Van Cac precedes the Thien Quang Well and doctoral stelae pavilions.
Dai Thanh area and Thai Hoc buildings lie farther inward.
Characteristic physical features include courtyard gates, Khue Van Cac with circular openings, Thien Quang Well, stone doctoral stelae on turtles, halls and tiled roofs.
Temple of Literature is associated with the following named places and physical sub-entities: Van Mieu Gate, Dai Trung Gate, Khue Van Cac, Thien Quang Well, doctoral stelae, Dai Thanh courtyard, Thai Hoc buildings, Van Lake.
The site is associated with confucian scholarship, imperial examinations and educational memory.
The Temple of Literature contains a sequence of enclosed courtyards connected by gates.
Van Mieu Gate forms the principal entrance to the historic complex.
Dai Trung Gate stands along the central approach.
Khue Van Cac stands between Dai Trung Gate and Thien Quang Well.
Khue Van Cac has circular window openings and a tiled roof.
Thien Quang Well is a rectangular water feature within the courtyard sequence.
The doctoral stelae stand in sheltered pavilions beside Thien Quang Well.
Stone turtles support the doctoral stelae.
The inscriptions on the stelae record successful candidates in imperial examinations.
The Dai Thanh area contains buildings associated with Confucian worship.
The Thai Hoc area is associated with the historical educational function of the site.
Van Lake is associated with the wider Temple of Literature landscape.

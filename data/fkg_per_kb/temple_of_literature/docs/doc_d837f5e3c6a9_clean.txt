Nestled in the heart of Hanoi, specifically at 58 Văn Miếu Street in Đống Đa District, lies the Temple of Literature, or Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám, a revered historical site that stands as a testament to Vietnam’s rich cultural heritage. Founded in 1070 under the reign of Emperor Lý Thánh Tông of the Lý dynasty, the Temple of Literature was initially established to honor Confucius, the great Chinese philosopher, and later evolved into Quốc Tử Giám, Vietnam’s first national university during the reign of Lý Nhân Tông. This transformation marked the beginning of a long-standing tradition of scholarly excellence and academic pursuit in Vietnam.

The architectural ensemble of the Temple of Literature is a masterpiece of traditional Vietnamese imperial design, characterized by its layered courtyards, ancient brick walls, and curved tile roofs. As one approaches the complex, the first visual cue is the imposing tam quan gate, a quintessential feature of Vietnamese temples, which leads to a series of serene courtyards. These courtyards are adorned with shady green trees and ancient brick walls that provide a tranquil atmosphere, contrasting sharply with the bustling city outside. The dark brown, curved tile roofs add a touch of elegance and solemnity to the overall ambiance, creating a space that feels both timeless and sacred.

One of the most striking features of the Temple of Literature is the khu bia tiến sĩ, or the area dedicated to the stone steles of doctoral laureates. Here, 82 stone steles, each mounted on the back of a stone tortoise, are arranged in neat rows. These steles bear the names of successful candidates who passed the imperial examinations, serving as silent witnesses to the golden age of academic achievement in Vietnam. The presence of these steles underscores the cultural significance of the site, symbolizing the enduring legacy of education and intellectual pursuit.

Beyond its historical and architectural grandeur, the Temple of Literature holds a special place in the hearts of Vietnamese people, particularly students and scholars. It is a place where dreams are nurtured and aspirations are fulfilled. During exam seasons, students flock to the temple to pray for success and seek blessings, believing that the spirits of past scholars will guide them towards a brighter future. The temple also hosts various cultural events and festivals throughout the year, attracting visitors from all walks of life who come to experience the blend of tradition and modernity that permeates the site.

Recognized as a Special National Relic and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Temple of Literature is open daily from 8:00 to 17:00, with an admission fee of approximately 30,000 VND. Its significance extends beyond its physical boundaries, embodying the spirit of studiousness and academic excellence that has been deeply ingrained in the Vietnamese psyche for centuries. The temple continues to serve as a beacon of hope and inspiration, reminding visitors of the enduring power of knowledge and the importance of preserving cultural heritage for future generations.

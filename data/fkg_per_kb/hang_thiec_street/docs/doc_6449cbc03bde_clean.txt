Hang Thiec Street is an ancient street located within Hang Gai ward, Hoan Kiem district, in the city of Hanoi. It is one of the few remaining historic streets in Hanoi that still preserves both the sounds and the traditional craft of metalworking. In the past, the name of the street originated from its role as a center for handmade tin and lead casting, serving the daily needs and spiritual requirements of the residents of the imperial capital.

Historically, the origins and settlers of Hang Thiec Street can be traced back to the village of Dan Hoi (formerly part of Ha Dong). According to the genealogies of long-standing families like the Tran clan, the founder of the tin-crafting profession was granted the title of "Tich cuc truong," or head of the Tin Management Bureau, by the royal court during the Le dynasty. Over time, artisans from nearby villages known for their metalwork, such as Phu Thu (now part of Hoai Duc), Thuan Tien, and Bac Ninh, migrated to Hang Thiec Street to establish their businesses, making it a bustling center for manufacturing household goods.

As time progressed, the materials used in the production shifted. Initially, all products were made from tin casting, primarily items associated with religious rituals and daily life, such as incense burners, candles, oil lamps, tea kettles, tea bags, and hat tips. By the mid-20th century, when pure tin products no longer had a large market, the craftsmen of Hang Thiec Street quickly adapted. They began recycling iron containers brought by the French, transforming them into washbasins, water scoops, and clothing chests. Despite changes in materials over time—from tin to iron, zinc, and now stainless steel—the traditional craft of handcrafted metalworking has remained intact.

The name of Hang Thiec Street has become deeply ingrained in the folk consciousness through classic folk songs about the streets of Hanoi:

"Let's play around Long City,

Thirty-six streets clearly named without error.

Street of Willow, Street of Silver, Street of Reed,

Sailor Street, Tin Street, Sandal Street, Tray Street..."

During the colonial period, the French authorities respected the local production identity and named the street Rue des Ferblantiers, meaning "Street of Tin Workers." In 1945, the then Governor of Hanoi, Tran Van Lai, officially restored the original Vietnamese name, Hang Thiec Street, which has been maintained until today.

Today, Hang Thiec Street retains a unique vibrancy within the old quarter. The most distinctive feature is the characteristic sound of hammering and clinking from small workshops along the street, creating a lively artisanal atmosphere amidst modern urban life.

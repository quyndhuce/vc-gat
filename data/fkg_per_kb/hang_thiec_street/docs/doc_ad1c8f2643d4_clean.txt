Hang Thiec Street, or Phố Hàng Thiếc in Vietnamese, is a hidden gem nestled within the thirty-six streets of Hanoi's Old Quarter. This ancient thoroughfare has been a hub of artisanal activity since the fifteenth century, when skilled craftsmen from Phú Thứ tin casting village brought their traditional craft to the imperial capital. The street's name, Hàng Thiếc, is deeply rooted in the consciousness of the people of Tràng An, as it has been a testament to the enduring craftsmanship of the region.

As one strolls down the narrow street, sandwiched between two rows of leaning nhà ống, the sparkling silver-gray hue of the tôn sheets and sắt tây immediately catches the eye. The unique identifying feature of the street is the multitude of hòm tôn, buckets, basins, and metal cake molds hanging precariously, strung across the facades. This bustling scene is nestled beneath a tangled network of electrical wires, adjacent to the narrow sidewalk always crowded with a stream of people and vehicles moving back and forth.

The street's history dates back to the fifteenth century, when artisans from Phú Thứ tin casting village brought their traditional craft to the imperial capital. Over time, they shifted from crafting incense burners and candle holders offered to sacred places to shaping and welding tinplate and galvanized iron. The rhythmic clinking sound of hammers, the engrossing sight of artisans deftly shaping shiny sheets of corrugated iron into buckets, basins, or household items, and the distinctive working atmosphere all combine to create a unique sensory experience.

During the Mid-Autumn Festival, the street becomes even more bustling and lively as crowds flock to buy handmade molds for baked mooncakes and sticky mooncakes. The experience of listening to the rustic street symphony brings a deep sense of connection, helping everyone fully understand the enduring craftsmanship of a thousand-year-old craft street in Thăng Long. As one stands amidst the sound-filled space, one's heart is suddenly filled with admiration mixed with nostalgia, a testament to the street's enduring cultural significance.

Hang Thiec Street is a must-visit destination for anyone interested in experiencing the rich cultural heritage of Hanoi's Old Quarter. The street's unique blend of history, architecture, and cultural significance makes it an unforgettable experience, one that will leave a lasting impression on all who visit.

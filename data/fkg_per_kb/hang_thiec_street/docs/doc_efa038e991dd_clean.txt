Nestled within the vibrant heart of Hanoi’s Old Quarter, Hang Thiec Street, also known as Phố Hàng Thiếc, stands as a testament to the enduring legacy of traditional craftsmanship. Emerging from the bustling tapestry of the thirty-six streets, this narrow thoroughfare has been a hub of metalworking since the fifteenth century. Originating from the skilled artisans of Phú Thứ tin casting village, who brought their traditional craft to the imperial capital of Thăng Long to establish their livelihood, Hang Thiec Street has become synonymous with the intricate art of tin and metalwork.

The street’s historical roots trace back to the mid-15th century when artisans from Phú Thứ began crafting incense burners and candle holders for religious offerings. Over time, these craftsmen shifted their focus to shaping and welding tinplate and galvanized iron, giving rise to the name Hang Thiếc, which translates to Tinware Street. This shift marked the beginning of a long-standing tradition that continues to thrive today, deeply embedded in the cultural consciousness of the people of Tràng An.

Visually, Hang Thiec Street is characterized by its narrow, straight path flanked by rows of倾斜的窄巷房屋（nhà ống），这些房屋之间悬挂着各种金属制品，如桶、盆和金属月饼模具。这些闪闪发光的银灰色锡片和铁板与岁月的尘埃交织在一起，构成了独特的视觉特征。街道上布满了错综复杂的电线网络，狭窄的人行道上总是挤满了来往的人流和车辆。

Hang Thiec Street not only serves as a center for metalworking but also holds significant cultural and historical importance. The rhythmic sounds of hammering and bending metal sheets create a distinctive atmosphere that draws both tourists and locals alike. Visitors are often captivated by the sight of artisans deftly transforming sheets of corrugated iron into functional household items, such as buckets and basins. During the Mid-Autumn Festival, the street transforms into a hive of activity as people flock to purchase handmade molds for baking mooncakes, adding a festive touch to the already bustling scene.

This vibrant street symphony, a blend of traditional craftsmanship and modern hustle, offers a glimpse into the rich heritage of Hanoi. It stands as a living museum, preserving the skills and traditions of generations past, and continues to be a vital part of the city’s cultural landscape.

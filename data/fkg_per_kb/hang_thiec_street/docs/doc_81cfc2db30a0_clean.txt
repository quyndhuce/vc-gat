The origins of this street date back to the time when it was part of the village of Yen Noi, situated in the former district of Tien Tuc, Tho Xuan.

In the past, Hang Thiếc Street was renowned for its traditional craft of casting tin to produce household items such as incense burners, tea kettles, and trays. These products were highly esteemed and represented the pinnacle of local craftsmanship. However, by the mid-20th century, the demand for tinware had diminished, leading artisans to adapt their skills to work with iron goods brought by the French. Items like oil cans were transformed into washbasins, ladles, and water carriers, earning the street the French name Rue des Ferblanties, or "Street of Tin Workers." Despite this, the locals continued to call it Hang Thiếc, preserving its original identity.

The period between the 1930s and 1940s marked the golden age of Hang Thiếc, where not only metalworking but also glass and mirror crafting flourished. The small street was a hive of activity, with workshops and stores operating side by side, contributing to a vibrant atmosphere that continues to attract visitors today.

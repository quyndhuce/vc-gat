The Montreal Masonic Memorial Temple, also known as the Temple maçonnique de Montréal, stands as a significant landmark in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, located at the intersection of Sherbrooke Street and St. Marc Street within the prestigious Golden Square Mile district. Officially dedicated and opened on February 12, 1930, this historic masonic temple was recognized as a National Historic Site of Canada in 2001, celebrated for its elegant Beaux-Arts architecture.

The concept of the Memorial Temple emerged as a tribute to Freemasons who lost their lives during World War I, replacing an earlier Masonic Temple situated in a mixed-use building on Dorchester Street since 1895. The Masonic order initiated renovations in 1908 and began fundraising for a new structure in 1923. In 1928, they commissioned architect John Smith Archibald, who had previously worked on the Dorchester Street Temple, to design and oversee the construction of the new temple.

The cornerstone-laying ceremony took place on June 22, 1929, with a grand procession of thirty-six lodges and approximately 2,000 Masons marching from the old Dorchester Street Temple to the new site. The Grand Lodge of Quebec convened for the first time in the Memorial Temple on February 12, 1930, during its 60th Annual Communication. The temple honors not only those who perished in World War I but also those who died in World War II and the Korean War.

Architecturally, the Memorial Temple's facades on Sherbrooke and St-Marc streets are clad in Queenston limestone. The main facade on Sherbrooke Street features a rusticated limestone base with four openings and a prominent central entrance flanked by two freestanding columns topped with terrestrial and celestial spheres. The main door is crafted from detailed architectural bronze, and a decorative belt course adorned with ornamental carvings and the words FIDES, VERITAS, CARITAS, LIBERTAS, and SPES (representing Faith, Truth, Charity, Liberty, and Hope) marks the upper part of the base.

In December 1930, the journal Construction highlighted the temple's architectural excellence, comparing it favorably to renowned works by architects such as McKim, Mead and White, and praising it alongside other notable structures like the Lincoln Memorial and the J.P. Morgan Library. One year later, the Royal Architectural Institute of Canada awarded its First Award, Class I, for Monumental Buildings to the Montreal Masonic Memorial Temple.

On January 30, 2010, Senator W. David Angus announced that the Government of Canada would provide infrastructure funding of $425,000 to support the conservation efforts of the Masonic Memorial Temple National Historic Site of Canada.

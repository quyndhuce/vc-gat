Nestled in the heart of Hà Nội, the revered Hồ Hoàn Kiếm serves as a serene backdrop for a cherished tradition that has flourished for decades. Along its picturesque shores, particularly near the bustling stone-paved paths, one can witness the timeless craft of portrait painting, an art form that has become an integral part of the city's cultural tapestry. This practice, which began during the challenging years of the subsidy period, has evolved into a beloved custom, drawing visitors from far and wide who seek to capture a piece of Hà Nội's enduring charm.

The portrait painters, often found under the shade of lộc vừng trees, work diligently with rustic wooden A-frame easels, their tools consisting of pristine white paper and deep black charcoal. These artists, with hands smudged from the fine charcoal dust, skillfully render the likenesses of their subjects, creating portraits that are more than mere images—they are windows into the soul of Hà Nội. The tranquil setting of the lake, with its emerald green waters reflecting the sky above, provides the perfect canvas for these intimate moments of artistic expression.

Visitors, whether locals or tourists, often find themselves captivated by the allure of having their portrait sketched. They sit quietly on small stools, posing patiently as the artists bring them to life on paper. The process is not just about capturing a likeness; it is about preserving a moment of tranquility, a snapshot of time spent in one of Vietnam's most historic and culturally rich cities. The portraits, with their delicate lines and expressive details, serve as tangible souvenirs of a visit to Hà Nội, embodying the elegant and romantic spirit of the city.

This tradition is deeply rooted in the historical and cultural heritage of Hà Nội, connecting the present-day bustle of the city with its storied past. The legend of King Lê Lợi returning the sacred sword to the golden turtle adds a layer of mystique and reverence to the practice, making each portrait a link between ancient solemnity and contemporary life. As visitors leave with their portraits in hand, they carry with them not only a piece of art but also a profound sense of the city's timeless beauty and the warm hospitality of its people.

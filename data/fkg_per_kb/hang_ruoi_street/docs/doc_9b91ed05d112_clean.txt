Hang Ruoi Street, a charming and historically rich artery in Hanoi's bustling Old Quarter, is a testament to the city’s dynamic past and vibrant present. The street, known for its former association with the sale of a local delicacy called rươi, has undergone significant transformation over the years, evolving into a hub of floral crafts and vibrant commerce. Originally named Rue des Vers Blancs by the French during their colonial era, the street was later renamed Hang Ruoi, reflecting its historical connection to the white larvae of certain fish species, which were once sold here in abundance.

The street stretches for 108 meters and is 6 meters wide, connecting Hang Luoc Street at one end and Hang Ma Street at the other. Historically, it was part of the ancient town of Vinh Truc, which included the northern section, and An Phu, which comprised the southern portion. The street is also associated with the Tong Huu Tuc, now known as Xuan Street, and the Hue Xuong district. Notably, the ruins of the Vinh Truc Pagoda can be found at number 59 Hang Luoc Street, while the An Phu Pagoda is located at number 17 Hang Ruoi Street, now converted into a residential building.

In the past, Hang Ruoi Street was renowned for selling rươi, a seasonal delicacy that was harvested during two periods each year: the beginning of May and the end of September according to the lunar calendar. The tradition of selling rươi was so integral to the street’s identity that even today, during these brief periods, visitors can hear the distinctive calls of vendors selling this rare delicacy. The phrase "Tháng chín đò mưới, tháng mưới mang năm" encapsulates the seasonal nature of rươi, highlighting its importance in Vietnamese culinary culture.

Today, Hang Ruoi Street no longer primarily sells rươi but has transformed into a bustling center for floral crafts. Over two-thirds of the shops along the street now sell various types of artificial flowers, made from materials like plastic and nylon, offering a range of colors and styles. These flowers are often arranged in intricate designs, creating a visually appealing display that attracts both locals and tourists. Many shops also offer workshops where visitors can learn the art of flower arrangement, adding an educational dimension to the shopping experience.

The atmosphere of Hang Ruoi Street is particularly festive during the Lunar New Year celebrations, when the entire street transforms into a sea of red and gold decorations. As early as December, the shops begin to decorate their storefronts with vibrant red and golden hues, hanging strings of lanterns, paper chains, and other festive ornaments. This creates a warm and welcoming ambiance that invites visitors to explore the street and immerse themselves in the holiday spirit.

Visitors to Hang Ruoi Street will find a variety of attractions and activities. The street is lined with boutique shops selling unique handicrafts, including Hoa La Phuong Linh at number 10, Hoa La Ha Phuong, and Hoa La Chuoc An, among others. These shops not only sell beautiful artificial flowers but also offer a glimpse into the local craft traditions and the creativity of the artisans who create them. Additionally, there are several cafes and hair salons that cater to the needs of shoppers and visitors, providing a comfortable space to rest and enjoy a cup of coffee or a haircut.

The street’s architecture reflects its historical significance, with many buildings dating back to the colonial period. One notable structure is the house at number 4, which served as the residence of Tran Phu, a prominent figure in Vietnamese history, during his tenure as the head of the Central Committee of the Communist Youth Union. Although he later moved to number 90 on Thanh Nhuom Street, the house remains an important landmark, symbolizing the intersection of political and cultural life in Hanoi.

Surrounded by other historic streets such as Dao Duy Tu, Cau Go, and Ca Tho, Hang Ruoi Street forms part of a larger network of narrow, winding lanes that make up the heart of Hanoi’s Old Quarter. Each street offers its own unique character and charm, contributing to the rich tapestry of Hanoi’s urban landscape. Visitors can wander through these streets, exploring the myriad of shops, cafes, and cultural sites, immersing themselves in the city’s rich heritage and contemporary vibrancy.

In summary, Hang Ruoi Street is a fascinating destination that combines historical significance with modern commercial activity. Its transformation from a street dedicated to the sale of rươi to a vibrant center for floral crafts showcases the adaptability and resilience of Hanoi’s community. Whether you are interested in learning about the city’s culinary traditions, admiring the intricate designs of artificial flowers, or simply enjoying the lively atmosphere, Hang Ruoi Street offers a memorable experience that captures the essence of Hanoi’s dynamic culture.

Hang Ruoi Street, formerly known as Rue des Vers Blancs during the French colonial era, is a historic street located in the heart of Hanoi, Vietnam. The street's name is derived from its past as a bustling market for rươi, a rare and highly prized delicacy found only in the northern region of Vietnam. This unique culinary specialty has given Hang Ruoi Street its distinctive identity and cultural significance, making it a must-visit destination for food enthusiasts and tourists alike.

The origins of Hang Ruoi Street date back to the time when the area was a river port on the To Lich River. Every year, during the ninth month of the lunar calendar, people from nearby provinces such as Thai Binh, Hai Duong, Hai Phong, and Nam Dinh would converge here to sell and buy rươi, giving rise to the name "Hang Ruoi." Under French rule, the street was officially named Rue des Vers Blancs, which translates to "Street of White Worms," reflecting the local dialect term for rươi. In 1945, after the establishment of the Vietnamese government, the street was officially renamed Hang Ruoi Street.

One of the most significant landmarks on Hang Ruoi Street is the house numbered 4, which served as the headquarters for the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Vietnam from 1929 to 1930. This site holds immense historical importance as it was where Tran Phu, the first General Secretary of the Party, worked diligently to draft the first Political Platform of the Party. This historical legacy adds a layer of cultural richness to the street, making it not just a culinary destination but also a place of historical significance.

The annual appearance of rươi coincides with the ninth month of the lunar calendar, specifically around the 20th of September and the 5th of October. During this brief period, the street transforms into a vibrant hub of activity, attracting numerous visitors eager to experience the unique flavors of rươi. Despite its short availability, rươi is considered a delicacy that embodies the distinct culinary traditions of Northern Vietnam. Unlike elaborate dishes like nem công chả phượng (pheasant meat rolls), rươi offers a simple yet unforgettable taste that few have had the pleasure of enjoying.

Today, Hang Ruoi Street is home to several restaurants that specialize in preparing various dishes made from rươi. These include chả rươi (rươi sausage), rươi hấp (steamed rươi), rươi xào (stir-fried rươi), rươi nấu (cooked rươi), nem rươi (rươi spring rolls), rươi kho (braised rươi), and rươi rang (fried rươi). Each dish showcases the versatility and richness of rươi, contributing to the street's reputation as a culinary haven. Scientific studies have recognized rươi as a nutritious food source, rich in protein and safe for consumption, further enhancing its appeal among food lovers.

While Hang Ruoi Street no longer functions primarily as a market for rươi, it has evolved into a dynamic commercial center with a diverse range of businesses. Many of the original households that once sold rươi have transitioned to other trades to increase their income. Today, the street is renowned for its floriculture, adding a touch of beauty to the bustling city of Hanoi. The vibrant colors of flowers and the aromatic scents fill the air, creating a serene atmosphere that contrasts with the hustle and bustle of urban life.

During the Mid-Autumn Festival, Hang Ruoi Street becomes one of the three designated locations in Hanoi for organizing lantern festivals for children. About a month before the full moon in August, the street is adorned with colorful decorations, drawing hundreds of children each night to purchase traditional toys such as paper lanterns, masks, and spinning tops. This festive atmosphere not only brings joy to the young but also enriches the cultural tapestry of the street.

As the Lunar New Year approaches, Hang Ruoi Street begins to prepare for the celebrations early, typically starting in December. The shops along the street are festively decorated with red and gold hues, symbolizing good fortune and prosperity. Decorations such as golden coins, couplets, flower garlands, lanterns, and potted plants adorn the storefronts, creating a lively and festive ambiance that sets the tone for the upcoming holiday season.

Throughout the year, Hang Ruoi Street remains a vibrant part of Hanoi's cultural landscape. Its historical significance, combined with its current role in promoting floriculture and hosting cultural events, makes it a unique destination for visitors. Whether one comes to savor the rare delicacy of rươi, admire the beautiful floral arrangements, or participate in traditional festivals, Hang Ruoi Street offers a rich and immersive experience that reflects the enduring charm and cultural heritage of Hanoi.

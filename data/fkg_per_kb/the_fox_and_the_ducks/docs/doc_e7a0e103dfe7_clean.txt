The Fox and the Ducks, known in Vietnamese as "Đánh cáo bắt vịt," is a vibrant and beloved traditional water puppet performance that captures the essence of rural life in Northern Vietnam, particularly in the Red River Delta region. This lively folk tale is set against the picturesque backdrop of tranquil ponds and rivers, where a flock of ducks leisurely swims across the water, only to be interrupted by a cunning fox seeking to catch its prey. The performance, which has been a staple of Vietnamese cultural heritage for centuries, reflects the close-knit relationship between farming communities and their livestock, symbolizing the harmony and resilience of village life.

Originating from the rich folklore of the Red River Delta, the performance dates back to the Ly Dynasty (1009-1225), a period marked by significant advancements in agriculture and cultural development. The fox in the story embodies cunning and mischief, while the ducks and farmers represent the peaceful, industrious spirit of village life. The performance not only entertains but also imparts valuable lessons about vigilance, cooperation, and the importance of protecting one's livelihood.

The water puppet stage, a unique architectural feature of Vietnamese puppetry, is typically constructed near traditional water pavilions or communal ponds. These stages are designed to allow the puppets to move freely in the water, creating dynamic and fluid performances. The puppets themselves are intricately crafted from wood and bamboo, with each figure meticulously carved and painted to reflect the characters' personalities and roles in the story. The fox, with its sharp eyes and agile movements, stands out as a master of deception, while the ducks, with their graceful gliding and playful interactions, embody the simplicity and charm of rural life.

Accompanying the puppet movements are energetic drumming and entertaining narration, which bring the story to life and engage audiences of all ages. The live folk orchestra, often featuring traditional instruments like the đàn bầu (monochord) and đàn tranh (zither), adds a layer of cultural richness to the performance. The shimmering reflections on the water and the rustic ambiance of the setting create a mesmerizing visual experience that transports viewers into the heart of rural Vietnam.

Today, The Fox and the Ducks remains one of the most popular water puppet acts, captivating both local audiences and international tourists alike. It serves as a window into the traditions and daily life of rural Vietnam, offering a charming glimpse into the country's cultural heritage. For many visitors, the performance provides a delightful introduction to the art of water puppetry, a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity since 2011. Through its humor and storytelling, the performance conveys simple yet profound messages about community, cooperation, and the joys of rural life, making it a cherished part of Vietnam's cultural landscape.

Nestled alongside the picturesque Hoàn Kiếm Lake, Hang Trong Street stands as a harmonious bridge connecting the charming Old Quarter to the grandeur of European architecture. Historically part of the Tự Tháp village, renowned across the imperial capital for its traditional crafts such as drum-making, parasol production, and vibrant woodblock printing, the area was named Hang Trong due to the resonant sounds of drums that once filled the air, symbolizing the cultural richness of Vietnam. Even today, despite the absence of the original craftsmen, the street retains its elegance and vitality, attracting both tourists and locals who wander through its narrow lanes, admiring contemporary art galleries, browsing exquisite lace embroidery shops, and savoring the famous cà phê trứng hidden in mossy alleyways. The architectural landscape is a blend of French villas and traditional tube houses, their pale yellow facades covered in moss, contrasting beautifully with the deep green canopies of ancient sấu trees. Art galleries and lace shops, their vibrant displays visible through transparent glass panes, add a touch of modernity to the historic setting. The serene ambiance, infused with the nostalgic scent of ink from the past and the rich aroma of coffee, evokes a profound sense of peace and pride, allowing visitors to fully appreciate the refined elegance of the ancient land of Thăng Long.

Hang Trong Street is an ancient street in Hanoi, running from the end of Hang Gai Street to the middle of Le Thai To Street. The street has a length of 396 meters. Prior to 2025, Hang Trong Street was part of Hang Trong ward in Hoan Kiem district, but now it belongs to Hoan Kiem ward, Hanoi city.

According to Hanoi scholar Nguyen Vinh Phuc, Hang Trong Street was built on land from several old villages: the section near Hang Gai Street was from the Cau Vu village; the middle section was from the Khanh Thuy Hau village; and the final section was from the Tu Thap village. All these villages were part of the former Tien Truc commune (later renamed Thuong My) in the old Tho Xuan county.

The name "Hang Trong" refers to the people who made communal drums from the Lieu Thang village (now part of Yen My district, Hung Yen province) who moved here to live and trade. They sold various types of drums such as large drums, small drums, table drums, rice-cooking drums, and carrying drums. Additionally, there were also residents from Dao Xa village (Thuong Tin district, former Ha Tay province) who specialized in making canopies, and people from Tu Thap village who were skilled in painting.

At the beginning and middle sections of the street, shops that make drums, canopies, and print paintings are located. At the end of the street, embroidery shops owned by people from Quat Dong and Huong Duong regions (Thuong Tin district, former Ha Son Binh province) are situated.

Today, Hang Trong Street still retains two ancient temples. In the middle of the street, at number 82, is the Dong Huong temple, also known as the Hang Trong temple, which worships a female performer. At the end of the street, at number 75, is the Nam Huong communal house, primarily dedicated to the White Horse deity and Ling Lang.

Bus route 31 runs through the entire length of Hang Trong Street.

Hang Phen Street, located in Hoan Kiem District of Hanoi, is a charming and historically significant thoroughfare that stretches for 100 meters and is 7 meters wide. The street runs from Thuoc Bac Street to Hang Ga Street, intersecting with Bat Su Street along its course. This narrow and bustling lane has a rich history dating back to the late Le and early Nguyen dynasties when it was known as Dong Thanh Market, a bustling trading hub. During the French colonial period, the street was referred to as rue du Vieux Marché, or Old Market Street, reflecting its longstanding role as a commercial center. In 1945, the street was officially renamed Hang Phen Street, a name that has remained unchanged since then.

The origins of Hang Phen Street trace back to the time when it was part of Dong Thanh Village, which belonged to the former Tho Xuan district's Tien Tuc Commune, later renamed Thuan My Commune. The street's name, Hang Phen, is derived from the various types of alum sold there, including alum powder, blue vitriol, and black alum. These substances were essential for daily life, particularly for water purification before the advent of modern plumbing systems. People would use these alums to clarify water drawn from rivers, lakes, and wells, ensuring it was safe for consumption. Additionally, the street was known for selling paper used for wrapping goods, such as rice paper, bamboo leaf paper, and alum paper, alongside other sundry items.

The cultural significance of Hang Phen Street extends beyond its commercial activities. It serves as a testament to the ingenuity and resourcefulness of the local population, who relied on natural resources and traditional methods to meet their everyday needs. The street's architecture reflects the typical Vietnamese urban layout, characterized by closely packed houses and shops, often with ornate wooden doors and windows. The buildings are constructed using traditional materials like brick, wood, and tile, showcasing the craftsmanship of local artisans.

Visitors to Hang Phen Street can immerse themselves in the vibrant atmosphere of a traditional market, where the sounds of vendors calling out their wares blend seamlessly with the chatter of shoppers. The narrow lanes are lined with small shops and stalls, each offering a unique array of products. The street's cultural importance is further highlighted by its continued relevance in the community, serving not only as a commercial hub but also as a social gathering place where locals exchange news and engage in casual conversations.

Despite the passage of time, Hang Phen Street retains much of its original charm and character. The preservation of its name and function underscores the enduring legacy of traditional practices and the resilience of the local community. Visitors can explore the street to gain insight into the historical and cultural heritage of Hanoi, experiencing firsthand the continuity of traditions that have shaped the city's identity over centuries.

Hang Ngang Street, also known as 行昂, is a street located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi. It runs from Hang Dao Street to Hang Duong Street and is one-way traffic.

The origin of its name remains unclear and uncertain. Historically, Hang Ngang Street was part of the Dien Hung ward, in the Tien Tuc district of Tho Xuan city during the time of Thang Long. In the early 18th century, the upper part of the street was adjacent to Hang Dao Street and was called Hang Lam Street, where goods such as blue silk were sold. By the 19th century, it was renamed Viet Dong Street, which was a bustling trading area for Chinese immigrants from Guangdong. The prosperity of this Chinese community might have given rise to the name "Hang Ngang," possibly due to the practice of closing two gates at both ends of the street each evening. Today, Hang Ngang Street belongs to the Hang Dao ward in Hoan Kiem district.

During the French colonial period, the street was named Rue des Cantonais (Street of Guangdong people) and had a tramway running through its center. Currently, Hang Ngang Street, connecting with Hang Dao Street, is a vibrant commercial area characteristic of Hanoi. It operates as a one-way street and becomes pedestrian-only on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays.

In the time of the Le dynasty, Chinese immigrants concentrated on selling tea, medicine, and luxurious fabrics like silk and brocade. Later, Indian merchants added shops selling high-quality textiles. Nowadays, Hang Ngang Street is known for selling fashionable clothing and luxury consumer goods. There are also several portrait painting shops along the street.

Among the notable landmarks on Hang Ngang Street is number 48, where President Ho Chi Minh wrote the Declaration of Independence in 1945, establishing the Democratic Republic of Vietnam. At that time, the house belonged to Mr. Trinh Phuc Loi, who allowed the government to use it for work purposes. Today, the house has been converted into a museum.

Hang Tre Street, formerly known as Hang Cau Street at its beginning and Rue des Bamboes during the French colonial period, is a street located in the ancient quarter of Hanoi, specifically within Ly Thai To ward, Hoan Kiem district. The street stretches for 300 meters and is 8 meters wide, running one-way parallel to Nguyen Huu Huan Street in an east-southeast direction.

The name Hang Tre Street originated from the fact that this area was once adjacent to the Red River, making it convenient for transporting bamboo and reeds. Consequently, vendors set up stalls selling these materials, leading to the street's current name.

Geographically, Hang Tre Street starts at the intersection of Hang Mam and Hang Muoi streets, passes through Bai Thai Buoi alley, and ends at the intersection of Lo Su and Hang Voi streets. It belongs to Ly Thai To ward, Hoan Kiem district. In the early days, the street was part of Trung Thanh village, Tả Túc district, Tho Xuong county. The initial section of the street was favorable for selling areca nuts due to its proximity to the riverside. The street consists of two segments: the upper segment runs from the intersection of Hang Mam to Hang Thuong, with houses on both sides; the lower segment extends from Hang Thuong to Lo Su, with residences on the even-numbered side and a long wall of government offices on the odd-numbered side facing the riverside.

In the 1950s, Hang Tre Street was a quiet and sparsely populated street. Since 1981, the street has been one-way and runs parallel to Nguyen Huu Huan Street.

Historically, Hang Tre Street was part of one of the ten villages in Trung Thanh, Tả Túc district, Tho Xuong county. By the late 19th century, the upper part of the street was called Hang Cau because it was a hub for traders dealing in fresh and dried areca nuts transported by boats from various provinces. The name existed before the French colonial era. During the French colonial period, public buildings were constructed along the riverside (now Tran Quang Khai Street), forcing areca nut traders to move their activities to nearby Hang Be Street.

Initially, Hang Tre Street lay on a sandy strip near the Red River before the construction of embankments. Houses were sparse, and the street was mainly used as a storage area for wood and bamboo, with sawmills and stables for oxen and horses. The French named it Rue des Bambous, which translates directly to Hang Tre in Vietnamese.

After the French took control of Hanoi in 1884, they established the High Court for all of Northern and Central Vietnam. The Vietnamese court handled only domestic cases, while French-related matters were brought to the High Court in Hanoi. Initially, the court was located on Hang Tre Street, hence its nickname. In 1896, after a new courthouse was built on Carreau Street (now Ly Thuong Kiet Street), the Hang Tre courthouse became the headquarters of the Indochina Public Works Department. By the early 20th century, as Hanoi expanded, the sale of bamboo and wood ceased on Hang Tre Street. Instead, small shops and residential buildings proliferated. The court was sometimes abolished and reinstated, and the French held significant trials here, including the trial of the patriotic scholar Phan Boi Chau.

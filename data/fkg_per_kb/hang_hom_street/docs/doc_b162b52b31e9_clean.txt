Hang Hom Street is located in the Old Quarter of Hanoi, specifically within Hoan Kiem District, stretching from the end of Hangquat Street to the end of Hanggai Street. The street is 120 meters long.

Historically, Hang Hom Street was part of the ancient village of Cau Vu Thuong, which belonged to the former district of Tho Xuan, later known as Thuong My. Today, it falls under the jurisdiction of Hanggai Ward, Hoan Kiem District.

Around the mid-19th century, residents from Ha Vi Village, known for its woodworking and lacquerware crafts in Thuan Tien District, moved to this area to open shops making coffins and trunks. Initially, they produced black-lacquered boxes for clothing and documents. Over time, they began crafting modern wooden coffins similar to those seen today.

The settlers from Ha Vi Village established a communal house called Ha Vi Communal House at number 11 on the street, dedicated to their founding ancestor, Tran Lu, who taught lacquer techniques to his village. According to the "Toan Viet Thi Luc," Tran Lu was born in 1470 and died in 1540. He was a native of Binh Vong Village and passed the imperial examinations in 1502. His teachings spread the craft to neighboring villages.

During the French colonial period, Hang Hom Street was known as Rue des Caisse.

Truc Bach Lake, a serene and tranquil oasis nestled in the heart of Hanoi, is a must-visit destination for tourists seeking a peaceful retreat from the bustling city life. This natural lake, located on Thanh Nien Street in Ba Dinh District, is a charming spot that offers visitors a glimpse into the quiet beauty of the Vietnamese capital. The lake's proximity to significant landmarks such as Ho Tay, Chua Chau Long, Chua Quan Thanh, and Chua Tran Quoc makes it easily accessible and a perfect starting point for exploring the historic district.

The origins of Truc Bach Lake date back to the 18th century when it was part of Ho Tay, situated towards its southeastern corner. Initially, the area was a single body of water until local inhabitants built a small embankment to facilitate fishing. Over time, this embankment grew larger and became known as De Co Ngut, meaning "to hold firm." Eventually, the name evolved into the current street name, Cau Nguye, which is now known as Thanh Nien Street. The separation of Truc Bach Lake from Ho Tay marked the beginning of its distinct identity.

The lake's name, Truc Bach, has an intriguing history. In the 18th century, Lord Trinh Giang constructed a palace beside the lake for his leisure, naming it Truc Lam. Later, the site became a prison for concubines who had committed offenses. These women were tasked with weaving silk, a craft that gained fame for its quality and beauty. The silk's luminous appearance earned it the nickname "bamboo white" or Truc Bach in Chinese characters. Consequently, the village around the lake became known as Truc Village, specializing in silk weaving, and the lake itself took on the name Truc Bach Lake.

Today, Truc Bach Lake is a haven for relaxation and recreation. Visitors can enjoy a variety of activities that cater to different interests. One popular activity is renting a pedal boat, available from 7:00 AM to 9:00 PM. Pedal boats come in various sizes, with smaller ones costing 100,000 VND and larger ones priced at 200,000 VND. Alternatively, visitors can rent bicycles to cycle around the lake and nearby Ho Tay, immersing themselves in the serene atmosphere.

The lake's surroundings are adorned with lush greenery, including rows of willow trees lining the paths and vibrant flower beds that add splashes of color throughout the year. Walking along the lakeside provides a peaceful escape, allowing visitors to breathe in the fresh air and enjoy the tranquil ambiance. Unlike the more famous Ho Hoan Kiem, which exudes a sense of mystery and antiquity, or Ho Tay, known for its romantic charm, Truc Bach Lake offers a simple yet captivating beauty that resonates deeply with locals and tourists alike.

Two small islands, Ngoc Sa and Chau Chu, dot the surface of Truc Bach Lake, adding to its picturesque scenery. Chau Chu Island, home to the ancient Thuy Trung Tien Temple, retains the tradition of worshipping dog spirits and female deities. Meanwhile, Ngoc Sa Island, separated by water, is renowned for its bronze drum casting tradition, initiated by the legendary craftsman Nguyen Minh Khong. These islands, though small, carry significant cultural and historical importance, reflecting the rich heritage of the region.

Adjacent to the lake, a unique sculpture commemorates a pivotal moment in modern history. On October 26, 1967, during the Vietnam War, U.S. Navy pilot John McCain was shot down over Hanoi while on a bombing mission. He parachuted into Truc Bach Lake and was captured by local residents, spending over five years in captivity before being released under the Paris Peace Accords in 1973. The sculpture serves as a poignant reminder of this event and McCain's subsequent role in fostering diplomatic relations between the United States and Vietnam.

For those looking to unwind further, Truc Bach Lake offers several cafes and restaurants where one can savor a cup of coffee or indulge in local cuisine. The cafe on a boat, a unique experience, allows visitors to sip their coffee while enjoying the serene views of the lake. Other notable cafes include Highland Coffee on Thanh Nien Street, providing a cozy setting for a casual meet-up or a quiet afternoon.

In addition to these attractions, the lake's vicinity is dotted with other points of interest. Nearby Ho Tay, a larger body of water, extends the recreational opportunities, offering boating, cycling, and scenic walks. The area around Truc Bach Lake also hosts a vibrant food scene, particularly during the weekend when the pedestrian street becomes a hub of culinary delights and social gatherings.

Overall, Truc Bach Lake stands out as a hidden gem in Hanoi, blending natural beauty with cultural significance. Its serene waters, historical landmarks, and lively atmosphere make it a cherished destination for both locals and tourists, offering a slice of tranquility amidst the urban landscape.

Truc Bach Lake, a picturesque body of water nestled within the bustling city of Hanoi, holds a rich history that spans centuries. Historically, Truc Bach Lake was part of a larger water system that included Ho Tay (West Lake) and Ho Co Ngua (Ancient Horse Lake), which were once connected and formed a segment of the Red River. Over time, people constructed embankments to separate these bodies of water, creating distinct lakes. According to ancient texts like the "Long Thanh Dac Su," the waters of Ho Tay were vast, deep, and often turbulent, but the portion belonging to Truc Yen Village had warmer waters, fewer waves, and abundant mud, making it a haven for fish. In the year 1620 during the reign of King Le Than Ton, residents from villages such as Yen Phu, Yen Quang, and Truc Yen collaborated to build a dam at the head of Yen Phu village, extending towards Yen Quang village, to retain fish as a communal resource. This dam, known as Co Nguyyen, later evolved into a dike and eventually became a road, though over time, due to transliteration errors, it came to be known as Co Ngua.

The village of Truc Yen was renowned for its bamboo weaving industry, with villagers planting extensive bamboo groves around their homes to supply raw materials. During the reign of King Le Yi Ton (1735-1738), Lord Trinh Giang allocated a portion of land in Truc Yen Village for the construction of a retreat palace called Truc Tam Vien. However, this palace soon transformed into a penal colony for concubines who had committed offenses. These women were required to support themselves through labor, many of whom excelled in silk weaving, producing high-quality textiles that gained popularity. The silk they produced became known as "Truc Bai" silk, named after the village. As a result, the section of West Lake near Truc Yen Village began to be referred to as Truc Bach Lake.

The decline of the Le-Trinh dynasty coincided with the liberation of these concubines, who no longer faced confinement. In 1788, during the reign of Emperor Chieu Tong, the palace was destroyed as part of a campaign to avenge past wrongs. Despite this, the legacy of Truc Yen Village persisted, with its bamboo weaving and silk production continuing to thrive. The dike built by the villagers evolved into the broad avenue known as Cau Ngua Street, which later became Thanh Nien Road following the reunification of Vietnam after the war against French colonialism. Today, the villages of Yen Phu, Yen Quang, and Truc Yen have transformed into vibrant urban areas, while Truc Bach Lake remains a beloved landmark, celebrated for its serene beauty.

Visitors to Hanoi frequently seek out Truc Bach Lake, alongside Ho Tay, to enjoy leisurely walks along Thanh Nien Road, where the lush foliage of phoenix trees and jacaranda flowers provides a stunning backdrop. Tourists from both within and outside the country are drawn to the area to experience the tranquil ambiance of the lake, the gentle breeze, and the expansive views of the water. The lake's surroundings reflect a harmonious blend of historical significance and modern life, with locals and tourists alike contributing to its upkeep and preservation. Efforts continue to ensure that Truc Bach Lake retains its pristine condition, maintaining its status as a cherished site for relaxation and reflection amidst the dynamic cityscape of Hanoi.

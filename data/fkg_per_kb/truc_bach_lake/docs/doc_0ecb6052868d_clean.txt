Trúc Bạch Lake, also known as Hồ Trúc Bạch, is one of the numerous lakes in Hanoi, the bustling capital city of Vietnam. This body of water gained international recognition due to an event during the Vietnam War when future U.S. Senator John McCain parachuted into its waters after his aircraft was shot down.

Located northwest of Hanoi’s historic Old Quarter, Trúc Bạch Lake lies adjacent to the eastern shore of the city’s largest lake, West Lake (Hồ Tây). Once part of the Red River, West Lake was separated from Trúc Bạch Lake by the construction of a narrow dike in the 17th century. This dike, originally named Cố Ngự, was intended for fish farming, but locals misinterpreted it as Cổ Ngư. The southern shore of Trúc Bạch Lake is home to Trúc Yen Village, where residents traditionally made bamboo blinds and cultivated a specific variety of bamboo.

In the late 1950s, the major thoroughfare Thanh Niên Road was constructed between the two lakes. During the 1730s, the Trịnh Lord Trịnh Giang commissioned the construction of Trúc Lâm Palace on the lake’s shore. Initially serving as a pleasure palace, it later became a prison for royal concubines convicted of crimes. The silk produced by these prisoners became renowned as "Bamboo Village Silk."

Today, access to the lakefront is limited to Thanh Niên Road, while the rest of the perimeter is lined with residential buildings and streets. Unfortunately, Trúc Bạch Lake is among the most polluted in Hanoi. Historical landmarks in the vicinity include Quán Thánh Temple to the southwest, Châu Long Pagoda to the east, An Trì Temple dedicated to a hero from the war against the Chinese Yuan dynasty, and Cẩu Nhi temple situated on a small hill near the lake’s northern corner.

A significant monument stands on the western shore of Trúc Bạch Lake, commemorating an important event from the Vietnam War. On October 26, 1967, U.S. Navy pilot John McCain was shot down by an anti-aircraft missile while on a mission targeting a power plant near Yên Phụ. Parachuting into the lake, he was captured by local citizens and military personnel. The monument, erected in 1985, bears an inscription detailing the incident. In 2015, at McCain’s request, the monument underwent cleaning and the inscription was corrected to accurately reflect his name and service branch as Navy, removing any derogatory terms previously used.

The Softshell Turtle, also known as the Hoan Kiem Turtle, is a majestic creature that has captivated the hearts of Vietnamese people for centuries. This enormous turtle, with its relatively small and wide head, short, round snout, and very short proboscis, is a sight to behold. Its yellowish-green back with yellow spots and pale white plastron make it a striking visual presence. The turtle's carapace is hard, not soft, as its name might suggest.

According to the remains of a turtle that died in the 1960s, currently preserved in Khieng Hom Temple, this magnificent creature can grow up to 2.1 meters in length and weigh a staggering 250 kilograms. The individual caught in Ho Guom Lake in 2011, a female, measured 185 centimeters in length, with a carapace width of 99 centimeters and a tail length of 35 centimeters, weighing 169 kilograms.

The Hoan Kiem Turtle is believed to have originated from Lam Kinh, Thanh Hoa, the same hometown as King Le Loi, who released it into Ho Guom Lake. This theory is supported by the DNA sample of the Ho Guom turtle, which shows similarities with turtles from Quang Phu-Thanh Hoa. In 2010, another softshell turtle of a different species, the ba ba Nam bo, measuring 1.2 meters in length and weighing 52 kilograms, was found dead in Ho Guom Lake and was preserved as a specimen by Dr. Vu Ngoc Thanh, a former staff member of the Faculty of Biology, Hanoi University of Science.

The Hoan Kiem Turtle is deeply rooted in Vietnamese culture and history. According to legend, King Le Loi returned the Thuan Thien magic sword to the Kim Quy Turtle God, and the lake's name is based on this legend from the early Later Le dynasty. The legend of the Kim Quy Turtle God dates back even further, to the story of the Kim Quy Turtle God helping An Duong Vuong build Co Loa Citadel and gifting the king a claw to make a crossbow trigger to fight against Trieu Da's army.

The Hoan Kiem Turtle has been the subject of several Vietnamese literary works, including the story "The Turtle Tower God" by Vu Khac Khoan and "Hoan Kiem Turtle" by Nguyen Dau (Truong Man Song). Vietnamese people often refer to the giant turtle as "Grand-Father," with a respectful connotation, and this is also a symbol associated with the legend of the Magic Sword and the history of national defense, a cherished spiritual part.

However, according to Assoc. Prof. Dr. Tran Lam Bien, it is essential to recognize that the Hoan Kiem Turtle is a living creature that needs urgent conservation, not an immortal deity from legends. The turtle's display at Ngoc Son Temple in Hanoi, surrounded by soft, controlled lighting, creates a solemn and tranquil atmosphere, emphasizing its conservation and symbolic value. The image of the Hoan Kiem Turtle, frozen in a moment of movement, is both striking and evocative, inviting viewers to contemplate its majesty and significance.

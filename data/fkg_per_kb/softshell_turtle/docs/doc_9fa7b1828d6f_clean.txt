The Hoan Kiem Turtle, known in Vietnamese as Rùa Hồ Gươm, is a colossal creature that resides in Ho Guom Lake, located in the heart of Hanoi, Vietnam. This turtle, with its relatively small and wide head, short round snout, and very short proboscis, stands out due to its distinctive appearance. Its carapace, which is hard rather than soft, is adorned with a yellowish-green hue and dotted with yellow spots, contrasting sharply with its pale white plastron. The largest recorded individual, whose remains were preserved in Khieng Hom Temple, weighed an impressive 250 kilograms and measured 2.1 meters in length and 1.8 meters in width. In 2011, a female turtle was captured for medical treatment, weighing 169 kilograms with a total body length of 185 centimeters, a carapace width of 99 centimeters, and a tail length of 35 centimeters.

According to the hypothesis proposed by Associate Professor Le Tran Binh, the Hoan Kiem Turtle may have originated from Lam Kinh in Thanh Hoa province, the birthplace of King Le Loi. It is believed that King Le released this turtle into Ho Guom Lake, as there was no prior record of such a large turtle species in Thang Long, the ancient name for Hanoi. Genetic analysis conducted by Professor Le Tran Binh revealed that the DNA of the Ho Guom turtle closely matches that of turtles found in Quang Phu, Thanh Hoa.

In 2010, a different species of softshell turtle, specifically the ba ba Nam bo, was discovered dead in Ho Guom Lake. This specimen, measuring 1.2 meters in length and weighing 52 kilograms, was preserved as a specimen by Dr. Vu Ngoc Thanh, a former faculty member of the Biology Department at Hanoi University of Science.

The Hoan Kiem Turtle is deeply embedded in Vietnamese folklore and cultural symbolism. The legend of King Le Loi returning the sacred Thuong Tian sword to the Kim Quy Turtle God is central to the turtle’s mythological significance. This legend not only gives the lake its name but also ties the turtle to the broader historical narrative of Vietnam. The story of the Kim Quy Turtle God assisting King An Duong Vuong in constructing the Co Loa Citadel and providing him with a claw to create a crossbow trigger to defend against Trieu Da’s forces adds another layer of historical and cultural importance.

Throughout the 20th century, the Hoan Kiem Turtle inspired numerous literary works, including "Thần Tháp Rùa" by Vu Khac Khoan and "Rùa Hồ Gươm" by Nguyen Dau. These works reflect the enduring fascination with the turtle and its role in Vietnamese cultural heritage. The turtle is often referred to affectionately as "Cụ," a term of respect that underscores its revered status among the Vietnamese people. However, according to Associate Professor Dr. Tran Lam Bien, the turtle should not be considered an immortal deity but rather a living creature in need of urgent conservation efforts.

The Hoan Kiem Turtle specimen is displayed in a glass case at Ngoc Son Temple, a prominent landmark in Hanoi. The display space is softly lit, creating a serene atmosphere that emphasizes the turtle’s conservation and symbolic value. Inside the glass case, the turtle is positioned in a natural swimming posture, with its body extended and limbs stretched horizontally. The large, slightly flattened shell, with its deep grayish-brown hue, conveys a sense of solidity and weight.

The Softshell Turtle of Hoàn Kiếm Lake is a group of large turtles that once lived in Hoàn Kiếm Lake. The last individual died on January 19, 2016. These turtles belong to the critically endangered species, the Swinhoe's Softshell Turtle (Rafetus swinhoei). As of 2023, only three individuals of this species have been found worldwide.

Currently, the Swinhoe's Softshell Turtle is considered one of the rarest turtle species, listed as critically endangered by the World Red Book. The possibility of its extinction is very high.

Some people suggest that these turtles should not be called turtles but rather large soft-shelled turtles or soft-shelled turtles. In Chinese, they are referred to as ban miết or lai đầu ngoan, which means soft-shelled turtle. They are not classified among the four sacred creatures—Dragon, Unicorn, Tortoise, and Phoenix—because they do not fit the traditional definition of a tortoise. However, scientifically, "turtle," "soft-shelled turtle," or "soft-shelled turtle" all refer to different subfamilies within the turtle family, making it correct to call them all "turtles."

The Softshell Turtle of Hoàn Kiếm Lake is notably large, with a relatively small and wide head, short snout, and very short neck. Its back is yellowish-green with yellow spots, and its belly is pale white. Contrary to their name, their shells are actually hard rather than soft.

A specimen from the 1960s, preserved at the Khiêng Hòm Shrine, weighed 250 kg with a length of 2.1 meters and a width of 1.8 meters. Another individual captured in April 2011 for medical treatment due to injuries measured 185 cm in total length, had a carapace width of 99 cm, and a tail length of 35 cm, weighing 169 kg. According to Professor Lê Trần Bình, the Hoàn Kiếm Lake Softshell Turtle may have originated from Lam Kinh, Thanh Hoa, the birthplace of King Le Loi, and was released into Hoàn Kiếm Lake by King Le. Genetic analysis suggests that the DNA of the Hoàn Kiếm Lake Softshell Turtle matches that of the Quang Phu Softshell Turtle in Thanh Hoa.

In 2010, a different species of soft-shelled turtle, specifically a Southern Softshell Turtle, measuring 1.2 meters long and weighing 52 kg, was found dead in Hoàn Kiếm Lake. This specimen was preserved by Dr. Vũ Ngọc Thành, a former staff member of the Biology Department at Hanoi University of Science.

Another individual was caught and survived in 2008 at Dong Mo Lake, Ha Tay (now part of Ha Noi), and was believed to be of the same species as the Hoàn Kiếm Lake Softshell Turtle. It measured 90 cm in length, 70 cm in width, and weighed approximately 80-90 kg. Its edges were yellow, its head was spotted, and its shell was grayish-blue. Douglas Hendri, director of the Asian Turtle Program, conducted DNA tests on the Dong Mo turtle at the Institute of Ecology and Biological Resources, confirming that it belonged to the same species as the Hoàn Kiếm Lake Softshell Turtle, Rafetus swinhoei, as did the two individuals kept in zoos in China. In 2018, an image of another individual was captured at Xuan Khanh Lake, increasing the known number of surviving individuals to three.

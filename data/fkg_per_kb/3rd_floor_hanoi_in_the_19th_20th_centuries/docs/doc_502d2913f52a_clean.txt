The third floor of the Hanoi Museum serves as a poignant gateway to the city’s transformation from a feudal capital to a bustling modern metropolis, spanning the 19th and 20th centuries. This exhibit, titled "Hanoi in the 19th–20th Centuries," unfolds like a nostalgic journey through the city’s most significant changes, capturing the essence of a period marked by profound architectural, cultural, and social shifts.

The exhibition begins with the city under the Nguyen Dynasty, where the ancient citadel of Bac Thanh stood as a symbol of imperial power. As visitors progress through the space, they encounter the arrival of French colonial rule, which introduced a new architectural landscape characterized by European-style buildings, schools, theaters, and urban planning. The juxtaposition of these Western structures with the traditional tiled roofs of Hanoi creates a visual narrative of coexistence and contrast.

A key feature of the exhibit is the display of everyday artifacts from this transformative period, including old radios, bicycles, and ration stamps. These objects serve as tangible links to the past, evoking memories of a simpler time for older visitors. The inclusion of black-and-white photographs and ancient maps provides a visual timeline of Hanoi’s evolution, from the colonial era to the post-war reconstruction and the dynamic modernization of the city.

The exhibit also delves into the cultural life of Hanoi during these periods, highlighting the sounds of trams, the aroma of street-corner tea stalls, and the leisurely pace of life that defined the city’s identity. For young visitors, these images offer a glimpse into a very different Hanoi, while for longtime residents, they stir feelings of nostalgia for a bygone era.

The architectural styles represented range from the ornate facades of French colonial buildings to the intricate designs of traditional Vietnamese houses, each telling a story of the city’s rich heritage. The exhibit’s design, with its wood-brown and deep-gray tones and soft lighting, enhances the atmosphere of nostalgia, inviting visitors to reflect on the city’s enduring spirit despite its many transformations.

In summary, the "19th-20th Century Hanoi" exhibit at the Hanoi Museum is a testament to the city’s resilience and adaptability. It not only documents the physical changes but also captures the cultural and social dynamics that shaped Hanoi into the vibrant, multifaceted city it is today.

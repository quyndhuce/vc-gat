As you ascend to the 3rd floor of the Hanoi Museum, the "Hanoi in the 19th-20th centuries" exhibition space unfolds like a stroll through the city's most profound transformations. This was the era when Hanoi, the capital of Vietnam, transitioned from a feudal capital to a modern metropolis, with Western-style streets rising alongside ancient tiled roofs, trams clanging through the streets, and a new pace of life gradually taking shape in the heart of the old land.

The exhibition space recounts this journey through multiple layers of memory, from Hanoi during the Nguyen Dynasty, when the Bac Thanh citadel stood tall, to the French colonial period, when European architecture, schools, theaters, and urban systems began to shape the city's appearance. The space is filled with everyday artifacts, documentary photos, and ancient maps, weaving a narrative of a Hanoi that went through wars, the subsidy period, and then transformed itself dynamically in the modern era.

Old radios, bicycles, ration stamps, and familiar household items often make elderly visitors pause for a long time, as if reuniting with a part of their own youth. Young visitors are often fascinated to see a very different Hanoi in black-and-white photos, while Hanoians sometimes find here a touch of nostalgia for the sound of trams, street-corner tea stalls, or the slow pace of life that has long since receded.

The exhibition space is brought to life with numerous black-and-white archival photos, urban life artifacts, and models of old streets arranged chronologically. Wood-brown and deep gray tones combined with soft lighting evoke a sense of nostalgia. Prominent highlights include familiar items such as old bicycles, radios, ration stamps, or models of Hanoi trams. The setting often features photo panels of the Old Quarter, French architecture, and corners recreating the daily life of former urban residents.

As you wander through the exhibition, you can almost hear the sounds of trams clanging, the aroma of street food wafting through the air, and the chatter of pedestrians on the streets. The lingering feeling is often one of deep emotion for a city that has gone through so many changes yet still retains its own unique soul.

As visitors ascend to the second floor of the Hanoi Museum, they are transported back in time to the era before the establishment of Thang Long, the ancient capital of Vietnam. The "Journey to Thang Long" exhibition area unfolds like a historical narrative, guiding visitors through the sedimentary layers of the Red River Delta, where ancient humans once resided, established villages, and built the first communities. The space is a testament to the transformation of the land, which would eventually become the heart of the country.

The journey begins with archaeological artifacts, ancient maps, and settlement models, which recount the story of Cổ Loa and Đại La, the regions that once comprised the Dai La – Thang Long area. The exhibit showcases the gradual development of the land, from the earliest human settlements to the establishment of the first power centers. The legendary story of King Lý Thái Tổ's vision of a "rising dragon" from the land by the Red River is also highlighted, explaining why the area was named Thăng Long – a land of aspiration for prosperity and longevity.

The exhibit's earthy brown tones, deep yellow, and soft lighting evoke a sense of antiquity, transporting visitors to a bygone era. The most striking features are the models of the ancient citadel, maps of the Dai La – Thang Long region, and archaeological artifacts on display. Historical interpretation panels, illustrations of the ancient capital, and a slow stream of visitors observing the exhibits create a sense of continuity and immersion.

As visitors explore the space, they are struck by the significance of the ancient capital, which was the foundation of the country's power and prosperity. The exhibit's narrative is a testament to the ingenuity and resilience of the people who lived in the region, who built the first communities and laid the groundwork for the country's future. The "Journey to Thang Long" is a journey through time, a journey that reveals the rich cultural heritage and historical significance of the area, which continues to captivate visitors from around the world.

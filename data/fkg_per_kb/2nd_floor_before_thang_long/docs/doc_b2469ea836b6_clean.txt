The second floor of the Hà Nội Museum, known as "Hành trình tới Thăng Long" or "Journey to Thăng Long," serves as a captivating portal to the distant past, inviting visitors to traverse the rich historical layers of the Red River Delta. This exhibition area unfolds like a meticulously crafted timeline, guiding one through the ancient epochs leading up to the establishment of the Thăng Long capital. The space is designed to immerse visitors in the pre-Thăng Long era, showcasing the gradual evolution of human settlements in what would eventually become the heart of Vietnam's political and cultural landscape.

The journey begins with the earliest human inhabitants of the region, who settled in areas such as Cổ Loa and Đại La, laying the groundwork for future urban development. Through a series of archaeological artifacts, ancient maps, and detailed settlement models, the exhibition illustrates the transformation of these early settlements into thriving communities. These exhibits include remnants of pottery, tools, and other relics that provide tangible evidence of the daily life and societal structures of these ancient peoples.

A significant highlight of the exhibit is the depiction of the legendary event that led to the founding of Thăng Long. According to historical lore, King Lý Thái Tổ witnessed a rising dragon from the Red River, which he interpreted as a divine sign. This vision prompted him to issue the Chiếu dời đô, or the decree to move the capital, in 1010, marking the official establishment of Thăng Long as the imperial seat. The exhibition captures this pivotal moment through vivid illustrations and historical documents, offering visitors a glimpse into the mythological origins of the city.

The architectural models of the ancient citadel, alongside detailed maps of the Dai La–Thăng Long region, further enhance the visitor’s understanding of the geographical and urban planning aspects of the area. These models not only depict the physical layout of the ancient city but also convey the strategic importance of its location at the confluence of the Red River and its tributaries. The visual cues, including earthy brown tones and deep yellow hues, combined with soft lighting, create an atmosphere that evokes a sense of antiquity and reverence for the historical significance of the site.

For students and scholars, the exhibition offers a wealth of educational material, including historical interpretation panels and illustrations of the ancient capital. These resources provide a deeper insight into the cultural and social dynamics of the period, making it a popular destination for academic research and learning. Foreign tourists, too, find the continuous narrative approach to the city’s history particularly engaging, as it presents the evolution of Hanoi as a seamless journey rather than a series of disjointed events.

As visitors leave the "Journey to Thăng Long" exhibition, they are left with a profound sense of the historical continuity and the quiet preparation that transformed a simple delta region into the vibrant and culturally rich capital of Vietnam. The experience underscores the enduring legacy of Thăng Long, now known as Hanoi, as a symbol of national pride and a testament to the resilience and adaptability of its people.

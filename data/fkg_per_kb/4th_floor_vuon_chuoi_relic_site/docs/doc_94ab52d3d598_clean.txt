In the heart of Hanoi, nestled halfway up the inverted pyramid architectural structure on Pham Hung Street, lies the 4th Floor: Vườn Chuối relic site, a tranquil oasis amidst the bustling metropolis. This enchanting space, aptly named Banana Garden, is a miniature representation of the Northern Vietnamese countryside, carefully recreated within the Hanoi Museum. The garden's humble beginnings date back to the Millennial Anniversary of Thang Long, when banana plants were chosen to be nurtured here, symbolizing the simple and loyal character of the Vietnamese people.

As visitors ascend to the 4th floor, they are greeted by the lush greenery of banana plants rising about three meters high, their broad leaves a vibrant contrast to the smooth, cylindrical trunks. The signature identifying feature is the drooping dark red banana blossom, a striking visual cue that draws the eye. The rustic patch of vegetation is enclosed by transparent glass walls and the angular concrete architecture of the exhibition corridor, creating a sense of depth and dimensionality.

The garden's cultural significance is deeply rooted in Vietnamese heritage, serving as a peaceful sanctuary where the elderly reminisce about their childhood memories and young visitors immerse themselves in the atmosphere of the folk culture exhibition. This green space is more than just a decorative miniature landscape; it is a deep spiritual imprint, a rustic quietude that gently lulls those souls yearning to reconnect with their national roots amidst the contemporary exhibition space.

As a testament to the city's rich cultural heritage, the 4th Floor: Vườn Chuối relic site is a must-visit destination for anyone seeking to experience the authentic essence of Vietnamese culture. The garden's tranquil atmosphere, combined with its historical significance and cultural relevance, makes it an integral part of the Hanoi Museum's offerings, providing a unique and enriching experience for visitors from around the world.

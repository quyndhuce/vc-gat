The Vietnam Women's Museum, a significant landmark in Hanoi, stands as a testament to the profound contributions and sacrifices of Vietnamese women throughout history. Established in 1987 and officially inaugurated in 1995, this museum is under the auspices of the Vietnam Women's Union. Situated at 36 Ly Thai To Street, now known as Ly Thuong Kiet Street, the museum is nestled in the heart of Hanoi, just a short walk from Hoan Kiem Lake, making it easily accessible for visitors.

The museum's location in the bustling city center draws thousands of tourists annually, both domestic and international, who come to explore its extensive collection of over 30,000 artifacts and documents that chronicle the lives and roles of Vietnamese women across various historical periods. The museum's exhibits are meticulously curated to provide a comprehensive understanding of the multifaceted roles women have played in shaping Vietnam's cultural and social landscape.

Upon entering the Vietnam Women's Museum, visitors are greeted by a series of permanent exhibitions spread across several floors. The second floor focuses on "Women in Family," where one can delve into the traditional roles of women as wives and mothers, along with the customs and rituals associated with marriage, childbirth, and family life. This section offers a glimpse into the intricate tapestry of Vietnamese family structures and the pivotal role women play within them.

Ascending to the third floor, the theme shifts to "Women in History." Here, the museum showcases the indomitable spirit and resilience of Vietnamese women through various historical periods. From their involvement in resistance movements to their contributions in contemporary society, the exhibits highlight the strength and determination of women in building and defending their nation. The displays include artifacts, photographs, and multimedia presentations that bring to life the stories of women who have been instrumental in Vietnam's development.

On the fourth floor, visitors encounter the "Fashion" exhibit, which explores the diverse traditional attire and accessories worn by women from the 54 ethnic groups in Vietnam. This section provides insight into the unique cultural identities and regional variations in fashion, reflecting the rich heritage of Vietnam's many communities. Traditional costumes, jewelry, and makeup tools are displayed alongside detailed descriptions, allowing visitors to appreciate the craftsmanship and cultural significance embedded in each piece.

In addition to these permanent exhibits, the Vietnam Women's Museum hosts a variety of special exhibitions that delve into specific themes related to women's roles and achievements. These include exhibitions on religious practices such as the "Maiden Worship Belief," which explores the spiritual traditions centered around female deities, and contemporary issues like entrepreneurship and social equality. Each exhibition is designed to offer a deeper understanding of the evolving roles of women in Vietnamese society.

The museum also features an online exhibition space, providing virtual access to its collections and educational resources. This digital platform extends the reach of the museum beyond its physical walls, enabling a broader audience to engage with the rich cultural heritage it preserves.

Throughout the year, the museum organizes events and activities that complement its exhibitions, enhancing the visitor experience and fostering a deeper appreciation for the cultural significance of its collections. Whether exploring the traditional roles of women in family life, their contributions to national history, or the vibrant world of Vietnamese fashion, the Vietnam Women's Museum offers a profound and enriching journey into the heart of Vietnamese culture and society.

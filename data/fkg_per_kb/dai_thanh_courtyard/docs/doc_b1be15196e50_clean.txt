The Dai Thanh courtyard is a significant architectural ensemble that stands as a testament to the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam, particularly its reverence for Confucianism. This sacred space is meticulously designed with a layered spatial arrangement that begins with the central courtyard known as the Dai Bai Square. The layout of the Dai Thanh courtyard is intricately planned, with two parallel rows of buildings flanking the square on the eastern and western sides. These structures harmonize perfectly with the main hall, the Bai Duong, which faces the Dai Bai Square, and the grand temple, the Dai Thanh Temple, located directly behind it.

The Bai Duong, or the Hall of Worship, is a majestic structure that once served as the venue for solemn ceremonies dedicated to the veneration of Confucius. Its imposing presence is accentuated by a series of steps made of stone leading up to the entrance, and the floor is paved with intricate patterns of tiles. The hall itself is divided into nine chambers, each supported by sturdy columns crafted from fragrant limewood. The roof is adorned with glazed ceramic tiles arranged in a distinctive curved shape, known as the "hat-shaped" roof, and atop this elegant structure, there are ornamental carvings depicting dragons paying homage to the sun and moon, symbolizing the reverence and respect accorded to the teachings of Confucius.

On either side of the Bai Duong's main facade and rear, there are wooden lattice windows with delicate carvings, showcasing the exquisite craftsmanship of the time. Below these windows, one can find beautifully carved wooden reliefs from the early Le dynasty, featuring mythical dragons intertwined with clouds and swords, adding a touch of mystique and historical depth to the hall’s interior.

Adjacent to the Bai Duong, the Dai Thanh Temple runs parallel to it, connected by a small pavilion. This temple is also composed of nine chambers, enclosed by walls on three sides, creating a serene and secluded atmosphere. The central seven chambers are enclosed by solid doors, while the two end chambers have fixed lattice windows, reflecting the architectural style of the late Le dynasty. The columns supporting the temple's roof are painted in vibrant red and gold, a traditional method that not only enhances the visual appeal but also imbues the structure with a sense of grandeur and sanctity.

Inside the Dai Thanh Temple, visitors can pay their respects to the revered figures of Confucius, his four principal disciples, and ten other prominent scholars. The central chamber houses a statue of Confucius, facing southward, with an altar behind it where memorial tablets are placed. The temple's roof, like that of the Bai Duong, is covered with ceramic tiles and features dragon motifs on the roof ridge, symbolizing the enduring influence of Confucian thought.

Visiting the Dai Thanh courtyard offers a profound experience, immersing one in the rich tapestry of Vietnamese culture and history. The meticulous attention to detail in the architecture, the symbolic significance of every element, and the tranquil ambiance of the courtyard create a setting that is both educational and spiritually uplifting. As visitors walk through the halls and temples, they are transported back in time, gaining insight into the deep-rooted traditions and values that have shaped the cultural landscape of Vietnam.

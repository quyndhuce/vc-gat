In the heart of Hanoi's historic Temple of Literature complex, nestled within the serene surroundings of the Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám, lies the majestic Dai Thanh Courtyard, also known as Sân Đại Thành. This expansive, culturally profound yard is a solemn space where scholars once gathered to pay homage to Confucius and prominent figures of Vietnamese education. The courtyard's imposing architectural block, adorned with three vaulted archways, rises majestically, its red roof tiles and weathered yellow walls blending seamlessly with the ancient moss-covered sections.

The unique identifying feature of this ancient gate is the grand inscription, a glittering and majestic ceramic and porcelain mosaic, which adorns the main gateway arch. This magnificent structure stands proudly under the lush canopy of ancient trees, its grandeur amplified by the serene surroundings of West Lake, whose rippling surface stretches out before it. The courtyard's architectural style is a testament to traditional Vietnamese court architecture, with its intricate carvings, ornate details, and harmonious balance of form and function.

As a symbol of the Confucian imperial tradition of the Le dynasty, the Dai Thanh Courtyard is a revered space that reflects the cultural and educational values of Vietnam's rich heritage. Visitors can stroll through the courtyard, taking in the tranquil atmosphere and admiring the intricate carvings and ornate details that adorn the ancient structures. The courtyard is usually open from 8:00 to 17:00, and visitors can explore this cultural gem for a fee of approximately 30,000 VND.

As a significant cultural and historical landmark, the Dai Thanh Courtyard is an essential part of Hanoi's cultural fabric, offering a glimpse into the country's rich educational and cultural heritage.

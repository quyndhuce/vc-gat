Hang Vai Street is located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi, stretching for 240 meters from Thuoc Bac Street at its junction with Lan Ong Street to Phung Hung Street, opposite the elevated railway crossing. Historically, Hang Vai Street was part of Dong Thanh village, Tan Khai ward, Ty Tuc district, in the old Tho Xuan county. Today, it belongs to Hang Bo ward, Hoan Kiem district. During the French colonial period, it was known as rue des Étoffes, also called Hang Vai Nau (Brown Cloth Street).

In the past, Hang Vai Street was divided into two sections: the eastern segment was named Hang Vai, while the western part was called Hang Cuoc because residents there produced hoe blades and plow blades. Before 1945, these two streets were unified and collectively referred to as Hang Vai Street. The predominant fabrics sold here were small-width dyed brown and dark-colored cloths. To distinguish it from another Hang Vai Street near Dong Thanh market (now the middle section of Thuoc Bac Street), which sells undyed small-width cloths, this street was specifically called Hang Vai Tham (Dark Cloth Street).

Currently, Hang Vai Street primarily sells bamboo products and related items such as bamboo poles, fishing rods, hooks, and pipes. This specialization stems from artisans from Buoi Village in Cuoc Bac, northern Vietnam, who migrated to settle here at the beginning of the 1900s. These artisans originally included brothers like Nguyen Trong Van, whose descendants continue the tradition today, such as Nguyen Trong Son, Nguyen Trong Trung, and Nguyen Trong Kha. As a result, the street was formerly known as Hang Cuoc Street.

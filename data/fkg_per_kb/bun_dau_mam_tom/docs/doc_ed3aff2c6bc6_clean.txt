Bun dau mam tom, also known as bun dau met or simply bun dau, is a simple and rustic dish in Northern Vietnamese cuisine, originating from Hanoi. It is often enjoyed as a light meal or snack, and sometimes serves as the main dinner course. The primary ingredients include fresh rice vermicelli, fried tofu, sticky rice cakes, boiled pork, fried spring rolls, sausage, and shrimp paste mixed with sugar, tamarind (or lime), and chili. For those who cannot tolerate shrimp paste, it can be substituted with fish sauce or another dipping sauce. Accompanying the dish are various herbs such as perilla, hyptis, basil, lettuce, pickled cucumber, and pickled chili peppers. In some regions, particularly in southern provinces, rice paper is also included.

Like other traditional dishes, bun dau mam tom is popular among the general public due to its affordability, making it a favorite among the working class. This popularity contributes significantly to the income of those selling the dish. The dish's simplicity and accessibility have made it a beloved part of Vietnamese culinary culture.

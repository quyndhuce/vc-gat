Thanh Nien Road is a street located between West Lake and Truc Bach Lake, or more specifically, between Tay Ho District and Ba Dinh District in Hanoi. The road stretches for only 1 kilometer, making it the shortest street in Hanoi. It runs from Yen Phu Slope to the intersection of Quan Thanh and Thuy Khue streets, approximately 3 kilometers west-northwest of Hoan Kiem Lake. Thanh Nien Road passes through the neighborhoods of Yen Phu and Thuy Khue in Tay Ho District, as well as Quan Thanh and Truc Bach in Ba Dinh District.

The history of Thanh Nien Road dates back to the Ly Dynasty when it was originally known as Co Ngut Slope. This ancient slope had a legend associated with a dog that founded the Thang Long Citadel. During the French colonial period, people often misread the Vietnamese name without tones, leading to the street being called Co Ngư or Rue Kô-Ngü by the French. Around 1959-1960, the road was expanded by the youth of Hanoi, and President Ho Chi Minh renamed it Thanh Nien Road.

West Lake and Truc Bach Lake were once connected. In the 17th century, local fishermen built a small embankment from Yen Hoa (Yen Phu) to Yen Ninh to avoid going around, which eventually became the embankment known as Co Ngut, meaning to keep it stable. At this corner of the lake, there was once a palace for Lord Trinh Doanh, which later became a place to house concubines who were either guilty or elderly. They planted mulberry trees and raised silkworms, producing fine white silk known as Truc Village Silk, giving rise to the name Truc Bạch. During the Le-Trinh period, Truc Bach Lake was used to grow lotus flowers for the lords. Five villages specializing in bronze casting moved to this area, creating a scene where the smoke from their furnaces mingled with the fragrance of lotus flowers, as described in a famous poem by Nguyen Huy Luong in 1801.

There are several historical sites along Thanh Nien Road, including the Tran Quoc Temple, said to have been built by Emperor Ly Nam De to store Buddhist scriptures; the Cau Nhi Shrine, established by Emperor Ly Thai To when founding the Thang Long Citadel; the Quan Thanh Shrine at the intersection of Quan Thanh and Thanh Nien Streets; the Chau Long Pagoda at number 112 Tran Vu Street; the Than Quang Pagoda at number 41 Ngua Xa Street; and the Yen Phu Communal House on Yen Hoa Street in Yen Phu Ward.

Public transportation options include Bus Route 50 and E09, providing convenient access to the area.

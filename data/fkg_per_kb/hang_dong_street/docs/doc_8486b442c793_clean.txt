Hang Dong Street, also known as 行銅, is located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi. During the French colonial period at the end of the 19th century, Hang Dong Street and Bat Su Street were part of a single street named Rue des Tasses, or Street of Bowls. Since ancient times, the residents of Hang Dong Street have specialized in producing and trading goods made from copper materials.

Currently, Hang Dong Street is situated in Hang Bo ward, Hoan Kiem district, Hanoi city. The street stretches approximately 0.13 kilometers in an east-northeast to west-southwest direction. At its eastern-northeastern end, it intersects with Hang Ruoi and Hang Ma streets, while at its western-southwestern end, it meets Hang Vai, Bat Su, and Lan Ong streets. It is positioned about 0.7 kilometers southwest of Hoan Kiem Lake and 0.3 kilometers northwest of Dong Xuan Market.

Historically, Hang Dong Street was part of the village of Yen Phu, which belonged to the Thien Tuc district of Tho Xuong county.

The traditional craft of Hang Dong Street involves manufacturing and selling copper utensils such as trays, pots, pans, and woks. From these common items, craftsmen have developed antique replicas, decorative spheres, and artistic bronze dishes. Additionally, the residents trade in cast bronze items like cranes, incense burners, and flower vases, which they purchase from villages such as He Nom in Hung Yen province and Ngua Xa in Hanoi.

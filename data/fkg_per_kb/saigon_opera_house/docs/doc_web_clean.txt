Source: Saigon Opera House Architectural Records & Vietnam Tourism Heritage
The Saigon Opera House, officially the Municipal Theatre of Ho Chi Minh City (Nha hat Thanh pho Ho Chi Minh), is located on Lam Son Square along Dong Khoi Street in District 1. Following a 1895 architectural competition, construction took place from 1898 and the theatre opened on January 1, 1900. French architects Felix Olivier, Ernest Guichard, and Eugene Ferret designed the monument in the ornate Flamboyant Beaux-Arts French Third Republic style, inspired by the Petit Palais in Paris. Notable engineering details include a raised floor roughly two meters above street level and a two-layer soundproof door system to insulate the auditorium from city noise. Its ivory-white stone facade displays a grand arched central portal framed by Corinthian pilasters, sculpted relief balustrades, caryatid statues supporting the triangular pediment, winged female angel figures, and ornate metal roof crests originally crafted in France. After serving as the seat of the National Assembly of South Vietnam from 1956 to 1967, the building returned to performing arts in 1975, underwent major architectural restoration between 1995 and 1998, and was designated a National Architectural and Artistic Relic in 2012. Today it hosts classical concerts, ballet, opera, and contemporary Vietnamese bamboo circus productions such as A O Show.

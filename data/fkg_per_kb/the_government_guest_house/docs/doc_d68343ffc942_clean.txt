The Government Guest House, formerly known as the Bac Bo Phu or the Government Guest House, is a five-story building located at No. 2 Le Thach Street, Hoan Kiem District, Hanoi City. It currently serves as the primary workplace for the President, Vice President, the President's Office, and the National Defense and Security Council. This historic site was once the headquarters of the Northern region during the colonial period and remains an important historical landmark in Vietnam.

Historically, during the French colonial era, the building served as the residence of the Governor-General of Tonkin. After Japan overthrew the French colonial government on March 9, 1945, it became the office of the Inspector General of Tonkin. During the August Revolution in 1945, forces of the Viet Minh and the people of Hanoi stormed and seized control of the building. Following the success of the revolution, President Ho Chi Minh and the provisional government of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam moved to work here until the beginning of the national resistance war. At this time, the building was renamed Bac Bo Phu.

At the outset of the national resistance war, on December 20, 1946, a fierce battle erupted between a platoon of the National Guard protecting Bac Bo Phu and French troops supported by tanks. This was one of the most intense and prolonged battles in the early stages of the Indochina War. Despite six failed attacks resulting in 122 French casualties, four tanks and armoured vehicles destroyed, and three transport trucks damaged, the French eventually captured the building. The National Guard suffered 45 fatalities.

Following the end of the Indochina War in 1954, Bac Bo Phu underwent renovations and was transformed into the Government Guest House. In 2005, it was designated as a revolutionary historical relic. On August 19, 2025, Bac Bo Phu was officially handed over to become the official workplace of the President's Office.

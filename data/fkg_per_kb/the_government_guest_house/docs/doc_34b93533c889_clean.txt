Situated near Hoàn Kiếm Lake, at the heart of Hanoi, The Government Guest House at 12 Ngô Quyền Street is one of the significant historical landmarks of the city. Constructed in 1918, the building stands on the site of the ancient Báo Ân Temple, embodying a classic French colonial architectural style. This structure is a testament to the grandeur of colonial architecture, featuring a harmonious balance in its layout and facade, adorned with intricate European decorative elements. Throughout its history, the guest house has played a pivotal role in the anti-colonial struggle against both French and American forces, serving as a symbol of national resistance. It was here that President Ho Chi Minh worked during the critical year of 1945, when it was known as the Bac Bo Phu (Northern Executive Office).

The Government Guest House occupies a strategic location, flanked by two streets: Ngô Quyền and Lê Thạch. On the Ngô Quyền side lies the Diên Hồng Garden, also known as the Con Cóc Garden, while the Lê Thạch side is home to Lý Thái Tổ Park. These green spaces contribute to a serene atmosphere, complementing the historic ambiance of the surrounding area, which is dotted with other colonial-era buildings. This setting makes it an ideal venue for hosting important events and gatherings.

The guest house's central position facilitates easy access to numerous iconic sites in Hanoi. Just a short walk away are attractions such as Hoàn Kiếm Lake, the Post Office on the lake shore, and the bustling Old Quarter. Nearby are popular destinations like Hang Ngang and Hang Dao Night Markets, the Lý Thái Tổ Monument, and the famous Trang Tien Ice Cream Shop. Additionally, cultural venues such as the Thang Long Theatre and the Hanoi Opera House are within close proximity, as well as administrative offices including the City People's Committee, the Hanoi Party Committee, and the State Bank of Vietnam.

Today, The Government Guest House continues to serve as a prestigious venue for diplomatic functions organized by the government, political parties, and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Yearly, it welcomes hundreds of international delegations visiting Vietnam, making it a microcosm of the country’s diplomatic engagements. The guest house hosts various events ranging from formal receptions and negotiations to more casual gatherings like banquets, weddings, birthday celebrations, class reunions, coffee meetings, breakfasts, and even overnight stays for guests.

In addition to its official duties, The Government Guest House regularly organizes smaller events, reflecting its versatility and importance in the local community. Its historical significance and elegant design make it a focal point for cultural activities, further enriching the city's vibrant social life. The guest house's role extends beyond mere hospitality; it serves as a living museum, preserving the legacy of colonial architecture and the resilience of the Vietnamese people through their struggles for independence.

The Government Guest House remains a vital part of Hanoi's urban landscape, blending seamlessly into the city's rich tapestry of history and modernity. Its presence not only underscores the architectural heritage of the region but also embodies the spirit of unity and progress that defines the nation.

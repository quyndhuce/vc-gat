Nestled along the picturesque shores of Hoan Kiem Lake in the heart of Hanoi, the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre stands as a testament to Vietnam’s rich cultural heritage. Since its establishment in 1969, this revered institution has been a beacon of artistic excellence, drawing visitors from around the globe to witness the enchanting performances that blend traditional puppetry with the vibrant energy of contemporary urban life. The theatre, located on Dinh Tien Hoang Street, is named after the ancient capital of Thang Long, a moniker that echoes the grandeur of the Ly Dynasty when King Ly Thai To relocated the capital here in the early 11th century.

One of the most captivating performances staged at the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre is "Le Loi Du Thuyen," a mesmerizing spectacle that recounts the legendary tale of King Le Thai To returning the sacred sword to the Golden Turtle. This performance not only captivates audiences with its intricate puppetry but also serves as a profound reminder of Vietnam’s historical aspirations for peace and unity. The story unfolds on the shimmering waters of Hoan Kiem Lake, a site steeped in myth and legend, where the Golden Turtle is said to have emerged to reclaim the sword that once belonged to King Le Thai To.

The performance begins with the resounding beats of festive drums and the melodic tones of traditional horns, setting the stage for a heroic narrative. A majestic dragon boat, adorned with vibrant vermilion and bright yellow lacquer, glides gracefully across the water, carrying the figure of King Le Thai To and his diminutive army of soldiers. The vivid colors and meticulous craftsmanship of the puppets are a testament to the skill and dedication of the puppeteers, who manipulate the figures with unparalleled finesse beneath the water’s surface.

As the performance reaches its climax, the giant Golden Turtle emerges from the depths, its luminous eyes fixed on the gleaming sword held aloft by the king. This iconic moment, captured against the serene backdrop of the lake, symbolizes the eternal bond between the Vietnamese people and their revered monarchs. The act of returning the sword to the turtle is more than a simple gesture; it represents the deep-seated values of honor, duty, and reverence for tradition that continue to resonate within Vietnamese society.

The Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre, with its storied past and ongoing commitment to preserving and celebrating Vietnam’s cultural legacy, stands as a vital link between the country’s ancient traditions and its modern identity. For both international tourists and local residents, the experience of watching "Le Loi Du Thuyen" is a journey through time, offering a glimpse into the historical narratives that have shaped Vietnam’s national identity. The theatre’s enduring popularity underscores the universal appeal of storytelling through the medium of water puppetry, a unique art form that continues to captivate audiences with its blend of historical significance and artistic innovation.

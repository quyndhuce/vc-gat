Nestled along the gentle banks of the Nhuong River, the village of Vạn Phúc in Ha Dong District, Hanoi, stands as a testament to Vietnam's rich tradition of silk weaving, a craft that has flourished for over a thousand years. This ancient village, known for its intricate silk production, is not only a cradle of textile art but also a vibrant cultural space that encapsulates the essence of Vietnamese craftsmanship and heritage.

The history of Vạn Phúc weavers dates back to a time when the village was a bustling center of silk production, renowned for its high-quality fabrics and skilled artisans. Over the centuries, the village has weathered numerous historical changes, from periods of prosperity to times of decline, yet it has steadfastly preserved its core traditions through the dedication of its people and the meticulous care taken in every step of the weaving process. The village's resilience is evident in the continued practice of silk weaving, which remains a vital part of local life and identity.

Today, amidst the rapid advancements in technology, the legacy of Vạn Phúc is being revitalized through innovative digital means. A notable initiative by YooLife involves the creation of a virtual reality (VR360) experience that aims to preserve and promote the traditional crafts of the village. This project offers visitors a unique opportunity to explore the heart of Vạn Phúc's silk industry, from the bustling streets to the intricate workshops where artisans continue their age-old practices.

The journey begins along the main thoroughfare, Ô Vạn Phuc Street, which serves as the gateway to the village's artisanal heart. In the digital realm, this street comes alive with vibrant decorations and traditional storefronts, each showcasing a variety of handcrafted products. From silk scarves and embroidered bags to intricately woven ao dai (Vietnamese traditional dresses), these items reflect the skill and artistic sensibilities of the village's craftsmen. The street is lined with shops that display a range of silk products, each piece a testament to the meticulous labor and artistic vision of the weavers.

One of the highlights of the VR experience is the workshop of Master Trieu Van Mao, a key figure in reviving the ancient Vân silk technique. The virtual representation of his workshop provides an intimate look into the daily activities of the artisans, capturing the rhythmic movements of the looms and the focused expressions of the weavers as they work tirelessly. These scenes not only illustrate the technical aspects of silk weaving but also convey the deep-seated passion and commitment of the artisans who have dedicated their lives to preserving this craft.

The VR360 experience delves deeply into the entire silk production process, from the initial stages of silk reeling to the final weaving of the fabric. Each step, from boiling cocoons to spinning threads on spindles, setting up the loom, and weaving the cloth, is meticulously recreated, offering viewers a comprehensive understanding of the intricate techniques involved. The use of 3D technology allows for detailed exploration of traditional tools such as silk reeling pots, spinning wheels, loom frames, and weaving looms, providing a tactile and visual appreciation of the craftsmanship required in each stage of production.

Beyond the technical aspects, the VR experience also immerses visitors in the cultural and spiritual dimensions of the village's silk-making tradition. Historical sites like the Vạn Phuc communal house, the Vạn Phuc temple, and the shrine dedicated to the village's founding ancestors are faithfully reproduced, reflecting the reverence and respect that villagers hold for their forebears who established and maintained the craft over generations. These representations serve not only to educate but also to honor the cultural significance of the village's heritage.

By integrating modern technology with traditional practices, the VR360 project bridges the gap between past and present, ensuring that the rich tapestry of Vạn Phuc's silk weaving legacy continues to thrive. The digital preservation of the village's crafts and stories helps to spread awareness and appreciation for the enduring value of handmade textiles, making the cultural heritage more accessible to a wider audience. This innovative approach underscores the importance of preserving traditional skills while adapting them to contemporary contexts, thereby fostering a deeper connection between the community and its historical roots.

The Banyan Tree Planted by Hồ Chí Minh stands as a revered landmark within the southern part of Thống Nhất Park in Hanoi, a testament to the enduring legacy of the beloved leader who planted it during the first Tết Trồng Cây (New Year Tree Planting) campaign in 1960. This special event was initiated by President Hồ Chí Minh, who sought to encourage the planting of trees as a way to beautify and sustain the environment, reflecting his deep commitment to the well-being of the nation and its people.

Located near the intersection of Đại Cồ Việt Street, the tree is easily visible from afar, its sprawling canopy casting a lush green shade over a significant portion of the park. The tree's majestic presence is marked by its thick trunk and numerous secondary trunks that branch out robustly, each one strong and imposing like columns supporting a grand structure. The tree's expansive foliage not only provides a serene haven for visitors but also symbolizes the continuity of life and growth that Hồ Chí Minh envisioned for Vietnam.

The planting of this banyan tree took place during the lunar new year of 1960, when Hồ Chí Minh personally participated in the ceremony, demonstrating his dedication to environmental conservation and community engagement. On that historic day, the young Nguyễn Đại Sơn, then just nine years old, witnessed the momentous occasion alongside other residents of Hanoi. The memory of seeing Hồ Chí Minh in his simple brown áo nâu sẫm (dark brown shirt), planting the tree with his own hands, remains vivid in Sơn’s mind, imbued with a sense of joy and pride. The act of planting the tree was more than just an environmental initiative; it was a symbolic gesture that united the people and inspired a sense of collective responsibility towards nature.

Since its planting, the banyan tree has become a cherished symbol of national unity and environmental stewardship.

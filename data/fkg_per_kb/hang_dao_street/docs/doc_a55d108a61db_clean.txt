Hang Đao Street, also known as 行桃, is a historic street located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi. Stretching approximately 260 meters from north to south, it begins at the Đông Kinh Nghĩa Thục square near Hoàn Kiếm Lake and ends at the intersection with Hang Ngang Street. The western side of the street features houses with even numbers, while the eastern side has houses with odd numbers.

The name Hang Đao Street originates from its historical role as a marketplace for red-dyed fabrics. Today, the street operates as a one-way thoroughfare for vehicles and remains a significant commercial area characteristic of Hanoi. It is part of the "spine axis" of the Old Quarter, which includes Hang Đao, Hang Ngang, Hang Duong, and Đồng Xuân streets. This axis is crucial for tourism, handicrafts, and transportation in Hanoi, serving as one of the main routes from Hoàn Kiếm Lake to the Ba Đình Post Office, a national tangible heritage site in Vietnam.

Historically, Hang Đao Street dates back to the early days of Hoa Lư, where a similar market existed. During the Later Lê dynasty, the street was part of Đồng Lạc and Đại Lợi wards, located in Tiền Túc district, Thọ Xương county. The Đại Lợi ward was home to residents from Đan Loan village (Bình Giang, Hải Dương) and Đình Loan, Đông Cao villages (Bắc Ninh), who specialized in silk dyeing since the Tran dynasty, through the Hồ period, and into the Lê dynasty. The market featured silk sales on the first and sixth day of each lunar month. Before 2025, Hang Đao Street was part of the Hàng Đào ward in Hoàn Kiemer district, now belonging to the Hoàn Kiemer ward of Hanoi city.

During the French colonial era, the street was named Rue de la Soie, meaning Silk Street. At that time, tram tracks ran along the street from Hoàn Kiếm Lake to Hàng Đậu Park. These tracks no longer exist today. Around 1925, Western fabrics began to dominate, and more than half of the shops shifted to selling Western cloth, leading traditional goods to disappear gradually. Over time, the street ceased selling dyed fabrics altogether, and shop owners with substantial capital transitioned to luxury goods.

Since 2003, Hang Đao Street has hosted a night market on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays, featuring handicrafts and local specialties for tourists. This nighttime market has become a new cultural feature of the capital, attracting both locals and visitors.

In certain periods starting from 1997, the area from house number 77, 53, 29, and 12 on Hang Đao Street, along with over 15 houses on Hang Đao, Hang Ngang, Cầu Đông, and Hang Duong streets, were controlled by the Tang family. In 1997, Mrs. Đoàn Bích Hương visited and surveyed the area from Hoàn Kiếm Lake to Đồng Xuân Market, finding favorable conditions and purchasing two plots of land on Hang Đao and Cầu Đông. She then brought her relatives to engage in clothing sales, eventually owning more than 20 houses in the "Old Quarter spine" by 1998. By 2018, the Tang family retained ownership of around 16 houses, making them the largest property holders in this area.

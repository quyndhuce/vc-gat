The Pen Tower, known in Vietnamese as Tháp Bút, is a striking five-story stone tower located on the northeastern side of Hoan Kiem Lake in Hanoi, Vietnam. Constructed in the 18th year of the reign of Emperor Tự Đức, which corresponds to 1865, the tower was conceived by the renowned Confucian scholar Nguyễn Văn Siêu. Situated just outside the entrance to The Huc Bridge and adjacent to Ngoc Son Temple, the Pen Tower stands as a testament to both literary and military heritage.

The tower is built on a small mound formed by stacked rocks, measuring 12 meters in diameter and 4 meters in height. This mound represents the ancient Độc Tôn mountain, which holds significant historical importance. The Pen Tower itself rises to a height of 28 meters, with each of its five square tiers narrowing progressively towards the summit. Atop the tower is a distinctive pen-shaped tip, an inverted brush pen nib that reaches skyward, symbolizing the spirit of learning and the rich literary tradition of Vietnam. The entire structure is crafted from stone, giving it a timeless and enduring quality.

Carved into the body of the three middle tiers of the tower are the Chinese characters "Tả Thanh Thiên," which translate to "Writing on the Blue Sky." These characters not only enhance the aesthetic appeal of the tower but also underscore its cultural significance. According to Nguyễn Văn Siêu’s essay "Bút Tháp chí," inscribed directly onto the tower, the Pen Tower commemorates the military achievements of Lord Trịnh in suppressing the rebellion led by Nguyễn Danh Phương, a rebel who had once occupied the Độc Tôn mountain in Thái Nguyên province. The construction of the tower thus serves as a dual tribute, celebrating both literary and martial virtues.

The Pen Tower is part of a larger architectural complex that includes the Inkstone Pavilion, situated at the beginning of The Huc Bridge. The pavilion houses a large blue stone inkstone, carved in the shape of half a peach and supported by three toad-like creatures, symbolizing stability and longevity. This inkstone, along with the Pen Tower, forms a harmonious pair that reflects the deep cultural roots of Vietnam, where literature and artistry are revered alongside military prowess.

The surrounding area of Hoan Kiem Lake provides a serene backdrop for the Pen Tower, with calm waters reflecting the tower’s elegant silhouette. Rows of lush green trees add to the tranquil atmosphere, creating a perfect blend of nature and human-made structures. As the light shifts throughout the day, the Pen Tower transforms, radiating warmth under the sun and exuding a sense of contemplation at dusk, adding to the unique charm of Hoan Kiem Lake.

Today, the Pen Tower continues to be a focal point for tourists and locals alike, drawing visitors with its historical and cultural significance. The tower stands as a proud monument to the enduring legacy of Vietnamese scholarship and valor, embodying the nation’s rich heritage and inspiring future generations.

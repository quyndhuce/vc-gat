Nestled within the exhibition space of the Vietnam Women's Museum in Hanoi, the Mẫu worship belief area serves as a portal that transports visitors from the bustling streets of the capital city into the ancient spiritual realm of Vietnam. The space is enveloped in a warm, subdued lighting that casts a serene glow over the vibrant red and golden altars, adorned with intricate carvings and gilded details. These altars, known as ban thờ sơn son thếp vàng, are the focal points of the room, housing the revered images of Tam phủ, Tứ phủ, and the Thánh Mẫu, which exude both grandeur and intimacy.

This belief system, deeply rooted in Vietnamese culture, traces its origins back several centuries and is closely tied to the legendary figure of Mẫu Liễu Hạnh, one of the Four Immortals of Vietnamese folklore. Revered as a symbol of protection, fertility, and maternal strength, Mẫu Liễu Hạnh embodies the essence of the mother in the spiritual lives of the Vietnamese people. Her legend, along with those of other Mẫu deities, has been passed down through generations, weaving a rich tapestry of cultural heritage and spiritual devotion.

The exhibition space itself is designed to evoke a sense of reverence and tranquility, with its enclosed structure and carefully curated visual elements. Deep red curtains and antique wood furnishings contribute to the sacred ambiance, while the colorful Hầu đồng costumes displayed nearby add a vibrant touch. These costumes, intricately embroidered with shimmering patterns, are a testament to the artistic beauty and cultural significance of the Mẫu worship tradition. Surrounding these costumes are statues of saints, offerings, and the faint wisps of incense smoke, all contributing to the immersive experience of the space.

As visitors explore this area, they are often captivated by the soft strains of chầu văn music, a traditional form of Vietnamese folk music that resonates throughout the quiet space. This music, along with the visual displays, brings to life the festive atmosphere of the temples and shrines found across northern Vietnam. Many visitors find themselves pausing for extended periods, either out of curiosity for the artistic beauty or in quiet reflection, as if standing before a familiar realm of memories. The space not only narrates the story of the Mẫu worship belief system but also reflects the profound connection between Vietnamese people and their natural environment, their reverence for the mother figure, and their quest for peace and harmony in daily life.

Upon leaving the exhibition, visitors are often left with a lingering sense of ethereal calm, as if they have just stepped out of a dream imbued with the fragrant hues of incense smoke and the soothing melodies of traditional music. This unique blend of cultural heritage, spiritual devotion, and artistic expression makes the Mẫu worship religious space a significant cultural landmark within the Vietnam Women's Museum, offering a profound glimpse into the heart of Vietnamese spirituality and cultural identity.

Hanoi cuisine reflects northern Vietnamese ingredients, trading traditions, household practices and urban street-food culture.

Hanoi cuisine includes pho, bun cha, cha ca, banh cuon, and other dishes.
Pho consists of rice noodles served in broth.
Beef pho uses beef as a principal ingredient.
Chicken pho uses chicken as a principal ingredient.
Bun cha combines grilled pork with rice vermicelli and dipping sauce.
Cha ca is a fish dish associated with Hanoi's culinary history.
Banh cuon consists of thin steamed rice rolls.
Egg coffee combines coffee with a whipped egg-based topping.
Street food vendors prepare food in small kitchens and roadside stalls.
Restaurants often arrange low tables and stools for diners.
Fresh herbs accompany many Vietnamese noodle dishes.
Fish sauce, lime, chili, and garlic are common condiments.
Traditional food preparation involves steaming, grilling, simmering, and stir-frying.
Hanoi food culture includes both market-based vendors and established restaurants.
Food traditions are associated with particular streets and neighborhoods.

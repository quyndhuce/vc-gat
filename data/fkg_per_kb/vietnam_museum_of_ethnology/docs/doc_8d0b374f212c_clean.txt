The Vietnam Museum of Ethnology stands as a monumental institution located at Nguyen Van Huyen Street in Quan Hoa, Cau Giay District, approximately 7 kilometers from the bustling heart of Hanoi. This expansive museum covers a vast area of 4.5 hectares, serving not only as a repository for the cultural heritage of Vietnam's 54 ethnic groups but also as a vibrant recreational destination that attracts a multitude of visitors. The museum's mission is to preserve and showcase the diverse cultural tapestry of Vietnam through its extensive collection of artifacts, interactive exhibits, and educational programs.

The museum's architecture is a testament to its cultural significance, featuring three main areas: the Drum Tower, the Kite-shaped Building, and the Architectural Garden. Each section is meticulously designed to reflect the unique characteristics of different ethnic groups and their traditions. The Drum Tower, named after its iconic shape, houses a vast array of exhibits that delve into the cultural practices, customs, attire, tools, and daily life of the 54 ethnic groups across Vietnam. The displays are thoughtfully arranged to provide visitors with a multi-sensory experience, enhancing their understanding of the rich history, traditions, and cultural nuances of each community. Interactive elements and modern technology are integrated throughout the exhibits, making the learning process engaging and immersive.

Adjacent to the Drum Tower is the Kite-shaped Building, a four-story structure that symbolizes the unity and harmony among various cultures. Its design is inspired by the kite, a traditional motif found in Vietnamese culture and throughout Southeast Asia. This building serves as a bridge connecting Vietnam with other nations, hosting exhibitions of cultural artifacts from ethnic groups in Southeast Asia and beyond. It also accommodates educational spaces for children, conference rooms, a cinema, and multimedia rooms, enriching the visitor experience with a variety of activities and presentations.

The Architectural Garden is perhaps the most beloved part of the museum, offering a serene and picturesque setting where traditional dwellings from various ethnic communities are preserved. These structures, ranging from Cham houses to Dai and Hmong stilt houses, are carefully reconstructed to reflect the authentic living conditions and cultural practices of their respective communities. Visitors can wander through these homes, each one a window into the past, gaining insight into the daily lives, rituals, and spiritual beliefs of the people who once inhabited them. During festive seasons, the houses are adorned with colorful decorations, and traditional festivals are reenacted, providing a vivid glimpse into the cultural celebrations of Vietnam’s ethnic minorities.

One of the highlights of the museum is the Discovery Room, specifically designed for children to explore and learn about the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam. Through interactive screens, sound systems, and hands-on activities, young visitors can engage with traditional games, music, crafts, and performances from various ethnic groups. This space encourages curiosity and creativity, fostering a deeper appreciation for the diverse cultural landscape of Vietnam.

Another unique feature of the museum is its cultural performance stage, which hosts a wide range of events showcasing traditional arts, crafts, and culinary practices. Visitors have the opportunity to witness live demonstrations of traditional crafts such as pottery and weaving, as well as taste regional dishes prepared by local artisans. These performances serve as a dynamic platform for preserving and promoting cultural traditions, bridging the gap between the past and the present.

The Vietnam Museum of Ethnology is more than just a repository of artifacts; it is a living museum that brings to life the vibrant cultural heritage of Vietnam. With its comprehensive exhibits, interactive programs, and beautiful outdoor spaces, it offers a profound and enriching experience for visitors of all ages, making it an essential destination for anyone interested in exploring the rich cultural diversity of Vietnam.

The Vietnam Museum of Ethnology, an organization under the Vietnam Academy of Social Sciences, is dedicated to scientific research, collecting, inventorying, preserving, restoring objects and materials related to ethnic groups, organizing exhibitions, performances, and other activities to introduce, disseminate, and educate about the historical and cultural values of ethnic groups both within and outside the country. It also provides research materials on ethnic groups for various sectors and trains researchers, professionals, and managers in ethnographic museum studies. The museum is located at Nguyen Van Huyen Street, Nghia Do Ward, Hanoi City, adjacent to Nguyen Khanh Toan Street and facing Nghia Do Park.

Designed by architect Ha Duc Linh, who is Tay ethnic, the museum's interior was designed by French architect Veronique Dollfus. The museum comprises three main exhibition areas:

The Dong Truong (Bronze Drum Hall) area features permanent exhibitions spread across two floors, introducing the 54 ethnic groups in Vietnam in a very logical manner. On the first floor, visitors can learn about the 54 ethnic groups through images and their regions of residence. They then delve into details about specific ethnic groups such as the Viet, Muong, Tay, Thai, Hmong, Dao, and others. There are also two temporary exhibition spaces that are regularly updated according to different themes. For example, in 2006, there was an exhibition titled "Life in Hanoi during the Planned Economy Era (1975-1986)," while in 2013, it featured a glimpse into the life of students studying away from home in major cities. In 2014 and 2015, the museum showcased photographs of the lives and people of the Central Highlands ethnic minorities taken by French photographer Jean-Marie Duchange in the 1950s. The second floor of the Dong Truong building offers visitors a detailed look at minority ethnic groups in Vietnam, arranged scientifically along the country’s geographical layout, starting from the north, moving through central Vietnam and the Central Highlands, and ending in the south.

The outdoor exhibition area includes a green garden with 11 traditional architectural structures representing various ethnic groups, including Cham, Dao, H'Mong, Ha Nhi, Bana, Ede, Viet, Khmer, Tay, Giarai, and Co Tu houses. Additionally, there is a medicinal herb garden, a dragon boat, and water puppetry performances from different regions.

The Southeast Asia Exhibition Area, designed by architects Nguyen Manh Thu and Doan The Trung, began construction in 2008 and was inaugurated on November 30, 2013, after six years of construction. Covering approximately 500 hectares, this area helps visitors understand more about countries in the Southeast Asian region through displayed artifacts. In December 2014, the museum opened an exhibition on Indonesian stained glass paintings. On October 24, 2015, the museum inaugurated two permanent exhibitions titled "A Glance at Asia" and "Around the World" on the second floor of the Southeast Asia building. These new exhibitions continue to attract visitors interested in learning about civilizations in the region and around the world.

In addition to these areas, the museum has administrative zones for research facilities, libraries, and object storage systems.

The Dai Trung gate stands as a monumental entrance to the second zone of the Van Mieu complex, marking a significant transition from the initial green space known as the Nhac Dao area. This area is characterized by lush greenery and tranquil ponds, creating a serene atmosphere that prepares visitors for the deeper cultural and educational journey ahead. The pathway leading to the Dai Trung gate is paved with red bricks, guiding visitors through a landscape adorned with trees and grass, flanked by two small ponds. This path not only serves as a physical route but also symbolizes a spiritual passage into the heart of the educational and cultural heritage of Vietnam.

Built during the Later Le dynasty (15th to 17th centuries), the Dai Trung gate exemplifies the architectural style of this period. Constructed from wood, it consists of three main sections supported by eight wooden columns, each resting on stone bases rather than being embedded into the ground. This unique foundation method relies on the weight of the roof to stabilize the structure, ensuring its longevity and stability over time. The columns are cylindrical and evenly spaced, contributing to the overall balance and harmony of the gate.

The roof of the Dai Trung gate is covered with traditional Vietnamese roof tiles known as "ngoi muoi hai" or dragon scales, a type of tile that has been used since the Ly dynasty and continued to be employed in subsequent dynasties. The roof's design features a straight slope, which is characteristic of imperial architecture, and is adorned with intricate decorations. Two carp fish sculptures flank a decorative vase, symbolizing the aspirations of scholars who seek success and honor through diligent study. These carps, often seen in Vietnamese culture as symbols of perseverance and achievement, are crafted from ceramic pieces, reflecting the high level of craftsmanship and artistic detail found in the construction of the gate.

The Dai Trung gate is elevated on a platform made of bricks and surrounded by stone steps, adding to its grandeur and sense of importance. The flooring around the gate is laid with Bát Tràng tiles, a renowned type of Vietnamese pottery known for its quality and aesthetic appeal. On either side of the Dai Trung gate stand two smaller gates named Thanh Duc and Dat Tai, each embodying values of moral integrity and intellectual excellence. These names reflect the traditional Vietnamese belief that true success requires both virtue and talent, a principle deeply ingrained in the educational philosophy of the region.

Visitors approaching the Dai Trung gate are greeted by a blend of natural beauty and architectural splendor. The surrounding gardens and water bodies provide a peaceful backdrop, while the gate itself stands as a testament to the rich history and cultural significance of the site. As one passes through the Dai Trung gate, they enter a world where past traditions meet modern reverence, offering a profound experience that combines the tranquility of nature with the awe-inspiring legacy of Vietnamese architectural and educational heritage.

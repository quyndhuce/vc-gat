In the heart of the Hanoi Museum, the Main Atrium emerges as the "breathing" space of the entire building, drawing almost every gaze upon first entering the museum. This expansive, multi-story space connects the exhibition areas with an impressive open structure, giving visitors the sensation of standing at the center of a colossal architectural wonder. The atrium's inverted pyramid shape, a signature feature of the building, not only serves as a connector but also becomes an integral part of the narrative of Hanoi – a place where the past, present, and future seem to converge in a shared space.

Completed in 2010 to commemorate the 1000th Anniversary of Thang Long - Hanoi, the Main Atrium was designed to harness natural light and create a sense of continuous movement between floors. The atrium's cream-white tones, glass, and bright metal reflecting the natural light create a sense of spaciousness and modernity. The most striking feature is the square central skylight, surrounded by tiered railings characteristic of the inverted pyramid architecture. The scene often features staircases, open corridors, and light pouring down from the glass roof onto the grand lobby, creating a captivating visual experience.

As visitors linger at the railings, they are drawn to the central void, as if observing a flow of time constructed through architecture. Many of the museum's most beautiful photographs are taken here, where light, shapes, and spatial depth blend together in a captivating way. What lingers in the Main Atrium is not just the overwhelming scale, but also a very special feeling: standing in the midst of it, one feels as though they are at the very intersection of Hanoi's thousand-year story.

The Main Atrium's design is a testament to the city's rich cultural heritage, reflecting the city's history, architecture, and cultural significance. As a symbol of Hanoi's resilience and growth, the atrium has become an iconic landmark, attracting tourists and locals alike. Visitors can explore the atrium's many levels, taking in the stunning views and architectural details, and gain a deeper understanding of the city's complex history and cultural identity.

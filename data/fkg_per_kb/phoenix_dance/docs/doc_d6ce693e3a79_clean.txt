Phoenix Dance Theatre, originally known as Phoenix Dance Company, was founded in 1981 in the inner-city Harehills area of Leeds by three young men—David Hamilton, Donald Edwards, and Vilmore James—who had started dancing at Harehills Middle School. Their teacher, Nadine Senior, would later become the first principal of the Northern School of Contemporary Dance. By the summer of 1982, Phoenix had expanded its roster with two additional dancers, Merville Jones and Edward Lynch, both members of the Harehills Youth Dance Theatre. In 1985, the company relocated to its first permanent base in Chapeltown.

David Hamilton left Phoenix in 1987 to pursue a solo career, and Neville Campbell took over as Artistic Director. Under Campbell's leadership, Phoenix achieved significant milestones, including winning the Grand Prize at the International Choreographic Competition in Bagnolet in 1990 for Aletta Collins' piece "Gang of Five," and receiving a Laurence Olivier Award nomination for the Most Outstanding Achievement of the Year in Dance.

Margaret Morris joined Phoenix as Artistic Director in 1991, and during her tenure, the company became well-established on the UK touring circuit and began performing internationally, particularly in the United States. Following Morris's departure in 1997, Thea Narissa Barnes led the company until 2000. In 1998, Phoenix was commissioned by the BBC to perform with Roni Size at the Windrush Gala concert, which was broadcast nationally.

In 2002, Darshan Singh Bhuller was appointed as Artistic Director and initiated a rebranding of the company, officially renaming it Phoenix Dance Theatre. He also directed the company towards larger venues, enhancing its profile. Bhuller was succeeded by Javier de Frutos, who led Phoenix to win the "Company Prize for Outstanding Repertoire (Modern)" in the Critics' Circle National Dance Awards in 2006. That same year, Phoenix was invited to perform at the 5th International Festival of Contemporary Dance - La Biennale di Venezia, where they closed the festival with their final performance.

Sharon Watson became the seventh Artistic Director of Phoenix Dance Theatre in May 2009, having been associated with the company since 1989 when she joined as a dancer under the stage name Chantal Donaldson. She choreographed pieces such as "Shaded Limits" and "Never Still" during this period. After leaving the company in 1997, Watson returned in 2000 as Rehearsal and Tour Director, and continued her involvement through various roles, including a fellowship with the Clore Leadership Programme and directorial positions at Northern Ballet Theatre.

In 2010, Phoenix Dance Theatre moved into a new, purpose-built dance center in Leeds, alongside Northern Ballet, marking a significant milestone in the company's history. This facility, the largest outside London, houses both a national classical and a national contemporary dance company.

Phoenix Dance Theatre's current repertoire includes a variety of performances, such as "BERNSTEIN DOUBLE BILL" choreographed by Dane Hurst, "THE RITE OF SPRING" by Jeanguy Saintus, and "Windrush: Movement of the People" by Sharon Watson. These performances reflect the diverse and evolving nature of the company's artistic output, showcasing the talents of both established and emerging choreographers.

The Flag Tower of Hanoi, also known as the Cột cờ Hà Nội, stands majestically on Điện Biên Phủ Street in the Ba Đình district of Hanoi, a testament to its enduring presence since its construction in 1812 during the reign of King Gia Long of the Nguyen dynasty. This iconic structure is located on the southern part of the ancient Thang Long Imperial Citadel, specifically where the Tam Mon gate once stood during the Le dynasty. The tower marks the southernmost point of the central axis of the citadel, symbolizing the beginning of the "ngu dao," or fish path, which leads through the Duong Mon gate to the heart of the citadel, the Kinh Thien palace.

The Flag Tower of Hanoi is one of the few architectural marvels that managed to survive the extensive destruction carried out by the French colonial authorities between 1894 and 1897. Its survival is a remarkable feat, preserving its original grandeur and significance for future generations. The tower's construction is a blend of traditional Vietnamese architectural elements and sophisticated engineering techniques, showcasing the skill and craftsmanship of the time.

The tower consists of three layers of base and a main pole. Each layer is designed with a square truncated pyramid shape, gradually reducing in size as they ascend, creating a harmonious visual balance. The first layer measures 42.5 meters on each side and rises 3.1 meters high, featuring two staircases made of bricks leading upwards. The second layer, measuring 27 meters on each side and rising 3.7 meters, has four entrances adorned with inscriptions. The eastern entrance bears the words "Nghenh Huc" (welcoming the morning light), the western entrance "Hoi Quang" (reflecting light), and the southern entrance "Huoung Minh" (directed towards light), while the northern entrance remains unadorned. The third layer, measuring 12.8 meters on each side and rising 5.1 meters, provides access to the main pole via a staircase. The main pole itself is an octagonal column, tapering upwards to a height of 18.2 meters, with each side at the base measuring approximately 2 meters wide. Inside the pole, there is a spiral staircase consisting of 54 steps leading to the top. The entire structure is illuminated and ventilated through 39 fan-shaped openings.

Atop the main pole sits a circular platform, forming an octagonal pavilion 3.3 meters high, with eight windows corresponding to the eight sides of the octagon. In the center of this pavilion, there is a cylindrical pillar with a diameter of 40 centimeters, extending to the top, where the flagpole is inserted. The total height of the Flag Tower of Hanoi is 33.4 meters, and if including the flagpole, it reaches 41.4 meters. Since 1986, a large red flag with five golden stars, measuring 24 square meters, has been permanently flying from the top of the tower, serving as a powerful symbol of national pride and unity.

On October 10, 1954, the day marking the liberation of Hanoi, the first time the red flag with five golden stars flew over the Flag Tower of Hanoi, it was a momentous occasion celebrated throughout the city. The event was attended by thousands of people who gathered around the tower to witness the historic moment. At precisely 3 PM, the city's clock tower sounded a long blast, signaling the start of the flag-raising ceremony. As the national anthem played, the flag was slowly hoisted up the pole, marking the end of colonial rule and the beginning of a new era of independence and freedom for Vietnam.

Today, the Flag Tower of Hanoi stands as a prominent landmark surrounded by ancient sycamore trees and a lush plum garden. According to historical records, in 1890, a photograph taken by Louis Sadoul, a French military doctor, shows the area beneath the tower as a pond used for bathing elephants during the Nguyen dynasty. The current grove of trees had not yet been planted at that time. During the French colonial period, the tower served as an observation post for the French troops, and later, during the Vietnam War, it was used by the North Vietnamese army for surveillance purposes.

Interestingly, the interior of the Flag Tower of Hanoi maintains a cool temperature even during the hottest days of summer, much like an air-conditioned space, thanks to its unique design. The structure's ingenious layout ensures that rainwater does not seep inside, maintaining the integrity of the internal spaces. The Flag Tower of Hanoi's enduring presence and symbolic importance have made it a focal point for national celebrations and a revered site for visitors seeking to understand the rich history and cultural heritage of Vietnam.

Visitors to the Flag Tower of Hanoi can explore the intricate architecture and learn about the historical significance of the site through guided tours and exhibits. The atmosphere around the tower is imbued with a sense of reverence and pride, reflecting the deep cultural and historical importance of the structure to the people of Hanoi and Vietnam. The Flag Tower of Hanoi continues to stand as a beacon of national identity and a symbol of resilience and unity, drawing tourists and locals alike to pay homage to its storied past and enduring legacy.

The Flag Tower of Hanoi, also known as Cột cờ Hà Nội, stands majestically within the Thăng Long Imperial Citadel, a testament to the city's rich history and cultural heritage. Constructed between 1805 and 1812 under the reign of King Gia Long, this iconic structure was built simultaneously with the Hanoi Citadel, showcasing the Vauban style of architecture. The tower's imposing presence, standing 60 meters tall, is a striking visual focal point amidst the surrounding landscape.

The Flag Tower's foundation is a multi-tiered base, shaped like a truncated pyramid, with each level gradually narrowing as it rises, creating a sense of balance and stability. The dominant color of the brick walls, a warm light yellow, is accentuated by the blue sky and lush greenery, highlighting the architectural form. The main body of the tower is a slightly tapering cylindrical shaft, featuring small windows evenly distributed around it, which not only add visual detail but also provide ventilation for the interior structure.

As sunlight falls on the tower, its edges and surfaces become more defined, creating contrasts of light and shadow that enhance the depth of the entire structure. At the top of the tower, a small pavilion with surrounding railings proudly displays a red flag, which flutters continuously in the wind, creating a dynamic contrast to the stillness and solidity of the tower below. From a distance, the entire structure rises like a strong vertical axis, connecting the ground to the sky, while serving as a solemn and iconic visual symbol of the historic landscape.

The Flag Tower of Hanoi is one of the most iconic relics of the Thang Long Imperial Citadel, a UNESCO-recognized heritage landscape. As a national relic of special significance, it is deeply rooted in Hanoi's long history and patriotic spirit. Located within the Thang Long Imperial Citadel complex in Ba Dinh District, Hanoi, the Flag Tower is a must-visit destination for tourists and history enthusiasts alike, offering a glimpse into the city's rich cultural heritage and architectural legacy.

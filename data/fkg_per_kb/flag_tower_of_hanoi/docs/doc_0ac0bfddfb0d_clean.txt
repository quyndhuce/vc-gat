The Flag Tower of Hanoi, known in Vietnamese as Cột cờ Hà Nội, stands as a prominent symbol of the city and was once part of the Hanoi Citadel, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. This tower, with its height of 33.4 meters (41 meters including the flag), was constructed either in 1805 or 1812 during the Nguyen dynasty as an observation post for the Hanoi Citadel. Unlike many other structures in Hanoi, the Flag Tower of Hanoi survived the French invasion from 1896 to 1897 and continued to serve as a military post.

From 1956 until 2024, the Flag Tower of Hanoi was situated within the grounds of the Vietnam Military History Museum, formerly known as the Military Museum. It is now part of the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long, located on Dien Bien Phu Street in Ba Dinh District.

Architecturally, the Flag Tower of Hanoi consists of three tiers and a pyramid-shaped tower with a spiral staircase leading to the top. The first tier measures 42.5 meters wide and 3.1 meters high, while the second tier is 25 meters wide and 3.7 meters high, and the third tier is 12.8 meters wide and 5.1 meters high. The second tier features four doors, each adorned with inscriptions: "Nghênh Húc" (To welcome dawn's sunlight) on the eastern door, "Hồi Quang" (To reflect light) on the western door, and "Hướng Minh" (Directed to the sunlight) on the southern door. The tower is illuminated by 36 flower-shaped and 6 fan-shaped windows. Atop the tower flies the National Flag of Vietnam.

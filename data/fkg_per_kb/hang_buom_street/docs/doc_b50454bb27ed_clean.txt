Hang Buom Street, also known as Xingfan in Chinese, is located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi. When the French arrived in Tonkin in 1872, the street was named Rue des Voiles. At that time, residents lived alongside the Nhị Hà River (now known as the Red River) and the To Lich River, engaging in various occupations related to waterways. The main products included baskets, woven mats, sails, and screens made from materials like bamboo and straw. From 1954 onwards, the street has been known by its Vietnamese name, Hang Buom.

Hang Buom Street stretches approximately 0.3 kilometers in an east-west direction and is situated in the Hoan Kiem district of Hanoi. Before 2025, it belonged to the Hang Buom ward, but now it falls under the Hoan Kiem ward. The eastern end of the street intersects with Dao Duy Tu and Ma May streets, while the western end meets Hang Ngang, Hang Duong, and Lan Ong streets. It is about 400 meters north of Hoan Kiem Lake and around 300 meters south of Dong Xuan Market.

Historically, Hang Buom Street was part of Ha Kou (also called Giang Kou), which was in the Huu Tuc prefecture of Tho Xuan county, at the confluence of the To Lich River and the Red River. In the 19th century, people from Guangdong in China expanded to this area from Hang Ngang Street, gradually taking control of the entire street. The Guangdong Association established a meeting hall here. Previously, there was a Bai Ma market near Bai Ma Temple, which later merged with the Cau Dong market into Dong Xuan Market.

During the French colonial period, the street was known as Rue des Voiles. Traditionally, the products made on Hang Buom Street included various types of baskets, bags, sail frames, and woven sails using bamboo and straw. Raw materials were transported directly to the street by boats, and the finished products were shipped out by the same means. After the Chinese community took over the street, these traditional goods gradually disappeared. Instead, many restaurants run by the Chinese community opened up along the street.

Today, Hang Buom Street features numerous shops selling candies, nuts, and various beverages such as wine, beer, and soft drinks. During festivals like Tet and Mid-Autumn Festival, the street becomes bustling with activity. Additionally, there are several well-known eateries offering dishes like roast pork, rice vermicelli, and salad.

The street is home to several historical sites including Bai Ma Temple at number 76, the Guangdong Association at number 22, and Tzu Yang Shrine at number 8. There is also the Quan Di Temple at number 28.

As we step through the doors of the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre, situated on the iconic Dinh Tien Hoang Street, we find ourselves in the midst of a cultural oasis, where the modern rhythms of Guom Lake converge with the mystical world of folk art. The main lobby, a spacious rectangular block with a high, airy ceiling, is a testament to the theatre's rich history, dating back to its establishment in 1969. The warm, betel-red and golden hues emanating from the wood paneling and glossy ceramic tiles create a cozy atmosphere, reminiscent of a traditional Vietnamese village.

The focal point of the lobby is the elegant row of glass cabinets, showcasing the exquisite lacquered water puppets that have become synonymous with Vietnamese cultural heritage. The surrounding ticket counter, vibrant posters, and glass doors reflecting the bright light from the street outside add to the excitement, as international tourists and locals alike eagerly await the start of the show. The air is alive with the sounds of cultural exchange, as visitors from around the world marvel at the intricate craftsmanship and vibrant colors of the puppets, while locals eagerly anticipate the rhythmic beats of the chèo drum, signaling the start of the performance.

As we pause in this space, we are transported to a world where tradition and innovation converge, where the resilience of a centuries-old art form is palpable. The main lobby is more than just a waiting area; it is a prelude to a journey of discovery, where the stories of wet-rice fields and cultural identity come alive. The Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre's main lobby is a testament to the power of cultural exchange, where the past and present blend seamlessly, and the boundaries between tradition and innovation dissolve.

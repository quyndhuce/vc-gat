Ta Hien Street is a historic street located within the Old Quarter of Hanoi, currently part of Hoan Kiem District. During the French colonial period, it was known as Rue Géraud, but locals often referred to it as Quang Lac Alley due to the presence of the Quang Lac theater at number 8 Rue Géraud since 1916. In the name comparison document from 1946, "Ta Hien Street" corresponds to "Rue Géraud." The Quang Lac theater initially staged traditional Vietnamese operas like tuong and hat boi, later transitioning to cải lương (reformed opera) by the late 1920s. Surrounding the theater were various eateries and refreshment stalls catering to theatergoers and night-time strollers.

In the late 19th century, a section of impoverished Chinese immigrants resided in the Quang Lac area, which now encompasses Ta Hien Street. This region, originally a swamp, was reclaimed towards the end of the 19th century, with houses along the street constructed around the early 20th century to accommodate theater patrons. The street's name honors Ta Quang Hien, a leader of the Can Vuong movement in Thai Binh province.

In 2004, Ta Hien Street was chosen as a pilot project for urban renewal. The renovation focused on a 52-meter stretch of the street, involving both French colonial-style buildings and traditional Vietnamese architecture. The project commenced in 2010 and was completed on December 29, 2011. It included improvements to the facades of buildings and public spaces, funded by the government, while residents were responsible for interior renovations at their own expense. The total cost of the project was nearly 15 billion Vietnamese dong.

Ta Hien Street has been listed as a first-tier preservation zone since 1999 under Decision No. 45/1999/QD-UB by the Hanoi People's Committee. In 2025, the Hanoi Municipal People's Council issued Resolution No. 24/NQ-HĐND, designating Ta Hien Street among 21 streets in the Old Quarter as first-tier preservation zones.

Today, Ta Hien Street is popularly known as the "Western Street" or "Western Street of the Capital," renowned for its vibrant nightlife and bustling with tourists. Since October 3, 2014, Ta Hien Street has been designated as a pedestrian zone, alongside other streets such as Hang Buom, Ma May, Hang Gay, Luong Ngoc Quyen, and Dao Duy Tu.

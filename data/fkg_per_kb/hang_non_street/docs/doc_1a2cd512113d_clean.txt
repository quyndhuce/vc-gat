Hang Non Street is a small street located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi, running through the districts of Hang Gai and Cua Dong in Hoan Kiem district. The name "Hang Non" (Hat Street) originates from its historical role as a marketplace for various types of hats. Today, the main products sold on this street include bedding, quilts, pillows, and mattresses, particularly near its intersection with Hang Dieu Street.

The street has existed since the time of the Middle Le Dynasty. During the French colonial period, the French renamed it Rue des Chapeaux, which translates to Hat Street. In 1945, Mayor Tran Van Lai restored the original name, Hang Non, which has remained unchanged since then.

Hang Non Street stretches for 216 meters, starting at the junction of Hangquat and Hanghom streets, passing through Hangmanh, Hangthiep, and Hangdieu streets, ending at Duongthanh Street. Historically, the upper part of the street was known as Ma Vi Street because it produced costumes, headdresses, flags, and props used in theatrical performances, all of which required horsehair. The lower part specialized in making and selling hats, hence the name Hang Non.

The street's origins date back to the Middle Le Dynasty, situated on the land of the former Dong Thanh ward, which was part of the old Tho Xuong district. Long before the French colonized Vietnam, this area was already home to many shops selling different types of hats, including the "tu lo" hats worn by monks in temples.

During the French colonial era, the French renamed the street Rue des Chapeaux, literally meaning Hat Street. In 1945, Mayor Tran Van Lai reinstated the original name, Hang Non. Until the late 19th century, both men and women in Vietnam commonly wore hats. Men wore conical hats with silver or brass tips, while women had a variety of options, such as the thung quai thao hat for the more affluent, and the ba tam or chao hat made from soft gourd leaves for laborers. The thung quai thao hat was a type of hat worn by northern Vietnamese women, made from palm or gourd leaves, with a diameter of 70-80 cm and a brim width of 10-12 cm. It resembled a mushroom cap with a handle and a flat top.

By the end of the 1910s, except for those in Hanoi, men no longer wore hats, opting instead for headscarves or umbrellas. Wealthy women carried parasols, while only hardworking laborers continued to wear straw hats outdoors. Consequently, the number of hat shops on Hang Non Street gradually decreased, eventually selling their products mainly in markets. On July 28, 1929, the founding meeting of the Red Trade Union of Northern Vietnam was held at No. 15 Hang Non Street. Shop owners shifted their focus to other goods, such as selling wooden clogs painted with lacquer, like the businesses My Sinh and My Thinh, owned by people from Ha Vy village, a traditional lacquer craft village. They moved from Hanghom Street to Hang Non Street, where they bought pre-carved wooden clogs, painted them, and sold them.

In the late 20th century, the hat-making trade was revived, primarily catering to tourists in hotels. Nowadays, Hang Non Street no longer sells traditional products but offers a wide range of goods.

In the heart of Hanoi's capital city, nestled along the tree-lined Tây Sơn street, lies the majestic Gò Đống Đa, a testament to the nation's rich history and cultural heritage. This imposing mound, shaped like a towering raspberry, rises from the earth, its dark green canopy of ancient trees and mossy gray stone steps a striking visual cue that beckons visitors to explore its storied past.

Legend has it that in the spring of 1789, King Quang Trung, the legendary leader of the Tây Sơn insurgent army, led his troops to a resounding victory at the Battle of Ngọc Hồi - Đống Đa. The enemy's corpses were piled into large mounds, which later became overgrown with lush vegetation, earning the site its name, Gò Đống Đa, in commemoration of that glorious triumph.

Today, the mound stands as a powerful symbol of Vietnam's struggle for independence and freedom, its imposing black bronze statue of King Quang Trung rising majestically against the sky. The surrounding red-brick courtyard, bustling with city life, provides a striking contrast to the serene atmosphere of the mound, where locals and tourists alike can stroll and pay homage to the nation's heroic past.

As the sun casts its golden light upon the ancient banyan tree, its rustling leaves whispering secrets of the past, visitors can't help but feel a surge of sacred pride and reverence for the nation's immortal epic. Stepping onto the stone steps, one is transported to a bygone era, where the echoes of history linger, a poignant reminder of the sacrifices made by those who fought for the country's freedom.

On the 5th day of the lunar Tết festival, the mound comes alive with the majestic fire dragon procession, a vibrant spectacle that bursts with intense vitality, as the city's residents and visitors alike gather to celebrate the nation's cultural heritage. As the dragon's fiery breath illuminates the night sky, the mound's significance is palpable, a testament to the enduring power of history and culture to unite and inspire generations.

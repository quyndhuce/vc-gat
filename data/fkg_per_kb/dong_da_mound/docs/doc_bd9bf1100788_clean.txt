Dong Da Mound, a significant area in the heart of Hanoi, is one of the most rapidly developing districts while still preserving many traditional cultural elements. This district is home to numerous historical landmarks that hold great significance not only for Hanoi but also for Vietnam as a whole. One of the most notable events associated with Dong Da Mound is the victory of King Quang Trung over the Qing forces at the Battle of Dong Da in 1789. Additionally, it was from this mound that the first batch of anti-French resistance leaflets were distributed across the country in 1954.

Visitors to Dong Da Mound can explore several key attractions that offer a blend of historical and cultural experiences. The Van Mieng - National Academy of Letters and Education, originally built during the reign of King Ly Than Tong, stands as a testament to the rich educational heritage of Vietnam. It is here where Confucianism was established, and where prominent scholars such as Chu Van An, a revered teacher and founder of the Vietnamese education system, once studied. The academy is also known for its tradition of "capping ceremonies" for students who have passed their exams, and for the custom of "kneeling and bowing" to show respect to the academy's legacy.

Another must-visit site is the Kim Lien Pagoda, also known as the Cao Son Pagoda, located at No. 148 Kim Hoa Street. This pagoda is one of the ancient temples in Thang Long, situated south of the old citadel walls. It is believed to be the place where Cao Son Dai Vuong, the son of Lac Long Quan and Au Co, resided. Today, within the temple, visitors can find several stone steles that bear witness to the historical and cultural significance of the site.

Chua Phuc Khanh, also known as Chua Su, is another important destination. Built towards the end of the Tran Dynasty and the beginning of the Le Dynasty, this pagoda has survived through centuries, preserving a wealth of artifacts and sculptures that reflect the artistic and religious traditions of the time. The pagoda houses numerous statues and paintings that date back to the Tran Dynasty, offering a glimpse into the spiritual and artistic life of ancient Vietnam.

The Dong Da Mound itself is a significant historical site, housing the statue of King Quang Trung, the central exhibition hall, and the Dong Da Mound relic site. The statue of King Quang Trung was erected in 1989 to commemorate the 200th anniversary of the Battle of Dong Da. The exhibition hall, located just behind the statue, showcases various artifacts and exhibits related to the battle and the history of the region. Surrounding the mound are two cannons that were used during the war against the French, adding to the historical ambiance of the site.

For those seeking a more relaxed and cultural experience, the Phu Khanh Temple Quarter offers a unique blend of academic and culinary delights. Known for its numerous schools, the area is also famous for its vibrant food scene, with a wide array of street food stalls and restaurants serving up local delicacies. Visitors can indulge in a variety of dishes, ranging from traditional Vietnamese snacks to more elaborate meals, making it a perfect spot for food enthusiasts. However, given the sheer number of options available, it might be wise to plan ahead or follow a recommended itinerary to ensure a memorable dining experience.

Vincom Pham Ngoc Thach, located at No. 2 Pham Ngoc Thach Street, provides a modern shopping and entertainment venue that complements the historical sites in the area. This bustling commercial center offers a range of retail and leisure activities, catering to both locals and tourists alike.

In summary, Dong Da Mound is a district rich in historical and cultural significance, offering visitors a chance to immerse themselves in the past while enjoying the vibrant atmosphere of contemporary Hanoi. From ancient temples and academies to modern shopping centers, the area presents a unique blend of old and new, making it a fascinating destination for anyone interested in exploring the layers of Vietnamese history and culture.

Nestled within the bustling heart of Hanoi, Dong Da Mound stands as a testament to both historical significance and cultural richness, offering visitors a unique blend of heritage and modernity. This site, known for its pivotal role in the legendary victory of Emperor Quang Trung over the Qing forces in the spring of 1789, is not merely a static monument but a dynamic space that brings history to life through its architecture, traditions, and community engagement.

The mound itself is part of a larger complex that includes a variety of structures designed to honor and educate visitors about the historical event. The entrance gate leads to a series of meticulously preserved sites, including the memorial hill, a ceremonial archway, a statue of Emperor Quang Trung, a dedicated temple, and a museum housing exhibits related to the battle of Ngoc Hoi-Dong Da. These elements collectively form a comprehensive narrative that immerses visitors in the story of the decisive battle that marked a turning point in Vietnamese history.

Beyond the physical structures, Dong Da Mound hosts an annual festival that celebrates the military prowess and patriotic spirit of the region. This event, which reenacts key moments from the battle, serves not only as a tribute to the past but also as a platform for fostering national pride and community cohesion. The festival's activities, which include traditional performances and educational programs, attract a significant number of tourists and locals alike, making it a vibrant cultural event that highlights the area’s historical importance.

Since the administrative restructuring of Phuong Dong Da in July 2025, there has been renewed focus on leveraging the cultural assets of Dong Da Mound to enhance the district's identity and economic prospects. The local government now views the mound as a critical component of their cultural heritage, positioning it as a symbol of national pride and a driver of sustainable development. This strategic emphasis underscores the potential for Dong Da Mound to evolve into a year-round tourist destination, rather than one primarily associated with seasonal events.

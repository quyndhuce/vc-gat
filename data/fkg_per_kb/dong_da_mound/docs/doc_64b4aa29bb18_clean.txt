Dong Da Mound, also known as Dong Da Hill, is a significant historical site located within Dong Da Park in the Dong Da District of Hanoi, Vietnam. The mound holds great importance due to its connection to the Battle of Ngoc Hoi-Dong Da, a pivotal conflict between the Tay Son forces and the Manchu Qing army. Following their defeat, the Qing general Sam Nghia Dong chose to commit suicide rather than surrender, hanging himself on O (Loa Son) hill. Post-war, to foster diplomatic ties with the Qing dynasty, his body was returned for a formal burial, and local Han Chinese residents were permitted to construct a temple on what is now Dao Duy Tu Street.

After the battle, King Quang Trung ordered the collection and burial of enemy corpses in twelve large mounds scattered across Thinh Quang and Nam Dong villages. Over time, these mounds became overgrown with banyan trees, giving rise to the term "Dong Da," which translates to "Heap of Banyan Trees." In 1851, during road construction and market development, additional remains were discovered and consolidated into a single large burial site adjacent to O hill. This new mound gradually merged with O hill and was eventually referred to as Dong Da Mound.

During the French colonial period, the French army excavated the twelve original mounds but left O hill intact, making Dong Da Mound the sole surviving thirteenth mound. To honor the 200th anniversary of the battle's victory, Dong Da Park was established in 1989.

Today, Dong Da Mound stands prominently within the park, featuring a temple gate at its summit inscribed with the words "Trung Lieu Mieu" (Trung Lieu Shrine), though the actual temple no longer exists. A paved path leads from the base of the mound to a statue of King Quang Trung-Nguyen Hue, accompanied by two relief sculptures and a museum.

The area around Dong Da Mound also hosts cultural events, such as the annual Dong Quang Pagoda festival held on the fifth day of the Lunar New Year. This festival commemorates the Battle of Dong Da, with villagers gathering to participate in various activities including reenactments of the battle and ceremonial marches into the ancient Thang Long Citadel.

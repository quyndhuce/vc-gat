To Tich Street, nestled between Hang Quat and Hang Gai streets in the bustling district of Hoan Kiem, is a narrow lane that stretches just under 100 meters, yet it carries a rich history and cultural significance that resonates deeply within the heart of Hanoi. Known by locals as "Phố Thợ Tiện" or "Hàng Tiện," this street was once renowned for its skilled artisans who specialized in woodworking, particularly in the craft of carving and shaping wood into intricate and functional pieces. The origins of To Tich Street date back to the time when it was known as "Ruelle de To Tich" during the French colonial era, but its roots run much deeper, tracing back to a period long before the arrival of the French, when it was simply referred to as "Phố Thợ Tiện."

The street's name, To Tich, evokes a sense of nostalgia and longing, reflecting the deep connection that the residents have with their traditional crafts. According to Mr. Nguyen Dong, who resides at number 26A at the end of the street, the majority of the inhabitants were originally from Nhị Khê village in Ha Tay province (now part of Hanoi). It was these villagers who brought their expertise in woodworking to To Tich Street, establishing a thriving community centered around the craft. In the 1960s, the sounds of sawing, carving, and sanding filled the air day and night, creating a symphony of activity that was both mesmerizing and indicative of the street's importance as a hub for traditional woodworking.

The products crafted by the artisans ranged from religious artifacts such as altars and incense holders to everyday household items like wooden trays, medicine boxes, and even toys for children. This concentration of skilled craftsmen made To Tich Street synonymous with high-quality woodworking, earning it the moniker "Phố Mộc." However, as time marched on, the demand for these traditional wooden goods began to wane, replaced by modern alternatives that better suited the changing tastes and lifestyles of the city's inhabitants.

In the late 1990s, a new trend emerged on To Tich Street, one that would forever alter its character. The introduction of fruit smoothie shops, or "hoa quả dầm," marked a significant shift in the street's identity. Initially, only a few vendors set up shop at the end of the street, but soon enough, nearly every available space was occupied by these colorful and aromatic establishments. Today, the street is lined with numerous fruit smoothie shops, each offering a variety of fresh fruits blended with milk, coconut water, and ice, creating a refreshing and vibrant atmosphere that attracts a steady stream of visitors, especially young people and tourists.

Despite the allure of these modern eateries, the legacy of woodworking remains, albeit in a diminished form. Among the towering buildings and neon signs, one can still find a solitary artisan, Mr. Le Dinh Thang, continuing the tradition of woodworking at his modest shop located at number 7. His shop, though small and unassuming, stands as a testament to the enduring spirit of the craft. Inside, Mr. Thang diligently works away, his hands moving deftly over the wood, creating pieces that reflect the skill and dedication of generations past. Yet, the number of customers seeking his services has dwindled, with most of his patrons being curious foreigners or occasional passersby.

Mr. Thang's story is emblematic of the broader transformation of To Tich Street. While many of his former neighbors have transitioned to other trades, he remains committed to preserving the legacy of woodworking. Even as the street evolves, with houses numbered 15, 21, 25, and 27 now housing fashion boutiques and souvenir shops catering to tourists, Mr. Thang continues to produce handcrafted wooden items, albeit in smaller quantities. His persistence is driven not only by a desire to honor his ancestors but also by a deep-seated belief in the value of traditional craftsmanship.

As dusk falls, To Tich Street transforms once more, its lights flickering in a dance of colors and shadows. The smoothie shops buzz with activity, their bright displays drawing in crowds of young people eager to enjoy the sweet, fruity concoctions. Meanwhile, the sound of saws and chisels, once the dominant melody of the street, has been replaced by the hum of traffic and the chatter of visitors. This juxtaposition of old and new, tradition and modernity, encapsulates the essence of To Tich Street—a place where the echoes of the past linger amidst the vibrancy of contemporary life.

Visiting To Tich Street today offers a unique blend of historical charm and modern allure. The street's architecture, while largely dominated by modern structures, still retains some remnants of its past, with a few older buildings standing as silent witnesses to the street's storied history. The atmosphere is a mix of nostalgia and excitement, with the scent of freshly cut wood mingling with the sweet aroma of fruit smoothies. For those interested in traditional crafts, To Tich Street provides a glimpse into a bygone era, where the art of woodworking was not just a profession but a way of life. For others, it is a vibrant destination for refreshment and socialization, a place where the flavors of the past and the present come together in perfect harmony.

To Tich Street, formerly known as Ruelle de Tố Tịch during the French colonial era, has a rich history that dates back to around 1920 when it was first expanded. Initially, the narrow path from the intersection of Hang Gai Street was a dirt and stone road that became muddy and treacherous during rainy days. The street's development reflects a blend of colonial influence and traditional Vietnamese life, with its architecture and cultural practices deeply rooted in the past.

The street is home to a two-story building constructed in 1920 by the Pham family, one of the prominent families in the area. This structure stands as a testament to the architectural styles of the early 20th century, featuring elements typical of the period such as ornate facades and functional layouts designed for residential and commercial purposes. Adjacent to the Dong Ha communal house, this building represents the legacy of the Pham family, who have been significant contributors to the community's social and economic fabric.

A significant portion of To Tich Street, particularly the odd-numbered side near Hang Gai Street, is inhabited by descendants of villagers from Nhi Khe (now part of Hanoi), who brought their expertise in woodworking and wood carving to the area. These artisans specialized in intricate crafts like carving wooden blocks for printing classical Chinese and Vietnamese scripts, crafting furniture, decorative items, and even toys. Their work required high levels of skill and precision, often using simple machines powered by foot pedals before transitioning to more modern electric motors. The production process was small-scale, employing minimal labor and generating little waste, primarily consisting of wood shavings.

One notable landmark on the street is the single-story building complex at the corner of Hang Gai Street, which belonged to the Ic Gia shop. This establishment thrived due to the skilled craftsmanship in wood carving and ivory work, reflecting the prosperity and cultural significance of these trades in the neighborhood. Another prominent feature is the two-story building located in the middle of the street on the even-numbered side, owned by a merchant who operated a paint shop in Hang Gai Street. This structure exemplifies the integration of various trades and businesses within the community, contributing to the vibrant economic landscape of the area.

Today, To Tich Street is affectionately known among locals as the "Street of Crushed Fruits" due to the numerous fruit crushing shops that line the thoroughfare. These establishments attract visitors with their colorful displays and enticing aromas, making them a popular destination for those exploring the nearby pedestrian zones of Hang Ngang and Hang Dao streets. On weekends, when these areas are bustling with tourists and shoppers, the fruit shops on To Tich Street experience a surge in visitors, creating a lively atmosphere filled with the sounds of laughter and chatter.

Visitors can easily find themselves drawn into the vibrant scene, where they can enjoy a variety of crushed fruits served in traditional and innovative ways. The fruit dishes here have become a beloved local specialty, attracting not only residents of Hanoi but also international travelers seeking authentic culinary experiences. The combination of fresh ingredients, creative presentations, and the warm hospitality of the vendors makes To Tich Street a must-visit spot for anyone interested in experiencing the unique flavors and culture of Hanoi.

In summary, To Tich Street offers a fascinating glimpse into the historical, cultural, and culinary heritage of Hanoi. From its colonial-era architecture to the traditional crafts and contemporary fruit shops, the street encapsulates the dynamic interplay between past and present, offering visitors a rich tapestry of experiences that reflect the city's enduring charm and vitality.

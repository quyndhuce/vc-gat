Van Lake, nestled within the UNESCO World Heritage site of the Van Temple - National Academy, is emerging as a vibrant cultural hub that seamlessly blends traditional heritage with contemporary creativity. This picturesque body of water, located opposite the main entrance of the Van Temple, is transforming into a dynamic space where ancient traditions meet modern life through a variety of innovative and engaging activities.

According to Dr. Le Xuan Kieu, Director of the Van Temple - National Academy Cultural and Scientific Activities Center, the area around Van Lake has undergone significant renovations aimed at preserving and enhancing its historical significance while fostering a rich cultural experience. Over the past year, the center has developed Van Lake into a multifaceted destination that integrates elements of heritage, artisanal crafts, artistic performances, culinary delights, exhibitions, and educational workshops.

Van Lake's strategic location within the Van Temple complex provides it with a unique advantage, offering visitors a serene and culturally rich setting. The lake's surroundings feature a harmonious blend of natural beauty and historical charm, making it an ideal venue for a wide array of cultural events and community activities. Regularly scheduled workshops showcase traditional crafts such as Dong Ho paintings, lacquerware, Bat Trang pottery, silk weaving from Meo Village in Hung Yen, bamboo and rattan weaving from Phu Vinh, and traditional paper-making techniques. These hands-on demonstrations not only preserve local crafts but also offer tourists an immersive experience into Vietnamese artistry.

In addition to these craft workshops, Van Lake hosts a diverse range of cultural programs including folk music and dance performances, modern art exhibitions, and educational sessions focused on heritage preservation. The lake's ambiance is further enhanced by a variety of food stalls serving traditional dishes like lotus tea from Tay Ho, herbal teas, Hanoi rice cakes, bun oc (snail noodle soup), bun cha (grilled pork and noodle bowl), and other regional specialties. This culinary diversity reflects the rich gastronomic heritage of Vietnam, providing visitors with a taste of local flavors.

The cultural significance of Van Lake extends beyond individual exhibits to create a cohesive ecosystem of experiences. It serves as a platform for various organizations, schools, embassies, and businesses to host large-scale events, fostering a collaborative environment that enriches the cultural landscape of Hanoi. The lake's evening activities are particularly noteworthy, offering a different set of experiences compared to daytime visits. Visitors can explore over thirty traditional-style booths arranged around the lake, each offering unique insights into Vietnamese culture and craftsmanship.

Van Lake's development aligns with the broader vision of the Van Temple complex to become a new cultural industry center in Hanoi. This ambitious goal aims to transform Van Lake into a distinctive cultural experience zone, attracting both domestic and international tourists. To achieve this, the center plans to organize regular themed events starting from July 2026, focusing on themes such as "Strolling Around Van Lake – Living with Heritage." These events will run daily from 8 AM to 8 PM, with extended hours during Hanoi's nighttime economy period. Every Saturday, visitors can participate in workshops and cultural experience programs, while the last Sunday of each month will feature special events showcasing artisans and traditional crafts.

The integration of Van Lake with the Inner Temple and Van Lake itself creates a seamless cultural journey for visitors. This interconnectedness enhances the overall experience of exploring the Van Temple complex, allowing tourists to transition smoothly between historical sites and interactive cultural spaces. The addition of Van Lake as an experiential zone complements the traditional reverence of the Inner Temple, offering a more dynamic and engaging visit for both domestic and international travelers.

With nearly one million visitors to the Van Temple complex in the first six months of the year, approximately 60% of whom were international tourists, Van Lake's expansion is expected to significantly boost tourist interest. The cultural activities and interactive experiences at Van Lake provide a fresh perspective on Vietnamese heritage, making it a must-visit destination for those interested in understanding the rich cultural tapestry of Hanoi.

Van Lake, also known as Ho Van or Ho Minh Duong, is a significant historical site nestled within the heart of Hanoi, Vietnam, specifically at Number 58 Quoc Tu Giam Street, Phuong Van Mieu, Dong Da District. This tranquil body of water is not only a testament to the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam but also a living museum that encapsulates the essence of Vietnamese scholarly traditions and intellectual pursuits. The lake's serene presence is a direct reflection of its historical importance, serving as a venue for scholars to engage in literary critiques and discussions during the feudal era.

Van Lake is situated within the expansive grounds of the Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site that has been a cornerstone of Vietnamese education since the early days of the Le dynasty in the 15th century. The lake's original purpose was to host gatherings where scholars would convene to critique poetry and prose, thereby upholding the tradition of academic excellence and scholarly discourse. At the center of the lake lies a small hill called Kim Chau, which once housed a pavilion known as Phan Thuy Dien, where scholars would gather to discuss literature and Confucian philosophy. Although the pavilion no longer stands, two stone steles remain on the hill, commemorating the beauty and significance of the lake. One of these steles, erected in 1865 during the reign of Emperor Tu Duc, documents the restoration of the Van Mieu and the dredging of the lake in 1863.

The lake itself is rectangular in shape, covering an area of approximately 12,000 square meters, surrounded by sturdy walls that protect its historical integrity. The central feature of the lake is the small hill, Kim Chau, which serves as a focal point for visitors and a reminder of the scholarly activities that once took place there. The surrounding area is adorned with ancient trees that provide shade and contribute to the tranquil atmosphere of the lake. The pathways around the lake are wide and well-maintained, ensuring easy access for visitors while preserving the natural beauty of the site. The lake's waters are kept clear through regular maintenance and drainage systems, enhancing its aesthetic appeal and ecological balance.

Throughout its history, Van Lake has undergone various transformations and restorations, each aimed at maintaining its historical value and cultural significance. During the Nguyen dynasty, the lake continued to serve as a cultural space for organizing examinations, traditional festivals, and folk art performances. Despite the passage of time and the changes brought about by modernization, Van Lake retains its original charm and remains a vital part of the educational and cultural landscape of Vietnam.

Today, Van Lake is a vibrant hub of cultural activities, hosting events such as calligraphy exhibitions, traditional book fairs, and folk art performances. These events attract both locals and tourists, offering a unique opportunity to experience the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam. Calligraphy exhibitions showcase works from renowned artists, providing insight into the art of calligraphy and the reverence for learning that has been a hallmark of Vietnamese culture. Traditional book fairs feature stalls selling antique and contemporary books, author signings, and discussions with prominent writers, appealing to readers of all ages. Folk art performances include traditional music, puppetry, and dance, bringing to life the cultural traditions of Vietnam in a dynamic and engaging manner.

In addition to these cultural events, Van Lake also hosts educational programs and guided tours designed for students and scholars. These initiatives aim to preserve and promote the historical and cultural significance of the site, ensuring that future generations can appreciate and learn from the legacy of Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam. The lake's serene ambiance and historical significance make it a must-visit destination for anyone interested in the rich tapestry of Vietnamese history and culture. Visitors can immerse themselves in the tranquil surroundings, reflecting on the scholarly traditions that have shaped the nation's intellectual heritage over centuries.

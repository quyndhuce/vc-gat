In the heart of Hanoi's historic district, a serene oasis awaits discovery - Van Lake, also known as Minh Đường, situated directly opposite the main gate of the revered Temple of Literature. This tranquil mirror-like lake, created in the 11th century alongside Vietnam's first university, embodies the essence of the entire complex. The name Van Lake symbolizes a brilliant civilization, where water gathered, accumulating the auspicious energy of heaven and earth, and where scholars of old would often recite poetry and discuss before examinations.

As one approaches the lake, the oval-shaped water surface, emerald green and calm, is framed by a sturdy grey stone embankment. The unique highlight in the center is Kim Chau mound, where the octagonal Phan Thuy pavilion rises, its ancient tiled roof a testament to the region's rich cultural heritage. Ancient trees, their branches stretching towards the sky, harmoniously intertwine with the busy street, where vehicles whizz by, creating a striking contrast between the tranquil lake and the bustling city.

The Temple of Literature, founded in 1070 under the Lý dynasty, with Emperor Lý Thánh Tông as its founder, is a national special relic and a UNESCO heritage site. Van Lake, an integral part of this complex, is a sacred space where Confucius worship and education reverence are deeply rooted. Every spring, the lake comes alive with the vibrant Spring Calligraphy Festival, as calligraphers create stunning works of art on star-dusted paper, filling the air with the sweet scent of ink and the soft rustling of paper.

As one pauses by the lake, the sense of peace is palpable, a profound bass note amidst the city's cacophony. The lake's tranquil atmosphere, surrounded by ancient trees and the majestic Phan Thuy pavilion, offers an experience that purifies the soul and connects one with a thousand-year-old cultural source.

Bat Trang Pottery Village, situated on the outskirts of Hanoi, Vietnam, is renowned for its exquisite ceramics and porcelain, which have been crafted there since the 14th century during the Ly-Tran dynasty. The village's location in an area abundant with clay suitable for ceramic production has contributed significantly to its historical prominence as one of the premier centers for pottery in Vietnam, alongside other notable villages such as Chu Đậu, Biên Hòa, Phù Lãng, Hương Canh, Lái Thiêu, and Bầu Trúc.

Bat Trang Pottery Village has evolved into a significant tourist attraction, drawing both domestic and international visitors who come not only to purchase unique ceramic pieces but also to experience the art of pottery making firsthand. This cultural significance was officially recognized by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism of Vietnam on December 20, 2019, when it was declared a national intangible cultural heritage. Today, the village boasts over 200 enterprises and more than 1,000 households engaged in the production and trade of a diverse range of ceramic goods, encompassing various types and designs.

Bat Trang Pottery Village (Làng gốm Bát Tràng) is located in Gia Lam district, Hanoi, on the Red River's eastern side. It is a traditional ceramics village.
Bat Trang is a long-established ceramic-producing village with a history commonly traced through the medieval period.
Craft lineages, kilns, trade and modern workshops shaped its identity.
Workshops and retail showrooms occupy the village street network.
The contemporary Bat Trang Museum is a distinct modern landmark.
Kim Truc Tu Pagoda is a religious site in the wider village.
Characteristic physical features include ceramic bowls, vases, glazed tiles, decorative pottery, pottery wheels, kiln equipment and narrow village lanes.
Bat Trang Pottery Village is associated with the following named places and physical sub-entities: Pottery workshops, ceramic market, Bat Trang Museum, kilns, Kim Truc Tu Pagoda.
The site is associated with clay preparation, throwing, molding, glazing, firing and craft tourism.
Bat Trang is a traditional ceramics village on the outskirts of Hanoi.

Ceramic workshops produce household vessels, decorative pottery, and religious objects.
Ceramic production involves preparing clay, shaping objects, drying objects, glazing surfaces, and firing wares.
A pottery wheel is used to shape certain vessels.
Molds are used to produce some repeated ceramic forms.
Kilns heat ceramic objects to high temperatures.
Ceramic glazes produce colored and textured surfaces.
Bat Trang is known for blue-and-white ceramic decoration.
Bat Trang artisans also produce carved and embossed surface decoration.
Workshops display bowls, vases, plates, and jars.
The village contains lanes connecting workshops, shops, and residences.
The Bat Trang pottery market sells ceramic products to visitors.
The Bat Trang Museum presents ceramics and the history of local craftsmanship.

As you step through the doors of the Hanoi Museum, the 1st floor – the main lobby – unfolds like a vast, open space in the heart of the city, where every journey to discover the thousand-year-old history of Hanoi seems to begin. Located at the very center of the building, beneath the soaring skylight of the inverted pyramid architecture, the space makes anyone who has just set foot inside feel small before the sheer scale and depth of the place. This is not only a reception area but also considered the "open heart" of the museum, connecting the exhibition floors and guiding visitors into the flow of the capital's history.

Since the museum's inauguration on the occasion of the 1000th Anniversary of Thang Long – Hanoi in 2010, the main lobby has become a regular venue for thematic exhibitions, cultural events, and community activities. Hanoi residents drop by to see new collections, students visit in groups to learn about the city's rich history, while foreign tourists often pause for a long time to gaze up at the overall architecture before starting their tour. The main lobby's central atrium, with its cream-white tones, glass, and light-colored stone, creates a modern, airy feel, while the large square void in the center of the structure, where natural light pours down, is a striking feature.

The scene is typically set with an information desk, an open exhibition area, staircases, and layered floor railings above, all of which contribute to the sense of grandeur and importance. As visitors explore the space, they are surrounded by the soft echo of footsteps and the gentle light pouring down from the skylight, making it easy to feel as though Hanoi is slowly unfolding its layers of memory before their eyes. The 1st floor main lobby is a gateway to the city's history, a threshold to the past, and a symbol of the city's enduring cultural heritage.

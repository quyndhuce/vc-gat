The 1st Floor: Main hall of the Hanoi Museum, established in 1982 and relocated to its current site at Phạm Hùng Street in Mễ Trì, Nam Từ Liêm in 2010, serves as a grand entrance and central hub for visitors. The museum's construction began on May 19, 2008, and was completed on October 6, 2010, marking a significant milestone in the city's cultural landscape. The building, designed with six floors, including four above-ground levels and two underground floors, occupies a vast area of over 54,000 square meters. The architectural design of the main hall is inspired by the iconic One Pillar Pagoda, reflecting a harmonious blend of modernity and traditional Vietnamese aesthetics.

The main hall is strategically positioned to welcome visitors from all four cardinal directions, symbolizing the convergence of diverse cultures towards the historic city of Hanoi. This design not only enhances the aesthetic appeal but also ensures a comfortable and welcoming atmosphere for tourists and locals alike. The spacious and open layout of the main hall facilitates easy navigation and allows for a seamless flow of visitors throughout the museum.

Inside the 1st Floor: Main hall, the architectural elements are meticulously crafted to create a sense of openness and connectivity. The floor plan is designed to accommodate various activities and exhibitions, making it a versatile space for both educational and social purposes. The main hall's orientation towards the four directions is particularly noteworthy, as it draws fresh air and natural light into the space, enhancing the overall ambiance and visitor experience.

In addition to its functional purpose, the main hall also serves as a focal point for cultural events and gatherings. It houses two large meeting rooms in the basement level, each equipped with advanced audiovisual systems and comfortable seating arrangements. The round meeting room can comfortably seat up to 300 guests, while the square room accommodates up to 500 attendees. These facilities make the main hall an ideal venue for conferences, seminars, and cultural events, further enriching the museum's role in the community.

The main hall's design also incorporates several unique features that enhance its visual appeal and functionality. For instance, the spiral staircase connecting the 1st Floor to the 4th Floor is inspired by the three concentric walls of the ancient Citadel of Co Loa. This innovative design not only adds a distinctive touch to the interior but also provides a convenient route for visitors, especially those with mobility challenges, to access different levels of the museum.

Moreover, the main hall is surrounded by lush gardens, ponds, and traditional architectural elements such as the Mong Phu village gate and the Tran Lang house, creating a serene and picturesque setting. These elements seamlessly integrate with the modern structure, offering visitors a unique blend of old and new. The presence of these traditional structures alongside the contemporary design underscores the museum's commitment to preserving and celebrating Hanoi's rich cultural heritage.

Visitors to the 1st Floor: Main hall are often captivated by the numerous photo spots and artistic installations that adorn the space. From the intricate shell-patterned wall to the charming corners that serve as perfect backdrops for photographs, the main hall offers a variety of settings for memorable snapshots. These areas have become popular among fashion brands and photographers, frequently hosting photo shoots and promotional events for renowned labels like Ivy, Elyse, and Seven AM.

The 1st Floor: Main hall is not just a gateway to the museum's exhibits but also a testament to Hanoi's storied past and vibrant present. As visitors step into this space, they are greeted by a blend of historical artifacts, modern amenities, and cultural symbolism, setting the stage for an enriching journey through the city's history and traditions.

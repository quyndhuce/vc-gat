Hang Thung Street, or Phố Hàng Thùng in Vietnamese, is a captivating thoroughfare that weaves its way across the heart of the Old Quarter, nestled between the bustling Red River dike bank and the winding main road connecting to the bờ đê. This historic street, born from the echoes of sawing and chiseling of centuries past, was once a hub for skilled carpenters who crafted wooden barrels, tubs, and bathtubs for the daily life of the imperial capital. The name Hang Thung, derived from the sounds of those bygone days, whispers tales of a bygone era.

As one strolls down this charming street, the faded, moss-covered yellow hue of the nhà ống buildings blends seamlessly with the dazzling lights and colorful plastic tables and chairs, creating a vibrant atmosphere that is quintessentially Hanoi. The street's unique identifying feature is the multitude of seafood stalls and platters of ốc, emitting thick smoke that spills out onto the roadside, enticing passersby with the savory aromas of the city's culinary delights.

Beneath the canopy of trees, adjacent to the main road, Hang Thung Street comes alive at dusk, as travelers and locals gather around the seafood stalls, creating an endless festive atmosphere. The air is filled with the sounds of laughter and chatter, as the scent of grilled seafood and steaming boiled snails wafts through the air, transporting visitors to a world of culinary delights. As the night falls, the street transforms into a vibrant hub of activity, with the soft glow of lanterns and the twinkling lights of the surrounding buildings casting a magical spell over the entire scene.

Hang Thung Street is a testament to the city's rich cultural heritage, a place where the past and present converge. It is a place where one can immerse oneself in the city's vibrant vitality and subtle charm, savoring the salty street flavor and the moss-covered space, and gaining a deeper understanding of the thousand-year-old Thang Long.

Hang Thung Street is located within the districts of Ly Thai To and Hang Bac in Hoan Kiem District, Hanoi. The street stretches approximately 216 meters in length and is 8 meters wide, running from Tran Quang Khai Street to its intersection with Hang Be Street (which continues to Cau Go Street), crossing through Hang Tre and Nguyen Huu Huan streets.

In the past, the name of the street originated from its role as the center for producing and selling wooden and bamboo barrels used to store fish sauce or water for daily use during an era when the city lacked a piped water supply system. Historically, this area was part of the village of So Trang (eastern half) and Dong Yen (western half), both belonging to the Tả Túc commune (later renamed to Hòu Túc), in the old Thọ Xương district. By the mid-19th century, So Trang merged with Tả Lâu to form Trang Lâu village. Remnants of this ancient area are still preserved in the Trang Lauer Temple at number 33 on the street. The Dong Yen Communal House, which once stood opposite the Trang Lauer Temple across Nguyen Huu Huan Street, was dismantled around 1970.

Due to its strategic location near the Dong Yen gate, leading directly to the Red River port, barges carrying logs and bamboo rafts from upstream regions such as Phu Tho and Tuyen Quang would dock here. The abundant and inexpensive local raw materials facilitated the thriving craft of making wooden barrels. Craftsmen in the area specialized in splitting wood from banyan and persimmon trees to construct barrels, then sealing them with tung oil to prevent leaks.

During the colonial period, the street was divided into two segments with different commercial characteristics and names:

The eastern segment, from Tran Nhut Duct Street to Nguyen Huu Huan Street, was called Phuc Chau Street (Rue Fou-tchéou) until 1919, when it was renamed Rongdoni Street (Rue Rondony). This section primarily housed large timber warehouses that imported logs, planks, and coffins.

The western segment, from Nguyen Huu Huan Street to Hang Be Street, was officially named Rue des Sceaux (Street of Barrels) by the colonial authorities, reflecting the local name "Hang Thung."

After the success of the August Revolution in 1945, the Viet Minh government renamed Rongdoni Street to Binh Chuan Street, after a feudal official responsible for foreign trade. By the end of 1945, the two segments were officially merged and reverted to the original Vietnamese name, Hang Thung Street. This name has remained unchanged throughout subsequent adjustments to the street's designation until today.

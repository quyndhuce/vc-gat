The High Sanctuary, nestled within the grand complex of the Temple of Literature and National Academy, stands as a testament to the profound reverence for Confucianism and scholarly tradition in Vietnam. This sacred space, known as the Bái Đường, is a place where ancient rituals and ceremonies were once conducted to honor the revered sages and scholars who have shaped the intellectual heritage of the nation. The sanctuary's architecture is a blend of intricate design and functional elegance, reflecting the deep-rooted cultural significance it holds within the heart of Hanoi.

Constructed with meticulous attention to detail, the High Sanctuary features a series of steps leading up to its entrance, each layer of stone paving symbolizing the ascent towards enlightenment and wisdom. The floor is laid with tiles that extend across the entire expanse, dividing the interior into nine distinct sections, each delineated by the imposing presence of forty wooden columns crafted from lim wood. These columns support the roof, which is designed in a sophisticated manner with intersecting beams and rafters, creating a harmonious structure that is both sturdy and aesthetically pleasing. The roof itself is covered with curved ceramic tiles, a style known as "ngói mũi hài," and adorned with ornamental motifs of two dragons facing the sun and moon, a visual representation of the balance between celestial forces and earthly pursuits.

The windows on either side of the main hall are framed with delicate carvings of lattice work, showcasing the skillful craftsmanship of artisans from the Lê dynasty. Below these windows, intricate wood carvings depict scenes of dragons amidst swirling clouds and swords, adding a layer of mystique and historical depth to the sanctuary's ambiance. At the center of the sanctuary, a large altar is placed, upon which various offerings are displayed, including incense burners and ceremonial vessels. On either side of this central altar stand pairs of bronze cranes perched atop tortoises, symbolizing longevity and wisdom. Above the altar hangs a banner inscribed with the phrase "Vạn thế sư biểu," paying homage to Confucius as the ultimate exemplar of learning and virtue throughout history. To the east of the altar, another banner reads "Cổ kim nhật nguyệt," alongside the Bích Ung bell, further enriching the sanctity of the space with its historical and spiritual significance.

In the past, students and officials would gather here to pay their respects to the ancient scholars and philosophers, lighting incense sticks in a gesture of devotion and respect. Today, the High Sanctuary continues to serve as a focal point for educational and cultural activities, attracting numerous school groups, families, and individuals from across the country who come to pay homage and celebrate the enduring legacy of learning and scholarship. The atmosphere within the sanctuary is one of solemnity and reverence, transporting visitors back in time to reflect on the profound philosophical teachings of the ancient sages. As one wanders through the halls, the intricate details of the architecture and the serene ambiance combine to create a deeply immersive experience, offering a glimpse into the rich tapestry of Vietnamese academic and cultural traditions.

Le Nin Park, originally known as Chi Lăng Flower Garden, is a significant green space located in Hanoi, Vietnam, dedicated to the memory of Vladimir Lenin. Situated across from the Vietnam Military History Museum, the park occupies a triangular plot of land spanning 17,183 square meters, bordered by the streets of Điện Biên Phú, Trần Phú, and Hoàng Diệu.

The history of the site dates back to before the establishment of the park, when it was home to a lake called Elephant Lake (Hồ Voi). Legend has it that soldiers would bathe their elephants in this lake. In 1894, the French colonial government filled in the lake and built a garden named Robin Flower Garden, which locals referred to as "Farming Scenery Flower Garden" due to a statue of a farmer plowing with a water buffalo.

Following the Japanese coup against the French colonial rule in March 1945, the new mayor of Hanoi, Trần Văn Lai, removed French colonial monuments and renamed several locations. The statues in Robin Flower Garden were dismantled, and the park was renamed Chi Lăng Flower Garden in honor of the Vietnamese victory at the Battle of Chi Lăng. This name persisted even after the Communist Party of Vietnam took control of Hanoi.

In 1982, the Vietnamese government decided to erect a monument to Lenin in the park. With assistance from the Soviet Union, a 5.2-meter-tall bronze statue of Lenin was crafted. The statue was unveiled on August 20, 1985, marking a significant shift in the park’s identity.

On October 7, 2003, the park was officially renamed Le Nin Park, reflecting its dedication to the Russian revolutionary leader. Interestingly, from 1980 to 2003, another park in Hanoi had been known as Lenin Park, but after the renaming in 2003, it reverted to its original name, Thong Nhat Park.

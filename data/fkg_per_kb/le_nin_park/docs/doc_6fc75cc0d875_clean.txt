Le Nin Park, also known as Thong Nhat Park, is a beloved green space nestled in the heart of Hanoi, Vietnam. This park, named after Vladimir Lenin, the iconic leader of the Russian Revolution and a symbol of the strong historical ties between Vietnam and the former Soviet Union, stands as a testament to the rich cultural and political heritage of the city. Situated at 28A Dien Bien Phu Street in Ba Dinh District, Le Nin Park is easily accessible from many parts of the bustling metropolis, making it a popular destination for both locals and tourists alike.

The origins of Le Nin Park date back to its initial construction by the French colonial authorities between 1894 and 1897. Over the years, the park has undergone significant transformations, reflecting the changing dynamics of Vietnamese history. Following Vietnam's independence in 1945, the park was renamed Chi Lang Garden. However, it wasn't until 1982 that the park took on its current form when the bronze statue of Lenin was erected. The statue, standing tall at 5.2 meters, including a base of 2.7 meters, was completed in 1985 and officially marked the renaming of the park to Le Nin Park in 2003. Today, this park remains one of the most important historical landmarks in Hanoi, serving not only as a recreational area but also as a site of cultural significance.

Visitors to Le Nin Park can enjoy a wide array of activities within its expansive grounds, which cover over 17,000 square meters. The park is home to numerous ancient saul trees, some of which have stood for centuries, providing ample shade and tranquility. These towering trees, with their broad canopies, create a serene atmosphere that is particularly appealing during the hot summer months. In the evenings, the park becomes a haven for people of all ages, who come to walk, jog, or simply relax under the cool breeze and peaceful ambiance.

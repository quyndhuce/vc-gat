The Stone Dragons of Kinh Thien Palace, known in Vietnamese as Rồng đá Điện Kính Thiên, stand as a prominent architectural feature within the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long, located in the heart of Hanoi, Vietnam. These majestic stone dragons were crafted during the Early Le dynasty in the 15th century, specifically under the reign of Emperor Le Thanh Tong, reflecting the pinnacle of stone carving artistry from that era. Situated symmetrically along both sides of the stone staircase leading to the elevated platform of Kinh Thien Palace, these dragons serve not only as decorative elements but also as symbols of royal authority and cultural prestige.

The dragons are arranged in a symmetrical pattern, with each dragon's elongated body following the contours of the stone steps, creating a visually striking and harmonious composition. Their dominant color is the light gray of natural stone, with surfaces showing signs of erosion and moss, adding to their historical character. Each dragon is depicted with a raised head, curved forms, and intricate details such as scales and claws, which are clearly carved to perfection. This arrangement of stone dragons, combined with the elevated foundation of Kinh Thien Palace, the open courtyard in front, and the surrounding spacious environment, creates a solemn and easily recognizable architectural ensemble.

Kinh Thien Palace, once the political center of the royal court, holds significant historical and cultural importance. The stone dragons, as part of this site, are integral to the cultural life of Hanoi, attracting visitors who come to explore the rich history and appreciate the ancient artistic values embedded in the stone carvings. The Imperial Citadel of Thang Long, where the Stone Dragons of Kinh Thien Palace are located, is designated as a national special relic site and is part of the UNESCO World Heritage complex, further emphasizing its cultural and historical significance.

Cultural events and major ceremonies of the capital often take place within the citadel area, reinforcing the symbolic importance of the stone dragons. These dragons, with their flowing forms and detailed craftsmanship, evoke a sense of authority, sacredness, and enduring historical depth.

As the sun sets over the tranquil Hoan Kiem Lake, the rustic charm of the Đánh Bắt Cá (Fishing) performance at the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre comes alive. Nestled on Dinh Tien Hoang Street, this cultural gem has been a staple of Vietnamese heritage since 1969, when it first welcomed audiences to its enchanting stage. The theatre's name, Thang Long, is a nod to the majestic name given to the capital city by King Ly Thai To, who moved the capital from Hoa Lu to Thang Long in the 11th century.

As the performance begins, the rustic brown hues of the puppeteers' attire blend seamlessly with the brilliant silver sheen of the lacquer layer on the wooden puppets, drawing the audience's gaze. The masterful casting of the bamboo lift net, sending white foam splashing, is a testament to the puppeteers' skill and artistry. The entire scene is set against the rippling lake surface, nestled in front of a rustic hanging bamboo screen, transporting the audience to a bygone era.

The Đánh Bắt Cá performance is more than just a display of puppetry; it is a vessel carrying the soul of Vietnamese culture, delivering a unique experience in the heart of the bustling city. As the water surface gradually settles, the audience is left with a poignant nostalgia for the homeland, mingled with a strangely serene peace. This performance is a celebration of the nation's soul, a testament to the country's rich cultural heritage, and a must-see attraction for any visitor to Hanoi.

The Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre is a sanctuary preserving the nation's soul, a place where the past and present converge. As the Đánh Bắt Cá performance comes to a close, the audience is left with a deeper appreciation for the cultural significance of this ancient art form, which has been passed down through generations. The theatre's commitment to preserving this cultural treasure is a testament to the country's dedication to its heritage, and a must-see experience for anyone interested in Vietnamese culture.

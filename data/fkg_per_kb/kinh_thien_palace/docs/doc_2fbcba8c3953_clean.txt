The Kính Thien Palace, originally known as Điện Kính Thiên (敬天殿), was a significant architectural landmark of the imperial palace complex during the Later Lê dynasty, situated in the capital city of Đông Kinh, now modern-day Hanoi. Constructed in the heart of the Imperial Citadel, it stood on the summit of Nùng Mountain, precisely where the former Lý dynasty's Thiên An Palace had once been. To the right of the Kính Thien Palace was the Chí Kinh Palace, while the Vạn Thọ Palace was positioned to its left. Across from it lay the Thị Triều Palace, where monthly court audiences took place, and in front of that stood the Đoan Môn Gate.

The origins of the Kính Thien Palace date back to 1428 when it was initially built under the reign of Lê Thái Tổ. Its construction was completed during the rule of Lê Thánh Tông. This grand hall served as the site where Lê Thái Tổ declared his ascension to the throne in 1428. Over time, it became a crucial venue for various important ceremonies, including the reception of foreign envoys, imperial conferences, and sacrificial rites.

In 1816, Emperor Gia Long of the Nguyễn dynasty ordered the reconstruction of the Kính Thien Palace. Subsequently, it was utilized as a temporary residence by Nguyễn dynasty emperors during their northern travels. However, in 1841, Emperor Thiệu Trị renamed the palace to Điện Long Thiên (隆天殿).

Unfortunately, the Kính Thien Palace met its demise in 1886 when French colonial authorities demolished it to construct an artillery command center. Today, only remnants of its steps and foundation can be found within the Hanoi Old Quarter, serving as a testament to its historical significance.

Nestled peacefully along the tree-lined peninsula shore of West Lake, the Ngũ Xã bronze casting workshop stands as a testament to the enduring cultural heritage of Hanoi. The name Ngũ Xã, meaning "Five Villages," was born nearly five centuries ago when elite artisans from five renowned bronze casting villages converged upon the imperial capital, igniting the first sacred fires to honor the kings and lords. Today, the preparation of the furnace remains a revered ritual that encapsulates the soul and essence of the city’s rich cultural tapestry.

The process of preparing the furnace begins with the meticulous construction of a towering cylindrical kiln block, a structure that dominates a corner of the workshop. Built from clay mixed with rice husks, this primitive yet sturdy kiln exudes a rustic grayish-brown hue, darkened by the presence of charcoal. The most striking feature of the kiln is its deep kiln door, which glows with brilliant red-orange sparks, a mesmerizing sight that draws visitors into the heart of the workshop. Surrounded by copper molds and a smoke-stained brick wall, the kiln stands as a focal point amidst the cluttered workspace, a symbol of the artisans' dedication and skill.

On days preparing for large casting batches, especially during festive seasons or early spring, the workshop buzzes with activity. Artisans diligently knead rice husk mud, arranging firewood to prime the furnace, their movements synchronized with the rhythm of the ancient craft. The air is filled with the distinctive pungent smell of smoke, a scent that permeates the entire space, blending with the sounds of calls and laughter. This bustling atmosphere captivates visitors, who stand in awe before the furnace, feeling a deep admiration for the artisans’ unwavering patience and commitment.

The furnace preparation is more than just a technical process; it is a ritual imbued with cultural significance. Before the molten metal is poured, the act of building the furnace and lighting the fire is seen as a respectful invocation of the spirits, a gesture that honors the legacy of generations past. This tradition, passed down through the ages, reflects the passion and dedication of the artisans who have preserved the essence of Thăng Long, the ancient name for Hanoi, through their craft. The heat emanating from the furnace is not merely a physical phenomenon but a vibrant symbol of the artisans' relentless pursuit of excellence, a testament to the enduring spirit of the capital city.

Source: Ben Thanh Market Heritage & Saigon Urban Architectural Archives
Ben Thanh Market (Cho Ben Thanh) in District 1, Ho Chi Minh City, is one of the oldest and most recognizable commercial landmarks in Saigon. Its name originates from the seventeenth century, when informal markets gathered near the Ben Nghe River wharf beside the Gia Dinh Citadel. After draining the Marais Boresse swamp in 1911, French contractor Etablissements Brossard et Mopin constructed the new market hall between 1912 and March 1914 over 13,000 square meters, originally naming it Les Halles Centrales de Saigon. Its signature architectural feature is the prominent cream-yellow southern clock tower with three clock faces overlooking Quach Thi Trang Square. Four wide main entrance gates—South, North, East, and West—open onto busy District 1 streets, adorned above with distinctive terracotta ceramic bas-reliefs depicting agricultural produce, cattle, and fish. Designed in an Indochinese-French colonial style adapted for tropical ventilation, the spacious interior hall features high corrugated metal and skylight trusses, wide walkways, and tightly packed stalls selling textiles, ao dai fabrics, conical hats, handicrafts, dried goods, coffee, spices, fresh produce, and traditional southern Vietnamese street food.

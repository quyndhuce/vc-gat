Source: Cholon Heritage Archives & Vietnam National Cultural Site Records
Ba Thien Hau Temple (Chua Ba Thien Hau), officially the Tue Thanh Assembly Hall (Hoi quan Tue Thanh), stands at 710 Nguyen Trai Street in the historic Chinatown of Cholon, District 5, Ho Chi Minh City. Founded around 1760 by Cantonese immigrants from Guangzhou in Guangdong, China, and renovated in 1800, 1842, and 1890, it is dedicated to Mazu (Thien Hau Thanh Mau), the Chinese sea goddess deified from Lin Moniang who protects sailors, fishermen, and merchants. Built with materials imported from China in a traditional Quoc-character rectangular layout, the temple connects a front vestibule, an open-air central courtyard (sky well) admitting daylight and ventilation, and a richly decorated main sanctuary. Its curved Yin-Yang tiled roofs and upper walls are famous for intricate, colorful Shiwan porcelain and ceramic reliefs depicting scenes from Chinese mythology, dragons, phoenixes, lions, and classical legends. Inside the dimly lit, atmospheric sanctuary hang hundreds of large red spiral incense coils suspended from the wooden rafters with pink prayer papers attached, drifting fragrant smoke above carved wooden altars, gilded lacquer calligraphy panels, bronze bells, stone steles, and the wooden statue of Lady Thien Hau flanked by guardians Thien Ly Nhan and Thuan Phong Nhi. In 1993, it was recognized as a National Architectural and Artistic Heritage Site.

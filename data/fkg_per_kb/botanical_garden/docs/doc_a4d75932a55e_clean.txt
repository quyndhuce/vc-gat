A Botanical Garden is a specialized institution that maintains a documented collection of living plants for the purposes of scientific research, conservation, display, and education. These gardens often feature labeled plants with their botanical names and additional information, allowing visitors to learn more about each species. They can house diverse plant collections, including cacti, succulents, herbs, and plants from different regions around the world. Specialized structures like glasshouses and shadehouses are common, housing tropical, alpine, or other exotic plants that do not naturally occur in the local area.

Most Botanical Gardens are accessible to the public, offering guided tours, workshops, courses, educational displays, art exhibitions, book rooms, and even open-air performances. They frequently collaborate with universities or other scientific organizations, maintaining herbaria and conducting research programs in plant taxonomy and other botanical sciences. Staff members typically consist of both botanists and gardeners, ensuring the scientific management and maintenance of the plant collections.

Many Botanical Gardens provide educational opportunities through diploma or certificate programs in horticulture, botany, and taxonomy, alongside internships for aspiring horticulturists. Students and researchers also benefit from the facilities, using the extensive plant collections for their studies.

Definitions of Botanical Gardens vary but generally encompass institutions that hold scientifically managed and documented collections of living plants for research, conservation, display, and education. The New Royal Horticultural Society Dictionary of Gardening notes that while some Botanical Gardens serve primarily as public recreational spaces, others engage deeply in scientific activities. The Botanic Gardens Conservation International offers a refined definition that emphasizes the scientific and educational purposes of these gardens.

In 1976, the Liberty Hyde Bailey Hortorium of Cornell University provided a detailed definition, highlighting the multifaceted roles of Botanical Gardens. These institutions are characterized by their scientific management, educational programs, and research activities, often featuring greenhouses, test grounds, herbaria, and arboreta. They support a scientific and horticultural staff and prioritize publication as a means of disseminating knowledge.

Botanical Gardens can operate independently, as part of government operations, or as departments within colleges or universities. Their primary focus is on scientific pursuits, distinguishing them from purely ornamental gardens, experimental stations, or public parks. The core mission is the acquisition and dissemination of botanical knowledge.

Historically, Botanical Gardens have adapted to contemporary interests and values. For instance, Ferdinand von Mueller, the director of the Royal Botanic Gardens in Melbourne from 1852 to 1873, emphasized the scientific and educational objectives of these institutions. He outlined numerous goals that Botanical Gardens pursued during the mid-19th century, reflecting their role in advancing botanical knowledge and serving as centers for scientific inquiry and public education.

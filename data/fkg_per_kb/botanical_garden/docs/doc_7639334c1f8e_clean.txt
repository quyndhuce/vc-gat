The Botanical Garden in Hanoi, often referred to as "the green lung" of the city, is a verdant oasis that serves as a popular destination for both residents and tourists alike. Established by French colonizers in 1890, this historic garden has been a part of Hanoi's landscape for over a century, offering a serene retreat from the bustling urban environment. Spanning approximately 10 hectares today, the Botanical Garden is nestled at No. 1 Hoang Hoa Tham Street, Phuong Ngoc Ha, Ba Dinh District, Hanoi, and remains a significant landmark within the city.

The Botanical Garden is renowned for its lush greenery and diverse flora, featuring a vast array of plant species and ancient trees that have stood the test of time. The garden's expansive grounds include hills, lakes, and forests, creating a picturesque setting that invites visitors to explore and immerse themselves in nature. Among the highlights are the numerous rare and exotic plants, many of which have been cultivated and preserved here since the garden's inception. Additionally, the garden houses a variety of wildlife, including birds and monkeys, making it an ideal location for children to learn about biodiversity and conservation.

One of the most notable features of the Botanical Garden is Lake Vị Danh, a tranquil body of water that adds to the serene ambiance of the park. The lake, with its clear blue waters, provides a reflective surface that mirrors the surrounding greenery, enhancing the overall beauty of the landscape. A small bridge spans across the lake, allowing visitors to walk out onto the water and enjoy panoramic views of the garden.

The Botanical Garden opens early each morning at 5:30 AM, welcoming those who wish to start their day with a peaceful stroll or a gentle bike ride through the lush gardens. Throughout the day, the park remains open until 10 PM, providing ample opportunity for visitors to experience the changing atmosphere of the garden as the light shifts from dawn to dusk. This extended operating schedule caters to a wide range of activities, from morning yoga sessions to evening picnics under the stars.

Visitors can reach the Botanical Garden via various modes of transportation, including public buses, subway trains, private cars, motorbikes, or taxis. The garden's convenient location near the Mausoleum of Ho Chi Minh makes it easily accessible to both locals and tourists. Upon arrival, one is greeted by the lush greenery and the soothing sounds of nature, immediately transporting them away from the hustle and bustle of the city.

The Botanical Garden not only serves as a recreational space but also plays a crucial role in education and research. With over 12,000 different plant species, the garden acts as a living museum, showcasing the rich botanical heritage of Vietnam and beyond. Educational programs and guided tours are regularly organized to help visitors understand the importance of biodiversity and environmental conservation. These initiatives contribute to raising awareness about the significance of preserving natural habitats and promoting sustainable practices.

In summary, the Botanical Garden in Hanoi stands as a testament to the enduring value of green spaces in urban environments. Its rich history, diverse flora and fauna, and serene atmosphere make it a must-visit destination for anyone seeking a moment of tranquility amidst the vibrant life of Hanoi. Whether you're looking to unwind after a busy week, engage in outdoor activities, or simply appreciate the beauty of nature, the Botanical Garden offers a unique and enriching experience that resonates with visitors of all ages.

The Botanical Garden, also known as the Garden of a Hundred Plants, is a lush green oasis nestled in the heart of bustling Hanoi, Vietnam. This verdant sanctuary, fondly referred to as the "green lung" of the capital city, spans approximately 33 hectares and serves as a haven for both residents and tourists seeking respite from the urban hustle and bustle. Established during the early 20th century under French colonial rule, the Botanical Garden was initially conceived as a research and conservation site for various plant species. Over time, it has evolved into a beloved public park that continues to attract visitors with its rich biodiversity and serene atmosphere.

The Botanical Garden's history is deeply intertwined with the colonial era, reflecting the legacy of French influence in Vietnam. It was during this period that the garden was established with the primary purpose of studying and preserving different types of flora. As the years passed, the Botanical Garden underwent several transformations, expanding its scope beyond mere scientific research to become a recreational space for the general public. Today, it stands as a testament to the enduring importance of natural conservation and public leisure spaces.

Visitors can enter the Botanical Garden through its main gate located at number 3 on Hoang Hoa Tham Street in Ba Dinh District, or alternatively, through a secondary entrance situated at number 2 on Ngoc Ha Street. The garden's vast expanse is divided into distinct zones, each showcasing unique botanical collections and landscapes. These areas not only highlight the diversity of plant life but also offer a tranquil setting for relaxation and recreation.

One of the most notable features of the Botanical Garden is its extensive collection of rare and exotic plants. The garden houses hundreds of species, with two-thirds being native to Vietnam and the remaining third originating from other parts of the world such as Africa, America, and Oceania. Among the standout specimens are the Muong Sleep tree (or Cong tree), which hails from the Americas and boasts a massive trunk and sprawling canopy; the Ba Bap tree from Africa, known for its thick trunk designed to store water; the ancient Buddha Tree, representing numerous plant families and holding significant ecological and scientific value; and the towering Coconut Oil tree, one of the tallest in the garden, reaching heights of 40 to 70 meters.

In addition to these remarkable trees, the Botanical Garden offers a variety of scenic spots for visitors to explore and enjoy. The expansive green lawns dotted with shade-providing trees provide ideal locations for picnics, reading, and simply lounging amidst nature. Strategically placed benches allow visitors to rest while taking in the serene beauty of the surroundings. The tranquil ambiance of the garden is further enhanced by the presence of picturesque lakes, notably Lake Vien Danh, which adds a touch of serenity to the landscape. The lake's clear waters reflect the lush foliage, creating a soothing environment that invites visitors to pause and appreciate the peacefulness of the moment.

Another distinctive feature of the Botanical Garden is its artificial hills and mountains, which add a dynamic element to the landscape. One of the prominent landmarks within the garden is Mount Nun, where visitors can ascend to the summit for panoramic views of the surrounding area. The climb up Mount Nun is not only rewarding with its breathtaking vistas but also provides opportunities for physical activities like jogging and exercise. Additionally, the garden includes the revered Temple of Mount Su, an ancient shrine dating back to the 19th century. This temple, dedicated to the deity Huyen Thien Hac De, who played a crucial role in defending Vietnam against Cham invaders, is a testament to the region's rich cultural heritage. The temple's traditional architecture, characterized by curved roofs and sturdy wooden columns, creates a serene and dignified atmosphere that resonates with visitors seeking spiritual solace.

The Botanical Garden's significance extends beyond its botanical collections and scenic beauty. It serves as a vital cultural and educational resource, hosting various events and activities throughout the year. For instance, the annual festival at the Temple of Mount Su draws large crowds of locals and tourists alike, offering a glimpse into local traditions and beliefs. Moreover, the garden's diverse ecosystem supports a range of wildlife, contributing to its ecological importance and making it a valuable site for environmental education and awareness.

As a popular destination, the Botanical Garden often sees a mix of visitors, including schoolchildren exploring the wonders of nature, elderly individuals enjoying quiet walks, and tourists captivated by the lush scenery. The garden's multifaceted appeal ensures that there is something for everyone, whether it's the joy of discovery, the peace of solitude, or the thrill of adventure. Its enduring charm lies in its ability to blend historical significance with contemporary recreational value, making it a cherished part of Hanoi's urban fabric.

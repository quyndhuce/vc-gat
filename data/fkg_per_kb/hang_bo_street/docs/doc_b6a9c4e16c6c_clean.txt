Hang Bo Street is located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi. During the French colonial period at the end of the 19th century, it was known as Rue des Paniers. After 1945, the street officially adopted its current name, Hang Bo. Historically, this street was home to shops selling bamboo and rattan weaving tools such as baskets, crates, and winnows. During Tet (Lunar New Year), traditional goods would fill the street, bustling with buyers and sellers.

Previously, Hang Bo Street belonged to the Hang Bo ward in Hoan Kiem district, but now it is part of the Hoan Kiem ward in Hanoi city. The street stretches approximately 0.27 kilometers from east to west. At its eastern end, it intersects with Hang Dao, Hang Ngang, and Hang Bac streets. To the west, it meets Hang The, Thuoc Bac, and Bat Dan streets. It is situated 0.35 kilometers north of Hoan Kiem Lake and 0.5 kilometers south of Dong Xuan Market.

The area around Hang Bo Street originally comprised parts of the villages of Xuan Hoa (to the east) and Nhan Noi (to the west), both part of the former Tien Tuc district of Tho Xuan county. By mid-19th century, Xuan Hoa village merged with Yen Hoa village to form Xuan Yen village. The Tien Tuc district also changed its name to Thuong My district. Under French rule, Hang Bo Street was called "Rue des Paniers." After the revolution, Hanoi officially named it "Hang Bo." In the early 20th century, many Chinese immigrants from Quanzhou settled in this area, where they weighed rice and fruits, and some opened photography studios.

Traces of the old villages can still be found in the form of remaining temples and shrines: the Xuan Yen Temple (formerly of Xuan Hoa village) at number 44 Hang Can Street worships Princess Lan Ngoc, another Xuan Yen Temple (formerly of Yen Hoa village) at number 6 Lương Văn Can Street worships Nguyen Quan Phu’s wife, and the Nhan Noi Temple at number 84A Hang Bo Street also worships Princess Lan Ngoc. Additionally, the Nhan Noi Communal House at number 33 Bat Dan Street honors the White Horse God. Until the beginning of the French colonial era, the middle section of Hang Bo Street was where shops selling woven baskets and other items made of bamboo and rattan were concentrated. The eastern part of the street, near Hang Dao and Hang Ngang streets, was formerly known as Hang Dep Street, where shops sold wooden clogs and sandals.

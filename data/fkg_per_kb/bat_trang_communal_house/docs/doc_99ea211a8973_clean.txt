The Bat Trang Communal House is one of the largest communal houses in the ancient region of Kinh Bac. Located in the village of Bat Trang, in Bat Trang commune, Gia Lam district, Hanoi city, the Bat Trang Communal House venerates six deities who have contributed to helping the people fight against enemies and protect the country. These include Liu Tianzi Great King, Bai Ma Great King, Trang Xuan Nghi Dung, Fan Great General, Cai Minh Great King, and Ho Quoc Shen Great King.

Constructed in 1720 during the reign of King Le Duc Tong, the Bat Trang Communal House was built in the shape of the Chinese character "Nhị". The interior consists of a rear hall with three rooms, while the exterior features a main hall with five rooms and two wings. The columns of the communal house are made from large lim trees, and each side room has wooden steps laid out in tiers as seating areas. The communal house faces the Nhị River.

Currently, the Bat Trang Communal House preserves over 50 imperial decrees for the tutelary deity, issued during the reigns of Kings Le Canh Hung, Nguyen Quang Trung, and Nguyen Canh Thinh.

Located within the complex of ancient pottery village relics in Hanoi, the Bat Trang Communal House was built in 1720. With its grand architectural style, it faces west towards the Red River. The architecture follows the "Nhị" style, with the rear hall housing the worship of the Six Deities, who are revered as the Six Tutelary Deities. In front is the main hall with five rooms and two wings, supported by many large lim tree columns that require multiple arms to encircle. At the center of the main hall is the communal altar, where two large golden-lettered plaques hang: "Thien dia hop qi de" (Heaven and Earth Unite Their Virtue), which reflects the first hexagram in the Book of Changes. This phrase also represents the guiding principle of the Bat Trang villagers: always prioritizing virtue in life, ensuring everything proceeds smoothly and favorably. Another plaque reads "Hieu nghia gap cong" (Loving Righteousness and Urging Public Service), which commemorates the villagers' contribution when the Nguyen dynasty built Hanoi, as they donated bricks from their ancestral courtyard to the court. On either side of the altar are couplets inscribed with the origins of the Bat Trang villagers: "Bo di thu nghe khai dinh vu - Lan net tam huong bai thanh than" (Bringing skills from the Bo village to build communal houses, hearts like orchid incense paying homage to the divine). The left and right wings house the altars for the Left and Right Walls, where those in the village without descendants are worshipped. This practice reflects the cultural value of filial piety among the Bat Trang villagers. The lowest step and courtyard of the communal house are paved with Bat Trang tiles, known for their durability and resistance to moss, and used extensively from palaces to villages. The four sloping roofs are adorned with ornamental dragons, soft yet powerful and solemn. Above the main entrance hangs a plaque with the words "Bach tho danh son" (White Earth Famous Mountain), evoking the early days of the white clay area (now Bat Trang) when the Nguyen Ninh Trang clan followed King Ly Thai To to move the capital from Hoa Lu to Dai La, producing the imperishable Vinh Ninh tiles for the construction of the new capital.

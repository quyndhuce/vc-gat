Artifact, a term with diverse applications across various fields, refers to different concepts depending on the context. In science and technology, it can denote a misleading alteration in data or observation due to flaws in technique or equipment, often seen in experimental science. This includes compression artifacts, which are losses of clarity caused by compressing images, audio, or video, as well as digital artifacts, which are unintended alterations introduced during digital processing. Sonic artifacts in sound and music production are unwanted sounds that arise from the editing process, while visual artifacts are anomalies in digital graphics and imagery. In the realm of software project management, artifacts in Scrum refer to documentation used for managing projects.

In archaeology, an artifact is an object crafted by humans, significant to archaeological study. The term also applies to "The Artefact," an annual journal published by the Archaeological and Anthropological Society of Victoria. In computing, artifacts encompass a range of by-products generated during software development, components of enterprise architecture, virtual objects in digital environments, and specific terms within the Unified Modeling Language (UML). Additionally, "Artifact" is the name of a news recommendation application available on iOS and Android platforms.

From a cultural studies perspective, an artifact is anything human-made that provides insight into the culture of its creators and users. In arts and media, the term takes on several forms. For instance, in film and television, "Artifact" can refer to a 2012 documentary directed by Jared Leto under a pseudonym, a 2007 horror film, and a fictional object featured in the TV series "Eureka." In gaming, artifacts are the most potent magic items in Dungeons & Dragons and a card type in Magic: The Gathering, as well as a 2018 digital collectible card game by Valve.

Music also features several references to artifacts, including albums by The Electric Prunes, Beirut, Nicole Mitchell, Steve Roach, and Swedish electronic musician Waveshaper, as well as songs like "Artefact" by Phoenix and "Artifact" by Taylor Swift. Beyond these, there are artifacts in ballet, such as a 1984 piece by William Forsythe, and in literature, like Gregory Benford's 1985 science fiction novel "Artifact."

Geographically, Artifact Creek and Artifact Ridge are notable locations in British Columbia, Canada. Historically, artifacting was a technique used on older computers to generate color in monochrome modes by exploiting television system artifacts. In educational contexts, learning artifacts are objects created by students during their coursework. Lastly, in religious contexts, an artifact can be a relic, an object left behind by a prophet or significant religious figure.

These varied usages highlight the multifaceted nature of the term "Artifact," reflecting its significance across multiple disciplines and cultural spheres.

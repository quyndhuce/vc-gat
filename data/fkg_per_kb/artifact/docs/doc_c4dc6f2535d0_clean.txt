The Vietnam National Museum of Ethnology, located at Nguyen Van Hieu Street in Cau Giay District, Hanoi, is a significant cultural landmark that houses a vast collection of artifacts and documents reflecting the diverse lifestyles, cultures, and traditions of the 54 ethnic groups in Vietnam. Established towards the end of 1987 and officially inaugurated in 1997, this museum spans over 4 hectares of land, making it a substantial repository of Vietnamese ethnic heritage. The architectural design of the museum was a collaborative effort between architect Ha Duc Linh, a Tay ethnic group member, and French architect Veronique Dollfus, resulting in a structure that harmoniously blends modern aesthetics with traditional elements.

The museum's layout is divided into three main exhibition areas: the Trong Dong Hall, outdoor exhibits, and the Southeast Asia section. The Trong Dong Hall, named after the ancient bronze drum symbolizing unity among ethnic groups, occupies an area of 2,500 square meters and is spread across two floors. It is meticulously organized into nine thematic sections, each dedicated to different ethnic groups and their unique characteristics. These include the Viet-Muong, Thai-Kadai, Mon-Khmer, Han-Tibetan, and other minority groups such as Cham, Hmong, Dao, and more. Each section provides visitors with a comprehensive understanding of the linguistic, cultural, and social aspects of these communities, offering insights into their daily lives, customs, and traditions.

Outside the main hall, the museum showcases a variety of traditional dwellings representative of various ethnic groups. These structures include the Ede and Gie-Trin longhouses, Tay stilt houses, Mong wooden-roofed homes, Kinh tile-roofed houses, Gia Rai earthen houses, and Bana bamboo houses. Each dwelling is constructed using traditional materials and techniques, providing a vivid depiction of the architectural styles and living conditions of different ethnic groups. This outdoor exhibit not only serves as a visual representation but also allows visitors to immerse themselves in the authentic environments of these communities, enhancing their understanding and appreciation of the diverse architectural heritage of Vietnam.

The Southeast Asia section of the museum offers a broader perspective by presenting a general overview of the cultural diversity found in Southeast Asia. This area is divided into five themes: textiles, daily life, social life, performing arts, and religion-beliefs. Through a wide array of artifacts, photographs, films, and audio recordings, visitors can explore the rich tapestry of Southeast Asian cultures, gaining insight into the intricate details of daily life, social interactions, artistic expressions, and spiritual practices.

With its extensive collection of over 15,000 artifacts, 42,000 color photos, 2,190 film reels, 273 audio recordings, 373 videos, and 25 CD-ROMs, the museum stands as a testament to the richness and complexity of Vietnamese ethnic cultures. It serves as a vital resource for both tourists and researchers, providing a platform for experiencing and studying the cultural heritage of Vietnam and beyond. Regularly hosting demonstrations of traditional crafts and cultural performances, the museum brings to life the vibrant traditions of various ethnic groups, fostering a deeper connection between visitors and the cultural narratives of Vietnam.

Recognized by the reputable travel website TripAdvisor, the Vietnam National Museum of Ethnology was ranked fourth among the top 25 museums in Asia in 2014, highlighting its significance as a destination for cultural exploration and education. The museum's commitment to preserving and showcasing the multifaceted cultural heritage of Vietnam makes it an essential stop for anyone interested in understanding the country's ethnic diversity and cultural richness.

Hang Giay Street is located within the Old Quarter of Hanoi, specifically in Hoan Kiem District, stretching from Hang Chieu Street to Luong Ngoc Quyen Street (formerly known as Hang Man Street). The street is 228 meters long.

Historically, Hang Giay Street was part of the ancient village of Co Luong, which belonged to Dong Xuan Commune in the former Tho Xuong district. This village was situated on the northern bank of the To Lich River. Crossing the To River at Ngoc Gach Alley would lead to Ha Khau ward. From there, moving towards Hai Tuong village marked the beginning of Hang Giay Street. Today, it falls under Hang Boom ward in Hoan Kiem District.

Originally, Hang Giay Street was home to shoemakers who were originally from Cham Village (Truc Lam, Van Lam, Phong Lam) in Gia Loc district, Hai Duong province. They established a temple dedicated to their leather craft guild at what is now number 16 Hai Tuong Alley. Until the late 19th century, around the mid-point of Hang Giay Street, there was a temple at number 30, which was initially the Inner Temple of Hai Tuong village. In 1895, residents of Hang Bac Street, whose origins were from Trau Khe village, purchased this temple, renovated it, and turned it into a place to honor the guardian deity of Trau Khe village. Consequently, the temple became associated with Hang Bac Street rather than Hang Giay Street.

During the French colonial period, the northern section of the street (formerly Hang Man Street) was called Rue Lataste. The southern section (Hang Giay Street) was named after Nguyen Duy Han, the governor of Thai Binh province, who was killed by Pham Van Trang, a member of the Vietnam Restoration Society, with a grenade in Thai Binh city on April 12, 1913. After the liberation of the capital, the official name of the street was restored to Hang Giay Street.

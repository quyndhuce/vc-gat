Hang Giay Street, nestled at the bustling intersection of Ngo Gach, is a culinary gem that has evolved from a once-thriving hub of traditional shoe-making artisans to a vibrant food scene. This charming street, situated in the Thirty-Six Old Quarter, has a rich history dating back to the time when artisans from Hai Duong province journeyed to the imperial capital to establish their livelihoods. The rhythmic clatter of shoe soles, which once filled the air, gave birth to the name Hang Giay, preserving the memory of a glorious handicraft era.

As the shoe workshops gradually faded, the street transformed into a culinary haven, with the aroma of sizzling street food wafting through the air. Today, tourists and locals alike flock to Hang Giay Street, gathering around plastic chairs by the roadside to savor a refined bowl of bun thang or enjoy a hot banh troi tau on a chilly winter morning. The experience of inhaling the simple kitchen smoke amidst the moss-covered street corner brings a profound sense of peace, helping everyone understand the culinary essence and eternal vitality of the thousand-year-old Thang Long.

The street's unique visual features include a series of food display cabinets and bubbling broth pots placed tightly together, spilling out onto the sidewalk. The narrow street stretches long, sandwiched between two rows of tube houses leaning closely together, with the white hue from kitchen smoke blending with patches of yellowed, moss-covered walls. The tangled mess of electrical wires above adds to the street's charm, as does the constant flow of people and vehicles moving back and forth along the busy roadway.

Hang Giay Street is a testament to the cultural heritage of Vietnam, where tradition and innovation blend seamlessly. As a culinary destination, it offers a glimpse into the country's rich gastronomic history, with each dish and every bite telling a story of the past. Whether you're a food enthusiast or simply looking for a unique cultural experience, Hang Giay Street is a must-visit destination in the heart of Hanoi's Old Quarter.

As the sun casts its warm rays upon the tranquil Hoan Kiem Lake, the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre, situated on Dinh Tien Hoang Street, comes alive with the enchanting performance of "Nhi đồng hí thủy". This iconic landmark, born in 1969, has been a proud testament to the enduring vitality of Vietnam's wet rice civilization, as embodied by the name Thang Long, which dates back to the era of King Ly Thai To's capital relocation. The theatre's rustic, hanging bamboo screen, nestled beside the lake's sacred waters, provides a picturesque backdrop for the mesmerizing spectacle.

As the melodious sounds of the đàn bầu blend with the clacking rhythm, the auditorium transforms into a riverside wharf, bustling with the breath of a countryside summer. The small, plump figurines of swimming children, with their rosy skin tone from the glossy lacquer on the wood core, instantly draw the gaze. Their signature feature is the sudden diving and surfacing that splashes white foam, creating a lively scene of frolicking that stands out on the rippling lake surface.

This performance is not only a masterclass in puppetry control but also a magical ferry ride, transporting the audience back to innocent memories in the heart of the bustling city. As the water surface gradually becomes still, what remains deep within everyone's consciousness is a strangely peaceful feeling and a poignant nostalgia for childhood. The performance is a testament to the enduring power of art to evoke emotions and transport us to a bygone era, as the audience is transported to a world of simplicity and joy.

The Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre, with its rich cultural heritage and historical significance, is a must-visit destination for anyone interested in immersing themselves in Vietnam's cultural identity. As the performance comes to a close, the audience is left with a sense of wonder and awe, carrying with them the memories of a magical experience that will stay with them long after the curtain falls.

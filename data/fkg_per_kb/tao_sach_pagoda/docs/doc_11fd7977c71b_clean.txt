Tao Sach Pagoda, also known as Chùa Tảo Sách, is a serene Buddhist sanctuary nestled peacefully by the vast, wavy waters of Tây Hồ, a tranquil haven that has been a source of spiritual solace for centuries. This ancient pagoda, built on the former residence of the revered Prince Linh Lang during the Mạc Dynasty in the 16th century, exudes an air of profound tranquility and absolute peace. The name Tảo Sách evokes a poetic quality, linked to an ancient legend that this place was once where the prince and literati conversed, read books, and recited poetry every morning.

As one approaches the pagoda, the majestic cổng tam quan, with its two-story soaring architecture, comes into view. The deep brown color of the gỗ lim blended with moss-covered ngói mũi hài is striking, and the unique identifying feature is the majestic lầu chuông proudly situated on the second floor of the gate arch. This sturdy architectural cluster, sheltered by ancient trees, faces directly out to the vast adjacent body of water, creating a sense of harmony and balance.

The pagoda's massive three-entrance gate, built from dark lim wood, is adorned with moss-covered curved tiles, adding to its serene ambiance. The gate's architecture is a testament to the ingenuity and craftsmanship of the artisans who built it, and its majestic bell tower on the second floor is a symbol of the pagoda's spiritual significance.

Today, Tao Sach Pagoda remains a popular destination for locals and distant visitors alike, who come to light spiritual incense, calm their minds by chanting sutras under the shade of the bồ đề tree, or get lost in admiring the sunset casting gold upon the waves. On full moon days or during the Vu Lan Festival, the space bursts with vibrant colors, yet retains its solemn reverence. Standing silently, feeling the breeze caress one's shoulders, the soul suddenly feels light, and the worries and troubles of the world seem to fade away. This peaceful haven, nestled by the lake, is a true reflection of the serenity and tranquility that can be found within its walls.

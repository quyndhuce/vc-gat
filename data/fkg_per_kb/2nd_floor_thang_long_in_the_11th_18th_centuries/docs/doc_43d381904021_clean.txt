As you step into the "Thăng Long 11th–18th centuries" exhibition area on the 2nd floor of the Hanoi Museum, you are transported to a bygone era, when the ancient capital of Thăng Long, under the Lý, Trần, Lê, and Mạc dynasties, flourished as a hub of politics, culture, and commerce. The exhibition space, with its large glass display cases, showcases an array of archaeological artifacts, including ceramics, ancient coins, royal relics, and scale models of imperial citadel architecture, evoking the majesty of the royal court and the vibrancy of folk life.

The deep yellow and wood-brown tones, accompanied by focused lighting, create an atmosphere of antiquity and solemnity, as if the very essence of the past has been distilled within these walls. The most striking feature, however, is the scale models reconstructing the Hoàng thành Thăng Long, alongside exquisite artifacts from the Lý – Trần dynasties, which transport visitors to a time when the city was a bustling metropolis, with the sound of horse hooves, market sessions, and palace lights casting their reflections on the Red River.

As you wander through the exhibition, you are surrounded by ancient maps, illustrations of life in the imperial capital, and a stream of visitors slowly observing, as if they are walking amidst the rhythm of life of the ancient capital. The stories of imperial examinations, handicrafts, bustling trade streets, and historical upheavals, such as the three resistance wars against the Yuan-Mongol invaders, are woven together to create a rich tapestry of a capital that constantly transformed over centuries.

The exhibition is a testament to the city's enduring cultural significance, as it not only tells historical stories but also evokes a sense of touching very closely a Hanoi that was once brilliant behind the dust of time. As you leave, you carry with you the feeling of having just stepped through a golden slice of the nation's memory, a sense of connection to the past that is both poignant and profound.

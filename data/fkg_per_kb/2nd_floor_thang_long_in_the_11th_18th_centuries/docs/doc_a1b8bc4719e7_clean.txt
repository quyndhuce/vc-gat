The second floor of the Hanoi Museum houses an exhibition area titled "Thăng Long 11th–18th Century," which serves as a gateway to one of the most illustrious periods in the ancient capital's history. This section of the museum traces the evolution of Thăng Long, initially a newly chosen site under the Lý Dynasty, into a significant political, cultural, and commercial hub of Vietnam during the reigns of the Lý, Trần, Lê, and Mạc dynasties. As visitors step into this space, they are immersed in the vibrant atmosphere of the ancient capital, where the echoes of horse hooves, bustling markets, and the flickering lights of the imperial palaces once resonated along the banks of the Red River.

The exhibition area is replete with archaeological artifacts, architectural models of palaces, ceramics, ancient coins, and remnants of citadels, all of which narrate the grandeur and vibrancy of Thăng Long. These exhibits include detailed models of the Imperial Citadel, showcasing the architectural splendor of the Lý and Trần dynasties, alongside exquisite ceramics and ancient coins that reflect the economic prosperity and cultural richness of the era. The displays also incorporate stories about the imperial examinations, traditional crafts, bustling trade streets, and historical events such as the three resistance wars against the Yuan-Mongol invaders, providing a comprehensive view of the capital's dynamic transformation over the centuries.

Visitors often find themselves captivated by the scale models reconstructing the Hoàng Thành Thăng Long, the imperial citadel, which stand as testament to the architectural prowess and grandeur of the time. These models, along with the intricate artifacts from the Lý and Trần periods, evoke a profound sense of connection to a past that is both majestic and intimately familiar. The exhibition space is characterized by large glass display cases filled with these treasures, set against a backdrop of deep yellow and wood-brown tones, enhanced by focused lighting that imbues the entire area with a sense of antiquity and solemnity.

The cultural significance of this exhibit extends beyond mere historical documentation; it serves as a poignant reminder of the enduring legacy of Thăng Long, now known as Hanoi. The exhibition not only educates visitors about the historical origins and development of the city but also underscores its role as a center of political power, cultural innovation, and economic activity. As visitors meander through the halls, they are transported back in time, experiencing the essence of a capital that thrived during the 11th to 18th centuries, a period marked by both stability and upheaval, prosperity and conflict. The exhibition thus stands as a vital link between the past and present, offering a glimpse into the golden age of Thăng Long and its enduring impact on modern-day Hanoi.

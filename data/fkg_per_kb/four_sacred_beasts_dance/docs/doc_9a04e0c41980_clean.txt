Welcome to the most sacred and magnificent moment on the water stage, the Múa Tứ Linh performance at the Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre, proudly situated on the tree-lined Đinh Tiên Hoàng Street, embracing the deep blue waters of Hoàn Kiếm Lake. Opened in 1969, this theatre stands as a testament to the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam, its majestic name, Thăng Long, commemorating the relocation of the capital by King Lý Thái Tổ, who moved the seat of power to this strategic location in the early 11th century. The name Thăng Long itself evokes the legendary moment when a golden dragon soared into the sky, symbolizing the ascension of the city to a new era of prosperity and cultural significance.

The theatre’s location, adjacent to the historic Hoàn Kiếm Lake, underscores its importance within the heart of Hanoi, a city steeped in ancient traditions and modern vibrancy. As visitors approach the theatre, they are greeted by the serene beauty of the lake, framed by the lush greenery of the surrounding area, which includes the iconic Ngoc Son Temple and the bustling streets of the Old Quarter. The theatre’s architecture, though modern, pays homage to traditional Vietnamese design, integrating elements that reflect the country’s long-standing cultural identity.

The Múa Tứ Linch performance is a mesmerizing spectacle that brings together the mythical creatures of Long (Dragon), Lân (Qilin), Quy (Tortoise), and Phượng (Phoenix) in a harmonious dance that transcends time and space. These creatures, each embodying different virtues and cosmic energies, are meticulously crafted from wood, their surfaces adorned with glossy lacquer that gleams in vibrant reds and yellows, creating a striking visual impact against the backdrop of the shimmering lake. The performance not only showcases the masterful puppetry skills of the performers but also serves as a spiritual ritual, invoking a sense of awe and reverence among both locals and tourists.

As the performance begins, the rapid beats of festival drums and the majestic rhythms of horns fill the air, setting the stage for a grand celebration. The water surface, initially agitated by the movements of the puppets, gradually calms, allowing the audience to focus on the intricate choreography and the symbolic gestures of the creatures. A highlight of the performance is the moment when Long, Lân, Quy, and Phượng simultaneously spray water bubbles and release mystical smoke, creating a breathtaking spectacle that seems to bridge the earthly realm with the divine.

This performance is more than just entertainment; it is a profound expression of Vietnamese culture, reflecting the nation’s deep-rooted beliefs in harmony between heaven and earth, and the enduring spirit of the wet rice civilization. It is a testament to the resilience and continuity of Vietnamese traditions, offering visitors a unique glimpse into the soul of the country. The Múa Tứ Linh performance at the Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre thus stands as a beacon of cultural preservation and a source of pride for the people of Hanoi, reminding them of their rich heritage and the timeless values that have shaped their society.

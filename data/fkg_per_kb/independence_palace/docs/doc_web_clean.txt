Independence Palace (Dinh Doc Lap), also officially named Reunification Hall (Hoi truong Thong Nhat), is a landmark historical monument located on Nam Ky Khoi Nghia Street in District 1, Ho Chi Minh City, surrounded by a vast shaded park of ancient tropical trees and expansive front lawns with a circular fountain. Its precursor was Norodom Palace, built by French colonial authorities between 1868 and 1871 as the residence of the Governor of Cochinchina. After a 1962 aerial bombing severely damaged the left wing, the old colonial structure was demolished and a new modernist palace was designed by Vietnamese architect Ngo Viet Thu, the first Asian laureate of the Grand Prix de Rome. Construction began in July 1962 and the building was inaugurated on October 31, 1966. Ngo Viet Thu integrated 1960s tropical modernist architecture with traditional East Asian philosophy: the ground plan resembles the character Cat symbolizing good fortune, vertical concrete sun-shading louvers across the white facade evoke stylized bamboo stalks, and the rooftop pavilion combined with the central flagpole forms the character Trung symbolizing loyalty. Inside are state banquet and presidential reception halls with lacquer paintings, underground war command rooms with vintage maps and telecommunications equipment, and a rooftop helipad where a historic UH-1 helicopter sits on display. Historic tanks number 843 and 390 crashed through the wrought-iron entrance gates on April 30, 1975, marking the end of the Vietnam War and national reunification.

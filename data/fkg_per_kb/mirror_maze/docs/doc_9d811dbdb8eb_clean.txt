The Mirror Maze, nestled near the main entrance of Thong Nhat Park on Tran Nhan Tong Street in Hanoi, is a landmark that has been a cherished part of the city's cultural heritage for decades. Originally constructed by the Czechoslovak government as a gift to Vietnam in 1979, this unique structure was once a beloved destination for generations of Hanoians, particularly those born in the 1970s and 1980s. Over time, however, the Mirror Maze fell into disrepair, its once vibrant interior marred by peeling paint, damp walls, and cracked mirrors. The neglect was palpable, and it seemed as though the Mirror Maze might fade into obscurity.

In response to this decline, the city of Hanoi initiated a comprehensive renovation project aimed at revitalizing the Mirror Maze and transforming it into a modern tourist attraction while preserving its historical significance. This ambitious endeavor was entrusted to a trio of talented artists—Nguyen Thu Thuy, Nguyen Hoang Tung, and Bui Viet Doan—alongside the Artistic New Hanoi Company. Their vision was to create a new space that would not only entertain visitors but also serve as a testament to Vietnamese culture and history.

The restoration process began in earnest, with meticulous attention paid to every detail. The Mirror Maze now boasts a stunning façade adorned with intricate ceramic tiles, each piece carefully selected and arranged to form a cohesive artistic masterpiece. Inside, the maze itself remains a labyrinth of mirrors, designed to challenge visitors and induce laughter as they navigate through the reflective corridors. The floors have been replaced with colorful ceramic tiles, adding a vibrant touch to the overall aesthetic.

One of the most striking features of the renovated Mirror Maze is the central room, which houses eight distorted mirrors imported from Czechoslovakia. These mirrors continue to delight visitors, causing them to giggle uncontrollably as they encounter their warped reflections. Surrounding these mirrors is a mesmerizing array of ceramic art, depicting scenes from Vietnam’s natural wonders, including the majestic Halong Bay and the vast expanse of the South China Sea. Each tile, handcrafted and meticulously placed, tells a story of the country’s rich maritime heritage and the importance of protecting its territorial waters.

The Mirror Maze also retains several original elements from its inception, serving as a bridge between past and present. Among these are three statues gifted by Czechoslovakia upon completion of the building in 1979. One of these statues, the Sea Goddess touching her anchor, stands prominently at the entrance, a symbol of the enduring connection between the two nations. The renovation project has not only restored the Mirror Maze to its former glory but has also elevated it to a new level of cultural significance. This recognition underscores the Mirror Maze’s role as a unique cultural asset and a must-visit destination for tourists and locals alike.

As the Mirror Maze prepares to welcome visitors once again, it promises to offer a blend of entertainment and education. The immersive experience within the maze invites visitors to lose themselves in a world of reflection and wonder, while the ceramic murals provide a visual journey through Vietnam’s coastal regions. Over time, however, the Mirror Maze began to show signs of wear and tear, reflecting the passage of decades since its inception. Despite these challenges, the Mirror Maze remained a nostalgic symbol for many Hanoians born in the 70s and 80s, who fondly recall their visits as a highlight of their youth. For younger generations, the Mirror Maze was less familiar, overshadowed by newer and more modern attractions that emerged over the years.

Source: Ho Chi Minh City Tourism & Heritage Architectural Guides
The Saigon Central Post Office (Buu dien Trung tam Sai Gon) stands on Cong Xa Paris Square in District 1, directly across from Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. Following an earlier 1860s Saigon Wire Department building, construction of the current grand post office headquarters began in 1886 and was inaugurated in 1891. Designed by French architects Auguste Henri Villedieu and Marie-Alfred Foulhoux (often popularly misattributed to Gustave Eiffel due to its exposed metal trusses), the building blends Gothic, Renaissance, and French colonial architectural styles with subtle Eastern decorative details. The symmetrical yellow exterior features arched windows, white ornamental pilasters, decorative relief plaques inscribed with inventors of electricity and telegraphy, and a large Parisian circular facade clock above the central entrance. Inside, the soaring barrel-vaulted ceiling is supported by green-painted iron arches and wrought-iron pillars reminiscent of a nineteenth-century European railway station. Two large historical maps hang on the interior entrance walls: 'Lignes telegraphiques du Sud Vietnam et du Cambodge (1892)' and 'Saigon et ses environs (1892)'. The main hall preserves antique wooden telephone booths, classic wooden service counters, patterned tiled floors, and a prominent portrait of President Ho Chi Minh at the far end.

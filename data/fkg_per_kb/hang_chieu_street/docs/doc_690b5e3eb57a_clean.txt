Hang Chieu Street is a small street located within the ancient quarter of Hanoi, situated in the Hoan Kiem district of Hanoi city. The street stretches for 275 meters within Dong Xuan ward, Hoan Kiem district, Hanoi city. It connects from the Quan Chau gate to the intersection of Hang Duong and Dong Xuan streets. Three streets branch off from the Quan Chau gate towards Hang Chieu Street: Quan Chau, Thanh Ha, and Dao Duy Tu. At the opposite end, it intersects with Hang Duong, Dong Xuan, and Hang Ma streets. Additionally, Hang Chieu Street runs through Nguyen Thien Thuat and Hang Day streets, and there is an alley that leads to Dong Xuan, connecting to Cau Dong street. The street has a width of 11 meters and is bustling with various goods.

Historically, Hang Chieu Street was built upon the old Thanh Ha village of Tho Xuong county. In the past, this area sold many mats made of rush and also included bowls, hence another name for the street was Hang Bat (Street of Bowls). The Thanh Ha communal house, which once stood near the Quan Chau gate, was moved to number 77 Hang Chieu Street in 1817 due to the expansion of the gate, but its main entrance faces number 10 Gach alley. This communal house honors Tran Luu, a general from the Tran dynasty.

During the French colonial period, the street was known as Rue Jean Dupuis and was primarily used for selling weapons and ammunition to the French army at the beginning of the street. It was here that Francis Garnier advanced into the lower city of Hanoi under General Hoang Duy. Hang Chieu Street was the first street the colonizers planned to expand after capturing Hanoi in 1883. In 1888, the colonists set fire to the entire row of houses on the street. Afterward, they seized land to open shops, forcing residents to buy Western-style bricks if they lacked funds, purchasing them at low prices otherwise. The street featured sidewalks, trees, and streetlights similar to those in France, making it the first street in Hanoi with Western architecture, hence earning the nickname New Street. During the anti-French resistance war, Hang Chieu Street served as a battleground for the troops. It remained steadfast until February 17, 1947, when the Capital Regiment withdrew from the city.

Today, Hang Chieu Street mainly sells bedding items such as quilts, mats, and pillows.

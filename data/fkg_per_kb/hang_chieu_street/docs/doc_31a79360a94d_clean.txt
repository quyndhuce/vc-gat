Hang Chieu Street, also known as Phố Hàng Chiếu, is a captivating thoroughfare nestled in the heart of the thirty-six streets and guilds area of the ancient Thăng Long imperial city. This historic street, originally situated next to the Eastern gateway of the imperial city, has been a bustling commercial hub for centuries. The name Hang Chieu is derived from its former role as a stop for merchants carrying sedge mats from distant craft villages, who would make their way upstream on the Hồng Hà River to trade.

As one walks down Hang Chieu Street, the faded yellow of the brick walls blending with the rustic color of the rolls of chiếu and tarpaulin mats stacked closely together immediately catches the eye. The unique identifying feature of this street is the moss-covered gray brick arch gate of Ô Quan Chưởng, standing majestically as a blockade at the very end of the street. This bustling scene is nestled amidst the hurried traffic, right next to the crowded sidewalk always packed with goods and a constant stream of people coming and going.

Throughout its history, Hang Chieu Street has been a hub of commercial activity, with locals and travelers alike hurrying to and fro, eager to shop for rolls of packaging and closely packed blinds. As night falls, the street takes on a new persona, with a lively stream of people gathering around bowls of steaming mì vằn thắn or famous warm chè sắn from the sidewalk. The pungent smell of sedge mats mixed with the flavors of late-night cuisine brings a familiar feeling, helping each person understand the enduring vitality and the everlasting prosperity of this thousand-year-old marketplace.

Hang Chieu Street is a testament to the enduring legacy of the ancient Thăng Long imperial city, where the rhythms of commerce and daily life have been woven together for centuries. As one stands silently watching the traffic pass by under the dim yellow lights, an indescribable nostalgia rises in one's heart, a sense of connection to the past that is palpable and real.

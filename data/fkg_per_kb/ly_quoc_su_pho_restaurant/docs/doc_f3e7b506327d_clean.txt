In the heart of Hanoi's Old Quarter, a culinary icon stands out amidst the bustling streets: Ly Quoc Su Pho Restaurant, a name that echoes the spirit of the imperial capital. This esteemed eatery, situated on the eponymous street, has been a staple of traditional Northern Pho flavor since the subsidy period. The name Ly Quoc Su honors the renowned Zen master and physician of the Ly dynasty, Nguyen Minh Khong, whose legacy is woven into the fabric of Vietnamese culture.

As you step into the restaurant, you're immediately struck by the vibrant orange signboard and the stainless steel preparation counter, a beacon of culinary excellence. The pièce de résistance is the giant nước lèo pot, billowing with thick white smoke, situated next to a glass display case filled with hanging pinkish beef. This sensory experience is nestled among rows of closely packed old houses, adjacent to a ceaseless flow of people and vehicles.

The restaurant's history dates back to the subsidy period, when it was a state-owned trading store. Over the years, Ly Quoc Su Pho has evolved into a golden guarantee for authentic Northern Pho, its flavors and aromas transporting diners back in time to the imperial era. Whether you visit at dawn or dusk, locals and tourists alike gather around cozy tables, eager to savor the steaming bowls of Pho. As you sip the sweet, clear broth and bite into the tender beef, you'll feel the cultural heritage of Vietnam coursing through your veins.

Ly Quoc Su Pho Restaurant is more than just a culinary destination – it's an experience that connects kindred spirits, a testament to the city's rich cultural heritage. As you leave, you'll carry with you the memories of a bygone era, the essence of traditional Vietnamese cuisine, and the warmth of the city's vibrant Old Quarter.

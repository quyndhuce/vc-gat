Nestled along the picturesque Dinh Tien Hoang Street, adjacent to the revered Hoan Kiem Lake, stands the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre, a cultural gem established in 1969. This enchanting venue not only preserves the ancient art of water puppetry but also embodies the rich heritage of Vietnam’s wet rice civilization. The theatre’s name, Thang Long, evokes the legendary tale of King Ly Thai To, who moved the capital to Thang Long, now known as Hanoi, after witnessing a celestial dragon soaring overhead—a symbol of divine approval and prosperity.

The performances at the Thang Long Theatre are a mesmerizing blend of traditional puppetry and live music, featuring the distinctive sounds of the đàn bầu and sáo trúc, which fill the auditorium with a festive atmosphere. The stage itself is a tranquil pond where intricately crafted wooden puppets, painted in vibrant vermilion red and glossy black, glide gracefully through the water. These puppets, made of durable wood and lacquered to perfection, are manipulated by skilled artisans hidden behind a rustic bamboo screen, adding an element of mystery and charm to the show.

The theatre’s interior is designed to enhance the viewing experience, with soft lighting that highlights the shimmering water and the delicate movements of the puppets. Traditional musical instruments are carefully arranged on either side of the stage, their presence enriching the performance with a harmonious blend of sound and sight. As the performance unfolds, the audience is captivated by the antics of characters like Chú Tễu, a mischievous figure that often elicits laughter and joy, and the dynamic movements of the dragons, which spray water in dramatic displays.

Each performance at the Thang Long Theatre is more than just an entertainment spectacle; it is a profound cultural experience that connects visitors to the heart of Vietnamese tradition. The theatre serves as a vital link between past and present, offering a glimpse into the rural life and folklore that have shaped Vietnamese identity. For tourists and locals alike, attending a water puppetry performance is a cherished ritual that fosters a deep sense of nostalgia and national pride, reminding them of the enduring legacy of their ancestors and the timeless beauty of their homeland.

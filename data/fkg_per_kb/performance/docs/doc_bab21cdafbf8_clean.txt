As you step into the enchanting Thang Long Theater, nestled on Dinh Tien Hoang Street, adjacent to the revered Hoan Kiem Lake, you are transported to a realm of mesmerizing water puppetry performances. Established in 1969, this artistic sanctuary has been carrying on the traditional theatrical art form, proudly bearing the name Thang Long, which echoes the legendary tale of King Ly Thai To's relocation of the capital, testifying to the enduring vitality of Vietnam's wet rice civilization. The rustic bamboo screen, suspended mysteriously, conceals the artisans as they skillfully manipulate the wooden puppets, their vermilion red and glossy lacquer glistening in the soft light.

As the performance begins, the auditorium comes alive with the vibrant rhythm of the đàn bầu and sáo trúc, transporting the audience to a festive celebration. The water puppetry, a unique blend of music, dance, and storytelling, weaves a spellbinding narrative, as the puppets, including the mischievous Chú Tễu, dance and play on the water's surface. The dragons writhe and spray water, their movements synchronized with the music, creating a mesmerizing spectacle.

The performance is not just a display of puppetry techniques, but an immersive experience that anchors the audience's souls in the beauty of Vietnam's countryside. As the curtain closes, the audience is left with a sense of nostalgia and affection, carrying with them the memories of the performance, which has transported them back to their sacred roots. The Thang Long Theater, with its unique blend of traditional and modern elements, has become a beloved cultural institution, attracting visitors from all over the world to experience the magic of Vietnamese water puppetry.

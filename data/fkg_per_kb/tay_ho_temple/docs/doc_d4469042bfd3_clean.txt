Tay Ho Temple, also known as Phủ Tây Hồ, is a sacred spiritual site situated on a picturesque peninsula jutting deep into the vast West Lake in Tay Ho District, Hanoi. This magnificent complex of relics has been a revered place of worship for centuries, dating back to the 17th century when it was established to honor Chúa Liễu Hạnh, one of the Tứ bất tử of Vietnamese folk religion. According to legend, the Holy Mother herself descended to earth, opened a tea stall, and engaged in call-and-response poetry with Trạng Bùng Phùng Khắc Khoan, captivated by the breathtaking scenery.

As one approaches the temple, the grand, majestically soaring cổng tam quan comes into view, its deep red hue blending seamlessly with the moss-covered ngói âm dương roof. The unique identifying feature of the temple is the exquisitely carved bức đại tự, positioned atop the main gate's roof, a testament to the skillful craftsmanship of the artisans who built this tranquil architectural structure. The temple is nestled amidst a spacious brick courtyard, entirely enveloped by the canopies of ancient si trees and three sides of vast, adjacent waters.

Tay Ho Temple is not only a steadfast pillar of faith but also a cultural symbol of Hanoi, attracting pilgrims from all directions who come to offer incense and pray for peace, especially during the Lunar New Year festival or on full moon days and the first day of the lunar month. Amidst the ethereal atmosphere of agarwood incense smoke and the resounding chants of chầu văn, visitors can feel a gentle stirring in their minds, cleansing their souls for purity amidst the vast waters. The temple's tranquil surroundings and peaceful ambiance make it an ideal destination for sightseeing, prayer, and leisurely walks, especially during quiet mornings, weekends, and festival days.

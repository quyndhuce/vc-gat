Tay Ho Temple is a shrine dedicated to Princess Liễu Hạnh, located in the capital city of Hanoi, Vietnam. According to legend, the temple was built around the 17th century. It sits on a large peninsula of Nghi Tàm village, jutting out into West Lake, at number 52 Đặng Thai Mai street, Tây Hồ ward, Hanoi. Although the legend suggests it was constructed during the 17th century, historical records from the early 20th century, such as Thăng Long cổ tích khảo, Long Biên bách nhị vịnh, Tây Hồ chí, and Hà Thành linh tích cổ lục, do not mention this site.

On February 13, 1996, the Ministry of Culture (now the Ministry of Culture, Sports, and Tourism) recognized Tay Ho Temple as a Historical and Cultural Relic. Additionally, there is an ancient banyan tree in the temple's courtyard that has been recognized as a "Vietnamese Heritage Tree." Adjacent to the temple is a Kim Ngưu Shrine, which, according to legend, worships the Golden Ox.

Today, Tay Ho Temple holds two main festivals annually, on the third day of the third lunar month and the thirteenth day of the eighth lunar month.

According to tradition, Princess Liễu Hạnh, also known as Quỳnh Hoa, is said to be the second daughter of Jade Emperor who was exiled to earth for breaking a precious jade cup. Roaming the land, she discovered West Lake, finding it a place of scenic beauty, and decided to stay there, opening a tea house as a pretext to enjoy literary pursuits amidst the mystical natural surroundings. This immortal maiden helped people settle down, eliminate evil spirits, and punish corrupt officials. During the Nguyen dynasty, she was granted the title "Mother of the World," becoming one of the four immortals of Vietnam.

When Trạng Nguyên Phùng Khắc Khoan visited the lake by boat and saw its beauty, he stopped at the Immortal’s tea house. They shared a poetic exchange, which resulted in the poem "Tây Hồ ngự quán" still recited today. No one knows how long the Immortal stayed, but when Phùng Khắc Khoan returned to find her, she was gone. To ease his sorrow, he established a shrine to honor his friend.

The architectural structure of Tay Ho Temple includes a three-arched gate adorned with dragon motifs, with a roof resembling tube tiles, and inscriptions under the eaves reading "Phong đài nguyệt các" (Wind pavilions moon towers), along with couplets recounting the story of Phùng Khắc Khoan meeting Liễu Hạnh. Beyond the gate lies a spacious courtyard adjacent to the lake. On the left side of the courtyard stands the Boys Tower, while on the right is the Girls Tower, symbolizing yin and yang. The main temple has a grand architectural design, with a two-tiered three-arched gate at the front, where the central roof bears the inscription "Tây Hồ hiển tích" (West Lake Manifestation). The four middle doors have carvings of the four seasons on top and the four divine creatures below, with peach blossoms and longevity symbols in between. Passing through the gate leads to a two-tiered octagonal pavilion. Behind it are the front hall and rear hall, followed by the Three-Temple Mountain Hall with three tiers and eight curved roofs, housing two levels: the upper level is dedicated to Guanyin, while the lower level contains three grottoes.

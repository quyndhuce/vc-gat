Nestled at the heart of Hanoi, the Hanoi Old Quarter, often referred to simply as phố cổ, is a historic district renowned for its intricate network of 36 narrow streets, each named after a traditional craft or commodity. This ancient area began to flourish during the Lý and Trần dynasties, transforming into a bustling commercial hub of the former imperial city of Thăng Long. Each street in the Old Quarter carries the legacy of a specific trade, evident from names like Hàng Bạc (Silver Street), Hàng Mã (Paper Goods Street), and Hàng Đào (Sandalwood Street), which reflect the economic and cultural life of Hanoians across various epochs.

The Old Quarter's architectural landscape is characterized by closely packed tube houses with their distinctive tiled roofs and French colonial-era iron balconies, creating a dense yet vibrant urban environment. These structures, along with the myriad of signboards and storefronts, form a living museum that encapsulates the rich history and traditional crafts of Vietnam. Despite enduring numerous historical upheavals, the Old Quarter has managed to preserve much of its original layout and lifestyle, making it a unique cultural emblem of the city.

This historic district is not only a residential area but also a dynamic center for cultural activities and festivals. It comes alive particularly during Tết Nguyên Đán, when the streets are festively adorned with decorations and filled with the traditional atmosphere of the Vietnamese New Year. The Old Quarter's small area contrasts sharply with its extremely high population density and intense business activity, contributing to a lively and colorful urban scene. Visitors can experience the interplay between past and present as they wander through the narrow lanes, where the echoes of centuries-old trades blend seamlessly with modern-day commerce.

In addition to its historical and cultural significance, the Old Quarter is a popular tourist destination, offering a glimpse into the daily life of Hanoi. The area around the Hanoi Old Quarter Night Market buzzes with energy as evening falls, illuminated by the glow of streetlights and the sounds of music and chatter. Here, visitors can sample local cuisine from street vendors who set up tables and chairs on the sidewalks, serving dishes that waft with the enticing aromas of Vietnamese street food. The Old Quarter thus stands as a testament to the enduring spirit of Hanoi, where tradition and modernity coexist in a harmonious and dynamic balance.

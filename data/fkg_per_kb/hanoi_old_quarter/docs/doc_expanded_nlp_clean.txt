Hanoi Old Quarter (Phố cổ Hà Nội) is located in Hoan Kiem district, Hanoi, north of Hoan Kiem Lake. It is a historic urban quarter.
The Old Quarter evolved through craft and merchant streets, with guild-related place names and dense urban trade.
The quarter is a connected street network rather than a single monument.
Hang Dao, Hang Bac and Hang Gai are distinct streets.
Ta Hien Street and Hang Dau Water Tower are identifiable sub-sites.
Characteristic physical features include narrow tube houses, shopfronts, street signs, markets, scooters, pedestrian lanes and old facades.
Hanoi Old Quarter is associated with the following named places and physical sub-entities: Craft streets, tube houses, Ta Hien Street, Hang Dau Water Tower, shop houses.
The site is associated with street commerce, craft memory, markets, food and living urban heritage.
Hanoi Old Quarter lies north of Hoan Kiem Lake.
The Old Quarter contains narrow streets and densely arranged shopfronts.
Many street names begin with the Vietnamese word Hang.
Hang Bac Street is associated historically with silverworking and jewelry trade.
Hang Dao Street is associated historically with textiles and dyeing.
Hang Gai Street is associated with silk and textile commerce.
Hang Chieu Street is associated with traditional mat-related trade.
Hang Ma Street is associated with paper votive goods and festive decorations.
Ta Hien Street contains restaurants, cafes, and nightlife venues.
The Hang Dau Water Tower stands near the northern edge of the Old Quarter.
The water tower has a cylindrical masonry form.
Traditional tube houses have narrow street frontages and elongated internal plans.
Some tube houses contain internal courtyards.
Shopfronts open directly onto narrow streets.
Temples and communal houses are interspersed among commercial buildings.
The Old Quarter preserves traces of historic craft guild organization.
Contemporary commerce has changed the functions of many historic streets.

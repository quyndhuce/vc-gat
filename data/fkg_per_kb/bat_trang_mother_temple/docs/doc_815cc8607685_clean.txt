In the heart of Hanoi's outskirts, nestled beside the majestic Red River, lies the revered Bat Trang Mother Temple, also known as Đền Mẫu Bát Tràng. This ancient architectural gem, with its elegant soaring đao roof system, rises majestically from the lush canopy of ancient trees, its deep brown color blending seamlessly with the moss-covered roof tiles. The temple's sole distinguishing feature is an exquisite gốm men lam inlaid bình phong, standing majestically before the porch, a testament to the skillful craftsmanship of the artisans who built this sacred site.

According to legend, the temple was founded by the first potters from Bồ Bát, who brought their expertise in ceramics to Thăng Long and established a thriving community. The temple is dedicated to the worship of Thánh Mẫu, the female deity who protects the villagers from the fury of the storms and waves, allowing them to trade and thrive. The temple's significance is likened to the embracing arms of a mother, cradling her children like the earth and fire that nourish them. In stark contrast to the fiery heat of the kilns, life around the temple flows smoothly, as villagers often visit to offer incense and pray for favorable weather, their ceramics emerging from the kilns with a perfect glaze.

During the village festivals, the temple's tranquil atmosphere is transformed into a vibrant celebration, as the villagers gather to perform rituals and ceremonies, accompanied by the rustling of flags and the scent of incense. Standing before the altar, surrounded by the wispy smoke of incense, visitors are enveloped in a sense of serenity, shedding their worldly worries and concerns. The temple offers a profound experience, allowing each visitor to grasp the essence of the artisans' skill and the cultural heritage that has been preserved for generations.

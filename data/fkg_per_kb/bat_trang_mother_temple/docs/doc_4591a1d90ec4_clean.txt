The Bat Trang Mother Temple is a shrine dedicated to the Vietnamese Mother Goddess cult, specifically honoring Mẫu Bản Hương, believed by devotees to be the reincarnation of the Jade Emperor's daughter. The temple was constructed at the end of the 16th century and the beginning of the 17th century, underwent major renovations during the reign of Emperor Tu Duc, and received further restoration during the time of Emperor Thanh Thai.

The temple's architecture reflects the style of the Nguyen dynasty, featuring a traditional wooden structure built with Bat Trang bricks and roofed with traditional tiles. Its layout follows the "Nhị" character design, with a main hall and a rear chamber each consisting of three parallel rooms facing the Red River. In front of these chambers lies a grand entrance gate and a spacious courtyard paved with Bat Trang bricks, surrounded by ancient trees.

The temple venerates a local woman named Tran Dong Tam, who is revered as Mẫu Bản Hương, also known as Chầu Bà Đệ Tứ Khâm Sai. According to legend, she was born in September 1568 after a nine-month pregnancy, and was said to have been a beautiful and gentle child, eating only fruits and displaying a serene demeanor. At twelve years old, when someone proposed marriage, she fell ill. On the 24th of September 1585, at noon, she bathed, bowed to her parents, and declared that she was the daughter of the Jade Emperor sent to earth for eighteen years and now had to return to heaven. She then passed away, and her body was placed in a coffin where people paid their respects. After a hundred days, her spirit appeared to villagers, prompting them to build a shrine in her honor. Her worship spread widely, leading to the establishment of seventy-two temples across Gia Bình and Siêu Loại.

The temple houses numerous thrones, dragon pavilions, canopies, gates, couplets, and large characters praising her virtues. One such inscription reads, "Worship through generations," indicating the enduring reverence for her. Another reads, "Divine grace extends far and wide," highlighting the widespread influence of her benevolence. Additionally, there are couplets like, "The brilliance of the clan's descendants shines through the incense smoke," and "The sun rises from the East, spreading its light; the moon illuminates the clear temple, filling it with fragrant incense."

Every year, on the 24th day of the ninth month according to the lunar calendar, the village holds a two-day festival to commemorate the deity's passing. This event includes ancestral rites performed by the Tran family, communal ceremonies conducted by the village, and offerings made by villagers and visitors alike. On the evening of the 24th, a lantern release ceremony takes place on the Red River in front of the temple, creating a mesmerizing spectacle of floating lights resembling stars twinkling in the night sky.

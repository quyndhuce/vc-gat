Hang Be Street is one of the ancient streets in Hanoi, stretching from the intersection of Hang Mam and Hang Bac to the junction of Cau Go and Hang Thuong, connecting with Hang Dau Street. It belongs to Hang Bac ward, Hoan Kiem district, Hanoi. In the past, Hang Be was part of an old dike where wooden rafts and construction materials from the northern regions were brought to sell. Hence, this section of the dike was named Hang Be, and the market on the dike was called the Hang Be Market. During the French colonial period, the street was officially named Hang Be Street (Rues des Radeaux).

The area where Hang Be Street now stands was originally part of the Nam Hoa village, which belonged to the Huu Tuc commune, Tho Xuan county. By the mid-19th century, it became known as Nam Pho village, Dong Tho commune. Historically, this location was close to My Loc wharf by the Red River. Rafts made of bamboo and reeds were transported here for sale, forming a raft market within the Hang Be Market. Initially, these rafts were sold at Hang Tre Street, but later moved to Hang Be Street. The beginning of Hang Be Street was once called Hang Dua Street because it was a place to trade dried betel nuts. When rafts could no longer reach the foot of the dike due to sedimentation pushing the river further away, the street became a place to trade betel nuts, hence its alternative name, Hang Dua Street.

In the past, annually, bamboo rafts from the southern regions would bring goods such as sugar from Quang, fish nets, shrimp, dried fish, fish sauce, dried betel nuts from Qui Nhon and Binh Dinh, bowls, paper, silk, copper pots, and tin trays. Large boats carried goods like altar tablets, ancestral shrines, tea cabinets, and even kept chickens and dogs. Chinese merchants also participated in trading, bringing goods in large quantities to storehouses along Hang Be Street until the river shifted, a new dike was built farther away, and the bamboo rafts and wood were relocated elsewhere, while betel nuts continued to be sold here. The new dike had a section named Bè Thượng, which the French called Rue de la Digue (Street on the Dike) when they first arrived in Hanoi, corresponding to Nguyen Huu Huan Street today.

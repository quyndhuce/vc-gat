Hang Be Street, one of the ancient streets of Hanoi, stretches from the intersection of Hang Mam and Hang Bac to the junction of Cau Go and Hang Thuong, connecting with Hang Dau Street. This historic thoroughfare is nestled within the bustling district of Hang Bac, Hoan Kiem, Hanoi.

The street's charm lies not only in its historical significance but also in its vibrant atmosphere, which reflects the rich cultural heritage of Vietnam. Hang Be Street is a testament to the city’s enduring traditions and the resilience of its people. The street’s origins date back centuries, with its name derived from the traditional craft of making bamboo rafts, a practice that once thrived here. Today, while the original craft may no longer dominate the area, the essence of this ancient trade still resonates through the narrow lanes and the stories shared by the locals.

Visitors can explore the intricate architecture of the old houses lining the street, each structure a blend of traditional Vietnamese design and colonial influences. The buildings are characterized by their ornate wooden doors and windows, often adorned with delicate carvings and lattice work, reflecting the skilled craftsmanship of local artisans. These structures stand as silent witnesses to the passage of time, their weathered facades telling tales of generations past.

The street itself is a living museum, where visitors can immerse themselves in the daily life of the community. The narrow pathways are lined with small shops and stalls selling a variety of goods, from traditional handicrafts to fresh produce. The atmosphere is lively and bustling, with the sounds of vendors calling out to customers and the aroma of freshly cooked food wafting through the air. This vibrant scene is a reflection of the street’s role as a hub of commerce and social interaction, where locals and tourists alike come together to experience the unique flavor of Hanoi.

One notable landmark along Hang Be Street is theAVA Lounge, a stylish venue that offers a modern twist on the traditional coffee shop. Here, visitors can enjoy a cup of aromatic Vietnamese coffee while taking in the relaxed ambiance and the sounds of live music. The lounge serves as a perfect spot for those seeking a moment of respite amidst the hustle and bustle of the city.

In addition to its commercial activities, Hang Be Street is also home to several interesting sites and events. For instance, there is a charming corner in the alley numbered 44, where one can find a quaint space for sale, offering a glimpse into the lives of those who have lived and worked in the area. Hang Be Street, located in Hoan Kiem District of Hanoi, is a charming and historically rich area that stretches for 172 meters and is 6 meters wide. The street runs from Hang Mam Street to the intersection of Hang Dao and Cau Go streets. Originally part of the Nam Hoa village, which was situated near the My Loc gate, this area was later renamed Nam Pho during the mid-19th century when the village's administrative division was changed to Dong Tho.

During the French colonial period, the street was known as "rue des Radeaux" or "Phố Hàng Bè," meaning "Raft Street." In 1920, it was renamed "rue Hulet" or "Phố Hu Lê," but in 1945, the original name "Hang Be" was restored. Today, Hang Be Street belongs to Hang Bac ward, Hoan Kiem district.

The origin of the street's name is rooted in its history as a marketplace for raft sellers and buyers. Due to its proximity to the Red River, the area became a hub for selling rafts made of bamboo and other materials. Initially, the market was held at the end of Hang Tre Street, where the Royal Hotel now stands, but it gradually moved to Hang Be Street. This transition led to the street's current name, reflecting its historical significance as a trading center for rafts.

Interestingly, the beginning of Hang Be Street was once called Hang Cau Street because it was home to many shops selling dried rattan. At number 29 on Hang Be Street, there is a temple dedicated to Ngũ Hầu, also known as Cao Tu, a legendary hero from the time of King An Duong Vuong. Inside the temple, there is a pair of poetic couplets that beautifully capture the spirit of the place:

"Sông Tô nổi sóng căm thù, một tấm lòng trung truyền sử Thục.

Thành Loa treo vầng nhật nguyện áo hoa phong tặng tự triều Đinh."

Translated, these lines mean: "The waves of the To River surge with anger, a loyal heart passing down the history of Shu. The Loa City hangs a sun and moon, flowers bloom in the wind, a gift from the reign of Emperor Dinh."

According to legend, Emperor Dinh Tien Hoang bestowed the title of king upon Cao Tu. The temple itself is not associated with the Nam Pho village but rather with the Ngũ Hầu village, which was originally located outside the ancient Thang Long Citadel, close to the Red River. To avoid flooding, the villagers eventually relocated to the land of Nam Pho.

Hang Be Street played a significant role during the early stages of the national resistance war, serving as a command post for the leadership of the First Regional Command and the 101st Regiment, a main force regiment of the region. This historical context adds a layer of importance to the street beyond its commercial origins.

Visitors to Hang Be Street can immerse themselves in the rich tapestry of Hanoi’s past, exploring the narrow lanes lined with traditional houses and small shops. The atmosphere is one of tranquility and nostalgia, with the sounds of daily life blending seamlessly into the urban landscape. The street offers a glimpse into the city’s heritage, where old traditions coexist with modernity, creating a unique blend of history and contemporary life.

The cultural significance of Hang Be Street extends beyond its physical boundaries, embodying the resilience and adaptability of the local community. It serves as a testament to the enduring spirit of Hanoi, where each corner tells a story of the past while embracing the future.

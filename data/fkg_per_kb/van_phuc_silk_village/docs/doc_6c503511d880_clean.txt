Van Phuc Silk Village, located in Ha Dong district of Hanoi, Vietnam, is celebrated as one of the oldest and most renowned silk weaving villages in the country, boasting a rich history that spans over a thousand years. By 2025, due to administrative changes, Van Phuc had been integrated into a newly formed ward within Ha Dong district.

Geographically, Van Phuc is situated approximately 10 kilometers southwest of central Hanoi, nestled along the Nhu River. It shares borders with several neighboring wards including Dai Mo to the north, Yet Kieu to the south, Mo Lao to the east, and La Khe and Quang Trung to the west.

The village is deeply embedded in Vietnamese culture, hosting various annual events such as the "Van Phuc - Sac Lua Nhin Nam" cultural and tourism week. This event features elaborately decorated streets with umbrellas, showcasing traditional handicrafts and silk-making processes. Visitors can explore the village temple, constructed in the 17th century, and admire other historical landmarks like the ancient banyan tree and well. These sites not only preserve the cultural heritage of the village but also attract a steady stream of tourists from both within and outside Vietnam. Tourists have the opportunity to witness the intricate silk-making processes, purchase unique silk products, and engage in activities such as silk painting.

Van Phuc Silk Village stands out among the over 90 officially recognized handicraft villages in the vicinity of Hanoi, making it a significant destination for those interested in traditional crafts and cultural experiences.

Stepping out of the bustling Trống Đồng building in the heart of Hanoi, one is instantly transported to a serene miniature Vietnam, where the outdoor folk architecture exhibition area of the Museum of Ethnology stands as a testament to the rich cultural heritage of the country. Nestled within a shaded garden, this tranquil space houses traditional houses that collectively recreate the living environments and honor the identities of the diverse ethnic groups inhabiting Vietnam’s S-shaped territory. Each structure in this unique ensemble was meticulously crafted by skilled artisans from various remote villages, who journeyed to the capital city to construct these authentic replicas using traditional techniques and age-old legends. The rustic brown hues of the wooden structures, combined with the worn thatched roofs, create a harmonious blend that immediately captures the eye, while the distinctive staircase carved in the shape of a nipple adds a unique visual cue to each dwelling.

The architectural complex is characterized by its majestic rise, with the soaring, axe-blade shaped nhà rông roof being the most prominent feature. These traditional houses are scattered along winding paths, providing visitors with a leisurely stroll through a landscape dotted with tranquil dwellings and lush greenery. The garden itself is a living museum, where one can observe artisans weaving fabric under the floors, adding an interactive element to the experience. During Tet holidays, the garden comes alive with vibrant celebrations, marked by the resounding sounds of gongs and the graceful movements of Xoe dancers, creating a festive atmosphere under a canopy of colorful flags.

This outdoor exhibition area not only serves as a cultural landmark but also plays a significant role in preserving and promoting Vietnam’s diverse ethnic traditions. It stands as a symbol of unity among the fifty-four ethnic groups that coexist within the nation, fostering a sense of shared identity and heritage. For tourists and locals alike, it offers a unique opportunity to explore and appreciate the intricate details of traditional Vietnamese architecture, from the intricate carvings on wooden beams to the woven patterns on rattan branches. As visitors touch the cool bamboo walls, they are reminded of the sacred blood-and-flesh connection that binds these communities together, reinforcing the idea of a unified yet culturally rich Vietnam.

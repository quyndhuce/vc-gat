In the heart of Hanoi's historic district, nestled within the serene surroundings of the Temple of Literature, stands the majestic Khuê Văn Các, a testament to the city's rich cultural heritage. This two-story pavilion, built in the early 19th century, is a masterpiece of Vietnamese architecture, its sturdy square brick base supporting a wooden architectural system adorned with a vermilion-red color and a layered tiled roof. The pavilion's unique round windows, carved with rays of light, resemble twinkling stars on the wooden wall, adding to its celestial charm.

As one approaches the pavilion, the moss-covered brick courtyard, surrounded by ancient trees, whispers secrets of the past. The air is thick with the scent of incense and the soft rustling of silk áo dài-clad visitors, who come to pay homage to the revered symbol of Vietnamese culture. The pavilion's name, Khuê Văn Các, borrows the image of the Khuê constellation, the brightest star in ancient astronomy, symbolizing literature and wisdom.

Khuê Văn Các was erected by Tổng trấn Nguyễn Văn Thành under Emperor Gia Long of the Nguyen dynasty, a testament to the emperor's vision for a nation built on the pillars of Confucian learning and reverence for education. Today, the pavilion remains a guiding lighthouse, inspiring generations of scholars and students to follow the path of learning and scholarship. On the night of Vietnam Poetry Day, the pavilion shines brightly, its vermilion-red color glowing like a beacon, as poets and literature enthusiasts gather to celebrate the nation's rich cultural heritage.

As one stands before Khuê Văn Các, the silence is palpable, the only sound being the soft rustling of leaves and the distant chanting of Buddhist monks. The pavilion's majesty is a reminder of the importance of preserving cultural heritage, a symbol of the nation's commitment to the pursuit of knowledge and wisdom.

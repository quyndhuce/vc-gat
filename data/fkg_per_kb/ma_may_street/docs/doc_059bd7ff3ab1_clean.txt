Ma May Street is a historic street located within the Old Quarter of Hanoi, stretching approximately 268 meters in length. The street runs southward and then turns westward. At its southern end, it intersects with Hang Bac Street, while at its western end, it connects to Hang Bo Street.

The name Ma May Street is derived from the combination of two former streets: Hang Ma (the southern segment) and Hang May (the northern segment). Today, the street primarily houses companies and guesthouses catering to tourists. Historically, the southern part of Hang Ma Street was dedicated to the production of paper goods used for funerals and processions, such as paper offerings and decorations. In contrast, the northern part of Hang May Street specialized in products made from rattan, including both finished goods and raw materials.

During the French colonial period, the street was known as Rue des Pavillons Noirs, meaning "Street of Black Flags," due to the presence of a Black Flag Army unit stationed there in 1883. The French also established several administrative offices in this area because of its proximity to the river and the Chinese trading district. Some French merchants concentrated in this neighborhood for their business activities.

Currently, most of Ma May Street is occupied by guesthouses, hotels, travel company offices, and restaurants that cater specifically to foreign tourists. Notable landmarks include the Ma May Temple, located at number 64 Ma May Street, which is dedicated to Nguyen Trung Ngan and dates back to the Tran Dynasty. Another significant site is the restored ancient house at number 87 Ma May Street, which exemplifies traditional architecture found in the Old Quarter.

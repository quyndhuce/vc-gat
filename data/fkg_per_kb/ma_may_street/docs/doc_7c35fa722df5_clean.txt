Situated in the heart of Hanoi's Old Quarter, Ma May Street is a narrow, bustling thoroughfare that holds within it a treasure trove of historical and cultural significance. One of the most notable landmarks on this street is the Heritage House No. 87 Ma May, a unique architectural gem that stands out among the myriad of old houses in the area. Recognized as a national relic since 2004, this house exemplifies the traditional architecture of Hanoi from the early 20th century. With its wide facade of just five meters but stretching over twenty-eight meters in length, the house boasts a total area of more than 157 square meters, making it a living museum of Hanoi’s past. The house’s layout includes a series of rooms separated by natural light wells and courtyards, which not only provide ventilation and lighting but also create a serene atmosphere that transports visitors back in time. The interior is adorned with intricate wooden furniture and ornate carvings, preserved almost entirely in their original state, offering a glimpse into the daily life of Hanoians from a bygone era.

Adjacent to this heritage site is the Cultural and Art Center at No. 22 Hang Buom, formerly known as the Guangdong Guild Hall. This expansive complex covers an area of 1,800 square meters and showcases a blend of Vietnamese, Chinese, and French architectural styles. The center frequently hosts traditional folk art performances such as ca trù and xẩm singing, as well as contemporary art exhibitions. Its spacious and distinctive design, combined with regular thematic displays, makes it a popular destination for tourists seeking to explore Vietnam’s rich cultural heritage and take memorable photographs.

Despite their unique charm and historical value, both the Heritage House and the Cultural and Art Center face challenges in fully engaging visitors. The Heritage House, while maintaining much of its original character, lacks interactive activities that could enhance the visitor experience. Currently, visitors can admire the architecture, take photos, and purchase souvenirs, but there are few opportunities for hands-on engagement. This limitation is evident when considering the potential for immersive activities such as reenactments of traditional Hanoi lifestyles, including demonstrations of daily routines, cooking, and crafting. Such activities could include recreating a typical Hanoi family dinner, showcasing traditional crafts like paper-cutting, calligraphy, and lantern-making, or even hosting workshops where visitors can learn to make traditional toys and decorations. These interactive sessions would not only enrich the visitor experience but also serve as a platform for promoting local culture and history.

Similarly, the Cultural and Art Center could benefit from additional programming that leverages its large space and diverse cultural offerings.

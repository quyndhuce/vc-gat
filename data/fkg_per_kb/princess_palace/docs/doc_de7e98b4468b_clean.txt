In the heart of Hanoi's Thăng Long Imperial Citadel, a majestic structure stands as a testament to the city's rich cultural heritage: the Princess Palace, also known as Hậu Lâu. This ancient edifice, built during the Nguyễn dynasty, is a masterclass in subtle elegance, its pale yellow weathered surfaces and thick brick walls exuding a sense of quiet solemnity.

As one approaches the palace, the irregular arrangement of small windows, some arched and others rectangular, creates a distinctive visual rhythm, punctuating the otherwise solid, rectangular form. The restrained roofline, with its less pronounced curves, adds to the overall sense of understated grandeur. The surrounding green spaces and open courtyard soften the heaviness of the building's mass, imbuing the entire structure with a sense of serenity.

As a royal residence, the Princess Palace was once a resting place for the cung nữ, the ladies-in-waiting who accompanied the Nguyễn kings on their northern expeditions. The tower's name, Tĩnh Bắc lâu, or Quiet North Tower, reflects its strategic location behind the Kính Thiên Điện complex, built to maintain peace in the northern part of the imperial citadel.

Today, the Princess Palace is a cherished part of the Thăng Long heritage site and a UNESCO World Heritage landmark, attracting visitors from around the world. As they explore the palace's ancient beauty, they are transported to a bygone era, one of imperial grandeur and cultural significance. The Princess Palace stands as a testament to the enduring legacy of Vietnam's royal era, its quiet, reserved atmosphere a poignant reminder of the country's rich cultural heritage.

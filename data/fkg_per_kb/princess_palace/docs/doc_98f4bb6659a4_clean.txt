Nestled within the expansive grounds of the Thăng Long Imperial Citadel in Hanoi, the Princess Palace, also known as Hậu Lâu or Tĩnh Bắc Tower, stands as a testament to the rich architectural and cultural heritage of Vietnam. Built during the Nguyễn dynasty, this ancient structure served as a resting place for the cung nữ, or palace ladies, who accompanied the Nguyễn kings on their visits to the Bắc thành, or Northern Citadel. The tower's strategic placement to the north of the Kính Thiên Điện architectural complex, which functioned as the hành cung, or temporary palace, underscores its importance in maintaining the tranquility and harmony of the northern sector of the citadel according to traditional phong thủy principles.

Constructed with thick, pale yellow brick walls that have weathered over centuries, the Princess Palace exudes a quiet grandeur. Its solid, multi-story rectangular form is characterized by a restrained roofline, simpler in design compared to the ornate roofs of other imperial structures. The exterior of the tower features a unique array of small windows, both arched and rectangular, arranged in an irregular pattern that contrasts with the strict symmetry typical of traditional Vietnamese architecture. These windows, when illuminated by natural light, cast intricate patterns of light and shadow across the weathered surfaces, adding depth and texture to the building's facade.

Surrounding the Princess Palace are serene green spaces and an open courtyard, which help to soften the imposing presence of the structure and enhance its tranquil atmosphere. The combination of its architectural simplicity and the natural beauty of its surroundings creates a sense of quiet solemnity, making it a significant cultural landmark within the larger Thăng Long Imperial Citadel. Recognized as part of the UNESCO World Heritage site, the Princess Palace not only serves as a silent witness to the historical transformations of the region but also as a vital component of Vietnam's royal legacy, attracting visitors interested in exploring the country's rich cultural and architectural heritage.

Nestled within the bustling heart of Hanoi’s Đống Đa District, at 58 Văn Miếu Street, lies the Temple of Literature - Quốc Tử Giám complex, a revered site that has stood as a testament to Vietnam’s rich educational heritage since its founding in 1070 under Emperor Lý Thánh Tông of the Lý dynasty. The complex, a UNESCO World Heritage site and a national special relic, encompasses a myriad of historically significant areas, among which the Nhap Dao section stands out as the introductory courtyard, marking the threshold between the secular world and the sacred realm of learning and spiritual growth.

The Nhap Dao section, or Khu Nhập Đạo, is a vast rectangular courtyard that serves as the initial approach to the inner sanctum of the Temple of Literature. Its architectural design, steeped in traditional Vietnamese court aesthetics, features a grand main path flanked by two rows of ancient trees, their branches casting a soothing shade over the moss-covered red brick pathways. These pathways, along with the lush green lawns, create a serene environment that contrasts sharply with the urban chaos just beyond its walls. The courtyard is enclosed by a tranquil gạch vồ wall, which not only provides a physical boundary but also symbolizes a mental transition for visitors entering the complex.

Historically, the Nhap Dao section was established during the early days of Vietnam’s higher education system, reflecting the profound philosophical principles that guided the country’s intellectual pursuits. As the first step towards the inner sanctum, it embodies the concept of shedding worldly distractions and embracing a path of moral cultivation, self-improvement, and character development. For centuries,学子们（nho sinh）在这里放下尘世的烦恼，踏上修身养性的道路。每日，人们在树荫下漫步，偶尔停下脚步，身着飘逸的越南长袍拍照留念。开学季或重要考试前，学生们怀着敬畏之心回到这里，双手合十祈祷，寻求内心的平静和智慧的启迪。漫步在覆盖青苔的红砖小径上，人们的心灵会突然感到一种难以言喻的宁静和平和。这种沉浸于静谧空间中的体验仿佛是一种净化仪式，为深入探索千年的知识世界做好了最庄重而宁静的心理准备。

In modern times, the Nhap Dao section continues to attract a steady stream of visitors, including students, tourists, and locals, who come to appreciate its cultural significance and serene atmosphere. The courtyard is open daily from 8:00 to 17:00, with an admission fee of 30,000 VND, making it accessible to a wide range of visitors. The site remains a vital part of Vietnam’s cultural heritage, embodying the nation’s enduring respect for education and Confucian values.

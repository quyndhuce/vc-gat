As we delve into the captivating world of "Nông nghiệp, cấy cày" (Agriculture and Farming) at the Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre, we find ourselves transported to a bygone era, where the rustic charm of rural Vietnam comes alive. Nestled on Đinh Tiên Hoàng Street, adjacent to the picturesque Hoàn Kiếm Lake, this enchanting artistic sanctuary has been a beloved cultural hub since its inception in 1969. The theatre's name, Thăng Long, pays homage to the era when King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital, honoring the thousand-year-old wet rice civilization that has shaped the country's identity.

As the performance begins, the wooden puppets, adorned with glossy sơn mài layers, spring to life on the rippling surface of the lake. The rustic bamboo screen, a testament to the region's traditional craftsmanship, provides a picturesque backdrop for the laborious scenes of farming life. The farmer, bent over beside the majestic buffalo, is a poignant reminder of the sweat and toil that has gone into cultivating the land.

The clacking rhythm and the sound of the đàn nhị fill the air, transporting the audience to a bustling harvest festival in the countryside. The puppets' movements, a masterclass in storytelling, bring to life the daily struggles and triumphs of the farmers, their faces etched with determination and joy. The performance is not merely a display of skillful puppetry technique, but a love song praising the farmers' hard work, offering an experience that touches deep into one's roots amidst the hustle and bustle of urban life.

As the performance comes to a close, the audience is left with a poignant wave of emotion, mixed with pride, as they reflect on the cultural significance of this ancient tradition. The Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre's "Nông nghiệp, cấy cày" performance is a testament to the enduring power of cultural heritage, a celebration of the land, the people, and the traditions that have shaped Vietnam's rich cultural tapestry.

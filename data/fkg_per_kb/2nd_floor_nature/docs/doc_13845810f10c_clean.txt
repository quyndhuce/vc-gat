As visitors ascend to the second floor of the Hanoi Museum, they are transported back in time to an era when the land of Hanoi had not yet taken shape as a city. The exhibition space, aptly titled "Nature," unfolds like a journey through the geological, climatic, and ecological history of the region. The story begins with the layers of alluvium from the Red River, which once flowed through the heart of the city, shaping the land and creating the fertile soil that would support the growth of a thriving metropolis.

The exhibit showcases the ancient network of rivers that once crisscrossed the area, giving rise to the mythical "coiling dragon and crouching tiger" landscape. This natural wonder was a key factor in King Ly Thai To's decision to establish Thang Long as the capital of Vietnam. The exhibit's centerpiece is a series of terrain models and biological specimens, which recreate the natural landscape of ancient Hanoi, accompanied by images of rivers, lakes, and geological maps.

The visual documentary screening area provides a glimpse into the region's rich ecosystem, showcasing the diverse flora and fauna that once thrived in the area. The exhibit's neutral tones and soft lighting highlight the rock, soil, plant, and organism exhibits, creating an open and scientific feel that invites visitors to explore and learn.

As visitors wander through the exhibit, they are struck by the realization that the history of Hanoi is not solely the product of human endeavor, but also the result of the natural environment that nurtured the city's growth. The exhibit's quiet, contemplative atmosphere invites visitors to reflect on the intricate relationship between nature and human civilization, and the ways in which the natural world has shaped the course of Hanoi's history.

In this sense, the 2nd Floor: Nature exhibition space is more than just a display of geological and biological specimens – it is a testament to the enduring power of nature to shape our understanding of the world and our place within it.

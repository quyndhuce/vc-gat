Hoa Phong Tower is a tower located on the eastern shore of Hoan Kiem Lake in Hanoi, Vietnam. It is the sole remaining structure of Bao An Temple, a significant Buddhist temple built in 1842 during the Nguyen Dynasty. This tower should not be confused with another tower of the same name at Thuong Thien Thanh Khương Pagoda in Thuong Thien Thuong Thien town, Bac Ninh province.

Bao An Temple was a large Buddhist temple constructed around 1842 under the Nguyen Dynasty, funded by donations from the public collected by Nguyen Dang Giai, the Governor of Ha Ninh. The temple was situated on the eastern bank of Hoan Kiem Lake, facing the Red River to the east and backed by the lake itself. It consisted of 180 rooms and featured a complex and intricate architectural design.

In 1888, the French colonial authorities demolished the temple to clear the land for the construction of the Hanoi Post Office, leaving only the Hoa Phong Tower behind. The tower stands three stories high and is known as a "four-gate tower" due to its four entrances on the first floor. Each of these entrances has a vaulted arch, and the second floor features a large Sanskrit character. Above each arch are inscriptions naming the respective gates:

- Bao An Gate,

- Bao Nghia Gate,

- Bao Duc Gate, and

- Bao Phuc Gate.

On the third floor, the eastern and western sides bear the inscription "Hoa Phong," while the northern and southern sides read "Bao Thien Tower."

This tower represents an important relic of Vietnam's cultural heritage, standing as a testament to the country's rich history and architectural traditions.

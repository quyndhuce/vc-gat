The Imperial Citadel of Thang Long, located in the heart of Hanoi, Vietnam, is a complex of historic buildings that reflect the rich history of Vietnam. Construction of this citadel began in 1010 under Emperor Lý Thái Tổ of the Lý dynasty and was completed in early 1011. The site's origins trace back even further, to the early Tang dynasty when modern Vietnam was part of the Annan protectorate. In 866, General Gao Pian of the Tang dynasty ordered the construction of the Đại La Citadel, which would later evolve into the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long.

The fall of the Tang dynasty led to a period of independence known as the Anarchy of the 12 Warlords, ending with the establishment of the Đại Việt monarchy. During the Song dynasty, the Đại La Citadel served as a crucial military site for defense against northern invasions and internal conflicts. It wasn't until the rise of the Lý dynasty in the early 11th century that the citadel became the center of power in An Nam.

Under the Lý and Trần dynasties (11th-14th centuries), the citadel underwent significant expansion and served as the seat of the Vietnamese court until 1789. The original structure consisted of three sectors: the outer defensive fortification (La thành or Kinh thành), the Imperial City (Hoàng thành), and the innermost Forbidden City (Tử cấm thành). In 1029, Emperor Lý Thái Tông rebuilt the entire Forbidden City after it was destroyed by the Three Kings Rebellion. Subsequent rulers continued to make additions and renovations to the complex.

The material remains of the original structure include terracotta foundations, a sophisticated drainage system, and various royal architectural decorations. These elements highlight the site's urban planning and design, reflecting the significance of the Lý dynasty as a golden age in Vietnamese history.

After the successful revolution against the Ming dynasty in 1428, Lê Lợi established the Lê dynasty, renaming the capital to Đông Kinh. Under Lê Thái Tổ, repairs were made to the citadel following damage from the war with the Ming army. The Mạc dynasty ruled Đông Kinh from 1527 to 1592, with construction efforts initiated by Mạc Mậu Hợp in 1585. After the Mạc dynasty was overthrown, the citadel was restored and served as a symbol of power and administrative center for the Trịnh lords.

When the Nguyễn dynasty took control in 1802, they moved the capital to Huế, constructing a new Imperial Citadel there. Much of the remaining Imperial Citadel of Thang Long was dismantled to aid in the construction of the new citadel. However, in 1805, Gia Long ordered an extensive reconstruction of the old Imperial Citadel, adopting a French Vauban-style outer wall. In 1831, Minh Mang renamed Thăng Long to Hanoi province, a name that lasted until 1888 when the Nguyễn dynasty ceded Hanoi to French colonial rule.

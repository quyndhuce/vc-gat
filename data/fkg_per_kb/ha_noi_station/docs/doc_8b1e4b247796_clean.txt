Ha Noi Station, also known as Hanoi Central Station or Ga Hà Nội in Vietnamese, stands as one of the principal railway stations in Vietnam, serving as the terminus for five out of the seven active routes in the national network. These routes include the iconic North-South railway, also referred to as the Reunification Express, the Hanoi-Lào Cai railway, the Hanoi-Haiphong railway, the Hanoi-Đồng Đăng railway, and the Hanoi-Quán Triều railway. Situated at 120 Lê Duẩn Street in Cua Nam Ward, Hoan Kiem District, the station is a vital transportation hub for the bustling capital city of Hanoi.

The history of Ha Noi Station dates back to 1895 when the Government of French Indochina selected the Hang Co Market area for its construction. Initially named Hang Co railway station, it was designed by Henri Vildieu and completed in 1902. At the time of its opening, the station featured a Western architectural style and was one of the largest in Southeast Asia. However, during the Vietnam War, the station suffered significant damage, particularly to its central hall which was bombed. In 1976, the central hall was reconstructed in a modern style while preserving the historic side wings.

Today, Ha Noi Station offers various passenger services. Among these, the train to Đồng Đăng operates on a metre gauge track and does not connect directly to the Chinese railway network. Therefore, passengers bound for China must transfer to a Chinese train at Pingxiang. For standard gauge tracks, they extend to Gia Lâm Railway Station, approximately 6 kilometers away from the main station across the river. Consequently, through trains to China depart from Gia Lâm rather than Ha Noi Station itself.

Additionally, trains to Hải Phòng arrive at Ha Noi Station only on weekends and holidays. Meanwhile, the train to Nanning and two other trains to Hải Phòng depart from Hanoi's Gia Lâm station. This arrangement underscores the strategic importance of Ha Noi Station in facilitating both domestic and international travel within the region.

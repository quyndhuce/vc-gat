The Red River Delta, also known as the Hong River Delta, is a vast, flat, and low-lying plain in northern Vietnam, formed by the confluence of the Red River and its distributaries with the Thái Bình River. The term "Hong" translates to "red" or "crimson" in Sino-Vietnamese, reflecting the reddish color of the river's waters. This delta, despite having the smallest area among all regions in Vietnam, boasts the highest population density, with nearly 23 million inhabitants as of 2019. After the 2025 merger of Vietnamese provinces and cities, the Red River Delta encompasses four provinces along with the capital city, Hanoi, and the port city, Haiphong.

The delta spans approximately 15,000 square kilometers and is well-protected by a network of dikes, which safeguard the region from frequent flooding. It is renowned for its fertile soil, ideal for rice cultivation, with most of the land dedicated to this purpose. In 2003, nearly a third of Vietnam’s population, around 24 million people, resided within the Red River basin, with over 17 million in the delta itself. The region is not only agriculturally rich but also economically diverse, hosting numerous industrial zones in cities like Viet Tri, Hanoi, Haiphong, and Nam Dinh. Despite the majority of the population being engaged in agriculture, the delta supports various economic activities such as fisheries, aquaculture, land reclamation, harbor construction, mangrove forestry, and more.

The Red River, the second largest river in Vietnam, significantly influences the hydrology of the Gulf of Tonkin through its extensive water and sediment discharges. Spanning roughly 150 kilometers in width, the delta is situated in the western coastal zone of the Gulf of Tonkin. Although the Red River Delta constitutes only 5% of Vietnam's total land area, it houses 30% of the country's population, making it the most densely populated region. About 80% of the population is involved in agriculture, yet each household typically owns less than half a hectare of farmland, constraining improvements in living standards.

Agriculturally, the Red River Delta is the second most crucial rice-producing area in Vietnam, contributing about 20% of the national crop. The region employs double cropping techniques to maximize rice production, achieving near-optimal yields. However, the rich soil offers potential for crop diversification and further development of aquaculture. These developmental pressures pose challenges to the estuarine environment and ecosystem, leading to issues such as pollution, over-fishing, and habitat destruction due to aquaculture practices.

The Red River Delta is also historically significant, being the birthplace of the Vietnamese nation and the origin of traditional water puppetry. In recent history, the region faced severe bombing during the Vietnam War and was later recognized as part of UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere Programme in 2004, designated as the Red River Delta Biosphere Reserve. Linguists have suggested that the Proto-Austroasiatic languages may have originated in this area around 4,000 to 4,500 years ago, highlighting the region's cultural and linguistic importance.
